## Recognise

A wildfire is a fire in woodland, heath, dunes, grassland or fields. Heat, drought and wind can turn a small spark into a large fire. The greatest dangers are the fire taking you by surprise and cutting you off, and the smoke.

**Fire danger in Flanders.** The Agency for Nature and Forests (Agentschap Natuur en Bos, ANB) gives a colour code per province for the fire danger in nature areas: green, yellow, orange or red. Green means little danger: there is no particular risk, and in case of fire the fire service turns out at normal strength. The higher the code, the greater the danger. When there is a danger of fire or storms, nature areas may be closed to the public. Check the code on natuurenbos.be before you set off. Owners of other public and private woodland face the same risk.

**Fire danger in Wallonia.** During drought the mayor or the governor can impose a local ban. Information signs are placed at sensitive locations. The ban is also published on the municipality's website.

**Information.** If there is a fire nearby, follow the official channels of the municipality and the authorities: radio, TV and police loudspeaker vans, and if the network works also BE-Alert, websites and social media. Do not rely on rumours or messages from unknown accounts on social media.

## Prepare

### Preventing a fire

- Do not smoke in woodland or nature. Do not light a fire there, not even a barbecue or campfire. In Wallonia lighting a fire in the woods is expressly prohibited.
- Never throw away cigarette ends in nature, not even from the car window.
- Do not park in long or dry grass. The exhaust or catalytic converter gets hot enough to set the grass alight.
- Only park in official car parks. Do not block a barrier, path or forest track: the fire service and ambulance need to get through.
- Be careful with tools that produce sparks or heat, such as an angle grinder or a heat gun.
- In Wallonia, do not burn green waste within 25 metres of a wood. Keep water at hand and follow the municipal rules.
- Place a barbecue on a stable, non-combustible surface such as stones, gravel or tiles, far from flammable plants. Keep a bucket of water or sand at hand.
- Charcoal and briquettes can keep smouldering after use. Dispose of them carefully.
- Take glass and rubbish home with you.
- Always take a mobile phone with you when you go out into nature. Install the 112 app in advance.

### Your home on the edge of woodland or heath

If you live close to nature:

- Clean the roof and gutters at least twice a year, in spring and autumn. Do it more often in dry periods.
- Have the flue swept and fit a spark arrester.
- Do not stack firewood against the house.
- Keep 3 to 5 metres between planting and buildings. Keep flammable planting at least 5 metres from a propane tank.
- Pines and conifers burn quickly. Plant them at least 10 metres from buildings. Birch, beech, oak, maple, chestnut, poplar and walnut burn less quickly.
- Clear away dead leaves, branches and pruning waste. Mow the grass short in dry periods.
- Lay gravel or stones as a firebreak. Keep paths and driveways free of plants.
- Make sure your house number is clearly visible and that the fire service can reach your home. A fire engine is up to 3.5 metres wide and 4.2 metres high.
- Keep a garden hose ready that reaches every part of your property. A pond with a pump can also help to keep a small fire wet.

### With your family

- Keep the car's tank at least half full, or the battery charged, so that you can leave quickly.
- Agree where you will meet, how you will reach each other and what you will take with you.
- Keep identity cards, medication, water, things for pets and phones together in a fixed place.
- Know your escape route, also when the roads are busy. On foot or by bicycle can be quicker and safer than by car.
- Agree in advance with family or friends where you can go.
- Have an emergency kit ready, including an N95 face mask against smoke.

## During

### At home

- Call 112 if you see a fire or if someone is in danger. If that does not work, have someone else call or ask for help nearby (see Emergency numbers, If you cannot call).
- Close windows and doors.
- Do not evacuate on your own initiative. Wait for the recommendations of the authorities via the radio and loudspeaker vans, and if the network works via BE-Alert.
- Check whether neighbours, older people and people with a disability need help.
- If you have to leave: shut off the electricity and gas, take your emergency kit and leave immediately.
- If you cannot get away and there is no order to leave: stay inside with windows and doors closed. In case of smoke: stay in a room that you can seal off from the outside air, and set the ventilation to recirculation or close the air intake.
- If you are really trapped: turn on the lights so that rescuers can find you. Call 112 and give your location. Expect delays.

### Outdoors or on the move

**Best option: away from the fire, at right angles to the wind.**

1. Follow the existing paths away from the fire and the column of smoke.
2. Walk at right angles to the direction in which the fire is moving. Do not run ahead of the fire and do not walk towards the fire. If the fire is coming from the south, go east or west, not north.
3. Go to a public road, a large paved area or the built-up area.
4. Help other people to a safe place.
5. Call 112 as soon as possible or use the 112 app. Give your location using landmarks: paths, ponds, lookout towers, car parks.

**No signal, or cannot get through?** Leave as quickly as possible and warn the people you meet on the way (see Emergency numbers, If you cannot call).

Do not take risks and follow the instructions of the fire service and forest rangers.

### In the car

- Do not block paths, forest tracks or entrances. Keep the road clear for the fire service and ambulance.
- Follow the instructions of the police and fire service. If you have to leave, take the route the authorities give.
- If the road is busy or closed, consider leaving on foot or by bicycle.

### At work or at school

- Follow the instructions of the authorities. If people are asked to stay indoors, children also stay indoors at school.
- Do not go to collect your children if the authorities ask people to shelter. The school will let you know when and how you can collect them.
- Be careful with tools that produce sparks or heat near nature.

### Children

- Agree in advance with your children what they will do and where you will find each other again.
- Put the child's health record book, nappies, spare clothing and some toys in the children's emergency bag.

### Vulnerable people

- People with asthma, COPD or another lung disease are particularly sensitive to smoke. Limit their exposure with an N95 mask or a sealed room with an air purifier.
- If you are ill, contact your doctor.
- In your emergency plan, arrange helpers near people with reduced mobility. Offer them a lift in an evacuation.

### Pets and animals

- Agree in advance how you will bring your pets to safety.
- Take a pet carrier or cage, food and medication with you.
- Small pets are sometimes allowed in a reception centre. Follow the official instructions.
- If an animal has to stay behind: tell the emergency services how many animals there are and where they hide. Leave water and food for at least 24 hours.

## Afterwards

- Do not enter the area without permission from the emergency services.
- Only return home when the authorities say it is safe.
- Stay away from hot ash, charred trees, smouldering debris and glowing remains. Heat in the ground can cause burns or a new fire.
- When clearing up, wear long sleeves, long trousers, work gloves and sturdy shoes with thick soles. Wear a face mask and wet the debris to limit dust.
- Take photos of the damage and contact your insurer.
- Only phone in an emergency. If the network works, preferably send messages, so that the network stays free.

## Do not

- Do not smoke in woodland or nature.
- Do not light a fire, campfire or barbecue in woodland or nature.
- Do not throw away cigarette ends, not even from the car window.
- Do not park in long or dry grass and do not block paths or forest tracks.
- Do not run ahead of the fire and do not walk towards the fire.
- Do not go to watch. Stay away from the nature area.
- Do not evacuate on your own initiative, but leave immediately if the authorities ask you to.
