## Recognise

Viruses spread from person to person, via objects, and also via people who are infected but have no symptoms.

**Symptoms of a respiratory infection** include: fever, chills, tiredness, cough, runny nose and headache. Also possible: chest discomfort, reduced appetite, diarrhoea, muscle or body aches, sudden loss of taste or smell, sneezing, sore throat, vomiting, weakness and wheezing.

**Warning signs.** Seek urgent medical help immediately if you have breathing problems or chest pain. Call 112. If that does not work, have someone else call or ask for help nearby (see Emergency numbers, If you cannot call).

**How you hear about an outbreak.** Follow the official channels: the authorities, the health services and your GP. The authorities inform you via radio and TV, and if the network works also via BE-Alert, websites and social media. In Flanders the Department of Care (Departement Zorg) publishes on its website the epidemiological situation and the colour code of the Respi-radar.

## Prepare

**Habits that always help.**

- Wash your hands regularly. This way you avoid contact with pathogens.
- Air shared rooms regularly or make sure there is good ventilation.
- Avoid close contact with people who are ill.
- Get vaccinated against the relevant diseases, on the advice of your GP.
- Find out about health risks, especially before you travel. Check the travel advice of FPS Foreign Affairs if there is an outbreak at your destination.

**A stock.**

- Gradually build up a stock of cleaning products, long-life food, water and your prescribed medicines for a few days to weeks. Buy calmly, so that everyone can get what they need.
- The National Crisis Center recommends being self-sufficient for at least 3 days. Think of drinking water (3 to 10 litres per person per day), long-life food, paper tissues, wet wipes, toilet paper, sanitary towels, disinfectant hand gel and a first aid kit.
- Keep a reserve of your essential medication for at least 72 hours in your emergency bag.

**A plan.**

- Prepare for the closure of schools, workplaces and meeting places. Arrange how you can work from home and how children can follow lessons.
- Make a family plan: what will you do, what will you need, who looks after whom if someone becomes ill.
- Put the number of your GP and of the GP out-of-hours service on a paper list of contact people.
- Check what your health insurance covers, including remote consultations.
- Keep a secure digital copy of your important documents. Watch out for scams and fraud.

## During

### At home

**Washing your hands, in five steps.**

1. Wet your hands with clean running water, turn off the tap and apply soap.
2. Work up a lather, including on the backs of your hands, between your fingers and under your nails.
3. Rub for at least 20 seconds. Hum a song twice as a timer.
4. Rinse well under clean running water.
5. Dry with a clean towel or a hand dryer.

If there is no soap and water, use hand gel with at least 60 per cent alcohol. Soap and water remains the best choice in most cases.

**When.** Wash your hands before, during and after preparing food, before and after eating, before and after caring for someone who is ill with vomiting or diarrhoea, before and after treating a wound, after using the toilet or changing a nappy, after blowing your nose, coughing or sneezing, and after contact with animals, animal food or waste.

**If you are ill yourself.**

- Consult your GP if you have flu symptoms or do not feel well. Phone first.
- Outside your GP's hours you can go to the GP out-of-hours service. In Ghent you call 1733 for this.
- If you cannot get through and it is urgent, see Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.
- Stay away from school, work and public places as long as you are ill.
- At home, also stay away as much as possible from members of the household who are not ill.
- Wear a face mask if you are around others or have to leave the house.
- Cough or sneeze into a paper tissue or into your arm. Throw the tissue into a closed bin. Then wash your hands.
- Drink enough: 1.5 litres a day.
- Air your home regularly.

**When you may go out again.** Follow the rules of the health services for the disease in question. As a general rule for respiratory infections, the American CDC says: you resume your activities when your symptoms have been improving overall for at least 24 hours and you have had no fever in those 24 hours, without fever-reducing medication. After that, take extra precautions around other people for another 5 days: fresh air, good hygiene, a well-fitting face mask, distance. You can still pass on the virus after you feel better. If you get a fever again or feel worse, stay at home again.

**If someone you live with or a friend is ill.**

- Wash your hands regularly with soap and water.
- Do not share glasses or plates with the person who is ill.
- Avoid close contact.
- Consider wearing a face mask.
- Air shared rooms regularly.
- Disinfect frequently touched surfaces, such as door handles and taps.
- Wash the laundry of the person who is ill at 60 °C or higher.

### Outdoors or on the move

- Keep your distance from people outside your household, as the health services recommend.
- Wear a face mask in public places if the authorities ask you to.
- Do not touch your eyes, nose and mouth with unwashed hands.
- Take hand gel with you for when there is no soap and water.
- If you are travelling, check the travel advice beforehand.

### In the car

- If you need to see a doctor, phone first.
- In a medical emergency, call 112. If that does not work, have someone else call or ask for help nearby (see Emergency numbers, If you cannot call). Stay where you are, wearing a face mask if possible, until help arrives.

### At work or at school

- Stay at home if you are ill. Do not go to work or school.
- Employers can provide for working from home and flexible rules for sick leave and carer's leave, so that employees who are ill and those caring for a sick family member can stay at home.
- Children who are ill stay at home from school and childcare.

### Children

- The rules also apply to children: wash hands properly, cough into the elbow, stay at home when ill.
- Prepare for a closure of school or childcare: arrange childcare and remote lessons.
- For young children, include nappies, baby food and spare clothing in your stock.

### Vulnerable people

- Some people are at greater risk of serious illness. Take extra precautions when you are with them, especially in the 5 days after you were ill yourself.
- People with a weakened immune system can keep passing on a virus for a long time.
- Make sure they have enough stock of their regular medication.
- Get vaccinated on the advice of your GP.

### Pets and animals

- Wash your hands after contact with animals, animal food and droppings.
- Bird flu can infect other animal species, including humans, although this rarely happens. Wild mammals, such as foxes, have also been infected by eating sick and dead birds.
- Do not touch dead or sick wild birds.
- If the phone works, report dead birds and dead foxes to the free influenza line of the FASFC (Federal Agency for the Safety of the Food Chain): 0800 99 777. This number can only be reached in the morning on weekdays. You can also contact the bird rescue centre (VOC) designated by the province, reachable from 08:00 to 22:00, also at weekends and on public holidays.
- Keep pets away from dead and sick birds.

## Afterwards

- Keep up the good habits: stay at home if you are ill, follow your doctor's advice, cough into your elbow and wash your hands.
- Wash the laundry of the person who was ill at 60 °C or higher.
- Review and improve your family plan. What went well, what did you need?
- Replenish your stock.
- Look after your mental health. It is normal to feel anxious. Keep in touch with others, also at a distance, and talk to someone if you are upset.

Source: [National Crisis Center: infectious diseases](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/health-risks/infectious-diseases) · [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/pandemic)

## Do not

- Do not go to work, school or public places when you are ill.
- Do not go to the GP unannounced. Phone first, unless it is an emergency.
- Do not share glasses or plates with someone who is ill.
- Do not use a cloth handkerchief. Throw paper tissues away immediately.
- Do not stockpile. Buy gradually, so that everyone has enough.
- Do not touch dead or sick wild birds.
- Do not spread incorrect information. It can cause harm and stigma.
