## Recognise

**When is it a cold wave**

- A cold wave is a period of at least five days in which it keeps freezing during the day as well, with a minimum temperature below -10 °C on at least three of those days.
- Wind makes the cold feel more severe.

**RMI cold warnings**

| Code | Threshold (maximum temperature) | What to do according to the RMI |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow | below -3 °C | Be vigilant. Protect people, animals and plants. Protect water pipes and taps. Dress appropriately. |
| Orange | -5 °C or lower | Limit exposure to the cold, dress warmly in several layers, avoid long or heavy exertion, offer homeless people a hot drink. Follow the advice of the authorities. |
| Red | -10 °C or lower | Stay indoors as much as possible. Wear a hat outside. Avoid heavy exertion and alcohol. Take in homeless people. Take a hot drink and a blanket with you in the car. Bring animals to a warmer place. Strictly follow the advice of the authorities. |

The RMI also warns of slippery conditions due to snow, freezing rain and hoar frost, and issues a warning without a colour code for CO poisoning when the weather makes it harder for flue gases to escape.

**Hypothermia and frostbite**

| | Signs |
|---|---|
| Hypothermia | Shivering, exhaustion, confusion, clumsy hands, memory loss, slurred speech, drowsiness. In babies: bright red, cold skin and little energy. |
| Frostbite | Redness or pain, then white or greyish-yellow skin, skin that feels hard or waxy, numbness. Especially the nose, ears, cheeks, chin, fingers and toes. People with frostbitten skin often do not notice it because the skin is numb. |

**CO poisoning**

- Signs: sudden headache, nausea, dizziness, tiredness, fainting. Suspect CO if several people or pets have symptoms at the same time, if symptoms appear while bathing or showering, or if they improve in fresh air.
- Other signs: soot on the wall near a water heater, a lot of condensation and yellow flames. A gas flame should be blue.

## Prepare

**Heating and home**

- Before the winter, check that your heating appliances work properly.
- Have appliances and chimneys serviced regularly by a professional. Central heating on heating oil: servicing and chimney sweeping every year. Stoves on coal, wood, heating oil or pellets: every year. Gas appliances: every two years in Flanders, every three years in Wallonia and Brussels.
- Check the insulation of your pipes. Insulate water pipes along outside walls. Cold can damage pipes and interrupt your heating.
- Seal gaps around windows and doors, but never block all gaps in a room with a combustion appliance. A bathroom with a water heater without a flue must by law have a grille of at least 150 cm² that cannot be closed.
- Fit a CO alarm near every appliance that poses a risk, at a height of about 1.5 metres. Choose a stand-alone CO alarm complying with standard NBN EN 50291. Also fit smoke alarms.
- If you have an open fire or stove, lay in a stock of wood, briquettes or coal.

**If the heating or the power fails**

- Keep blankets, sleeping bags and warm clothing ready: wool, hat, gloves and scarf.
- An emergency kit for at least 72 hours, with water, food you can eat cold, a torch, a battery-powered radio and a power bank.
- If you buy a gas heater, choose one with protection against oxygen depletion and a flame failure device.

**The car**

- Fit winter tyres, from about 7 °C, with at least 4 mm of tread.
- Have the battery tested before the first frost.
- Put a blanket, an ice scraper and a bottle of water in the car. Also useful: warm clothing, a torch, a charged mobile phone with charger, something to eat and sand or grit salt.
- Keep the tank well filled.

## During

### At home

**The heating works**

- Set the thermostat to a minimum temperature that is still comfortable. Do not set it too high.
- Close off rooms that you use little or not at all.
- Air your home for 10 minutes every day. Air it more in fog, still or mild weather, because flue gases then escape less well.

**The heating or the power fails**

In order of preference:

1. Stay in one room, preferably the smallest, and keep the doors closed. Hang blankets over the windows and put rugs or mats on the floor.
2. Dress in layers and use blankets and sleeping bags. Warm clothing and blankets can replace the heating for a while.
3. Use a stove or open fire that is properly connected to a chimney, with dry wood, and air the room regularly.
4. A portable paraffin or gas heater only if there is no other option: no more than one hour at a time, never while you sleep, and with ventilation.
5. If you cannot stay warm, go to friends or family, or to a reception centre run by your municipality.

**Avoiding carbon monoxide**

- Never use a gas cooker or oven for heating. Do not put an upturned flowerpot over a gas burner.
- Never use a barbecue or charcoal indoors or in the garage.
- Never run a generator or engine indoors or in a closed garage.
- Choose an electric heater for bedrooms.
- Do not put a heater that is too large in a small room and keep the heater's air vents open.

**If you suspect CO poisoning**

1. Make sure of your own safety first. Do not enter a room where someone is lying unconscious without protection.
2. Open windows and doors.
3. Switch off the appliance if it is safe to do so.
4. Bring the casualty out of the room.
5. Call 112. Say where you are, that it may be CO and how many people are unconscious.
6. Start resuscitation if the casualty is not breathing. Put someone who is unconscious but breathing in the recovery position.
7. If there is no immediate danger, still call 112 for help. For questions you can call the Poison Centre on 070 245 245.

### Outdoors or on the move

**Clothing**

- Wear several loose layers and cover your whole body. Without a hat you can lose about 30 per cent of your body heat.
- Cover your face and mouth with a scarf, wear mittens or gloves, a water-repellent coat and boots.
- Cover especially your nose, ears, toes, cheeks, chin and fingers.

**Behaviour**

- Limit the time you spend outside and avoid heavy exertion. Clearing snow or walking through snow puts a strain on the heart.
- Watch out for slippery surfaces. Report slippery conditions via the RMI app, so that gritting services can act sooner.
- Cyclists: ride more slowly and keep a greater distance, so that you need to brake less.

### In the car

- Postpone your journey if you can.
- Adjust your speed and be extra careful on bends. In snow or ice keep at least 4 seconds' distance and avoid sudden braking, steering or gear changes.
- Tram rails, manhole covers, bridges and viaducts can be very slippery. Leave at least 1.5 metres of space when you overtake cyclists and pedestrians.
- Before you set off, clear all windows, mirrors, lights, the roof and the bonnet of snow and ice. Never pour boiling water on the windscreen.
- Use dipped headlights at dusk, in the dark or when visibility is less than 200 metres.
- An electric car can have 20 to 30 per cent less range in the cold.
- Take a blanket, a hot drink or water with you, in case you get stuck in traffic.
- If you are stuck in snow or traffic, stay in your car.
- Never let the engine run in an attached garage, not even with the door open.

### At work or at school

- Follow the advice of the authorities and the measures of your municipality in case of code orange or red.
- If you work outdoors: limit your exposure, dress in layers and avoid long or heavy exertion.

Source: [RMI: cold legend](https://www.meteo.be/en/weather/warnings/legend-cold) · [CDC: winter weather](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html)

### Children

- Babies in a cold room are particularly at risk of hypothermia. Watch for bright red, cold skin and little energy.
- Young children breathe faster than adults and absorb CO more quickly.

Source: [CDC: winter weather](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/index.html) · [Poison Centre: CO poisoning](https://www.antigifcentrum.be/Toxinfos/co-vergiftiging/)

### Vulnerable people

- Older people without enough food, clothing or heating, people who are outside for a long time, people who use a lot of alcohol or drugs and babies in cold rooms are most at risk of hypothermia.
- For people older than 65, hang an easy-to-read thermometer in a place where it is seen often.
- Pregnant women, people with cardiovascular disease, thyroid problems, asthma or anaemia and older people are more sensitive to CO.
- Offer homeless people a hot drink. With code red the RMI asks for homeless people to be taken in.
- Check whether neighbours need help, especially if the heating or power fails.

### Pets and animals

**Shelter**

- Animals that live outdoors need a sturdy, waterproof and windproof shelter that is large enough. Trees, shrubs and hedges are not enough in winter.
- Lay a thick layer of dry bedding, such as straw. Put movable hutches on a raised base, such as wooden pallets, and bring them inside in frost if possible.
- Young and old animals do not belong in the field in freezing weather. Provide dry places in the field. Sheep, goats and donkeys cope less well with rain than cattle and horses.

**Water and food**

- Remove ice and change the water every few hours, or use a heated drinking trough.
- Never add antifreeze or salt to drinking water, and preferably no sugar either.
- Animals need more food. At -10 °C an animal can use about 35 per cent more energy.

**Dogs and cats**

- Walk the dog at the warmest time of the day, preferably in the sun.
- Grit salt can damage the pads of the paws. Trim the hair between the pads, rub the paws with petroleum jelly before the walk and rinse them afterwards with lukewarm water and dry them well.
- Outdoor cats sometimes crawl into the engine compartment of a car. Knock on the bonnet before you set off.

**Poultry**

- In severe frost, rub the combs and wattles of chickens with petroleum jelly against frostbite.

## Afterwards

**In case of hypothermia**

1. Hypothermia is a medical emergency. Call 112 if you suspect severe hypothermia, especially with a body temperature below 35 °C (95 °F).
2. Bring the person to a warm place and remove wet clothing.
3. Warm the centre of the body first: chest, neck, head and groin, with dry layers or blankets. Also wrap the head and neck.
4. A hot drink can help, never alcohol. Do not give anything to drink to someone who is unconscious.
5. If someone does not seem to be breathing, start resuscitation and continue until the person responds or the emergency services arrive.

**In case of frostbite**

1. Seek medical help.
2. Bring the person to a warm place and remove wet clothing.
3. Put the frostbitten parts in water that feels warm, or warm them with body heat.
4. Do not rub or massage. Do not use a fire, heat lamp, radiator, heater, heating pad or electric blanket to warm frostbitten skin.
5. Do not walk on frostbitten feet or toes unless you have to.

**When it thaws**

- Watch for damaged or leaking pipes. Frozen pipes may have burst.

## Do not

- Do not heat with a gas cooker, oven, barbecue or charcoal.
- Do not leave a portable paraffin or gas heater burning while you sleep.
- Do not block all gaps in a room with a combustion appliance.
- Do not run a generator or engine indoors or in a garage.
- Do not rub, massage or warm frostbitten skin with a heat source.
- Do not drink alcohol to warm up.
- Do not put antifreeze or salt in animals' drinking water.
- Do not leave an animal in the car in freezing weather.
