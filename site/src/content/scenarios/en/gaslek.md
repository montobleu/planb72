## Recognise

**Smell of gas**

- You smell gas in a room, in the stairwell or in the street.
- A gas leak can cause an explosion in a residential area. In such an explosion, buildings up to 500 metres away can be at risk. Strong wind or drought can make a fire or explosion worse.

**Gas bottles**

- When they leak, propane and butane sink downwards and ignite at the slightest spark. That is why such bottles do not belong in a cellar, storeroom or boiler room.

**No smell of gas, but symptoms: think of carbon monoxide**

Carbon monoxide (CO) is something different from a gas leak. CO has no smell and no colour and is produced by incomplete combustion, for example in a heating appliance or water heater running on natural gas.

- Symptoms: headache, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, tiredness, fainting. They usually disappear when you leave the room.
- Suspect CO if several people or pets have symptoms at the same time, or if the symptoms appear while bathing, showering or washing up.
- Other signs: soot near the water heater, a lot of condensation in the house and yellow flames. A gas flame should be blue.

**Too much, too little or no gas**

- If your gas appliance is not working properly because the pressure is too high or too low, or if no natural gas is coming through, that is a fault and not a leak.

## Prepare

**Know where and who**

- Know where your gas meter and the gas meter valve are, and how to turn the valve off. Make sure they are easy to reach.
- Write down the gas odour number of your network operator on your paper list of emergency numbers:
  - Fluvius (Flanders): 0800 65 0 65, free, day and night. Calls to this number get priority.
  - ORES (most of Wallonia): 0800 87 087, free, day and night.
  - If you live in Brussels (Sibelga) or in part of the province of Liège (RESA), look up the gas odour number on your network operator's website in advance and write it down.
- Deaf or hard of hearing: Fluvius has a contact form for reporting a gas smell for people who cannot phone, but only via the internet. So also agree with members of your household or neighbours that they will call for you.

**Maintenance**

- Have gas appliances checked by a qualified professional: every two years in Flanders, every three years in Wallonia and Brussels.
- Choose appliances with a CE mark. New appliances have a safety device that switches off if the flue is not working properly.
- Fit a CO alarm near every appliance that poses a risk, at a height of about 1.5 metres, complying with standard NBN EN 50291.

**Gas bottles**

- Do not store propane or butane bottles in a cellar, storeroom or boiler room.

**Family**

- Agree who helps whom to get outside, and where you will gather outside.

## During

### At home

**You smell gas in your home or your building**

1. Stay calm.
2. Avoid fire, sparks and electricity. Do not switch lights or electrical appliances on or off. Do not touch any switches. Do not light a candle and do not smoke.
3. Do not use a phone or mobile phone indoors if the leak seems to be coming from your home.
4. Turn off the gas meter valve.
5. Open windows and doors wide.
6. Leave the building with everyone you live with. Warn the neighbours by knocking on their door.
7. Outside, in a place where you can no longer smell gas, call your network operator's gas odour number.
8. Wait outside until the response team arrives. Stay at a safe distance as long as the leak has not been sealed.

The response team sets off immediately after your call, measures the gas concentration on site and looks for the leak.

If you cannot get through, have someone else call or ask for help nearby, always in a place where you cannot smell gas. See Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.

**Gas fire or explosion**

- Stay well away.
- Let the fire burn as long as the leak has not been repaired. Do not try to put it out.
- Call 112 and then your network operator's gas odour number. If you cannot get through, see above.

**Pressure too high or too low, or no natural gas**

- Turn off the gas meter valve and switch off your gas appliances.
- Report the fault to your network operator if you can get through by phone. For Fluvius this is the fault number 078 35 35 00.

**Suspected CO poisoning**

1. Make sure of your own safety first. Do not enter a room where someone is lying unconscious without protection.
2. Open windows and doors.
3. Switch off the gas appliance if it is safe to do so.
4. Leave the room and bring the casualty outside.
5. Call 112 or have someone call. Say that it may be CO and whether anyone is unconscious. If you cannot get through, see above.
6. Start resuscitation if the casualty is not breathing. Put someone who is unconscious but breathing in the recovery position.
7. For questions where there is no immediate danger, if the network is working: Poison Centre 070 245 245, or a qualified installer.

### Outdoors or on the move

**You smell gas in the street or outside a building**

- Avoid sparks, fire and anything that can ignite.
- Stay well away.
- Call your network operator's gas odour number. If that does not work, have someone else call or ask for help nearby.

**A larger gas leak in your area**

- Wait for the instructions of the authorities or the emergency services. Sometimes staying inside is safer than leaving.
- They come via the radio and police loudspeaker vans, and if the network is working also via BE-Alert and government websites.
- If you have to evacuate, take your identity card, money, water, mobile phone and charger, keys, your list of phone numbers, essential medicines and hygiene products. If there is immediate danger and you do not have an emergency bag ready, leave immediately without taking anything.
- Help neighbours with reduced mobility.
- Only return when the authorities say it is safe.

### In the car

- Stay away from a gas leak and follow the instructions of the emergency services.
- If you have to evacuate over a longer distance, you can do so in your own vehicle. For short distances you usually go on foot.

Source: [Fluvius: gas odour](https://www.fluvius.be/nl/gasgeur) · [National Crisis Center: evacuating](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/evacuate)

### At work or at school

- Follow the emergency plan of your workplace or school and the instructions of the people in charge.
- Apply the same rules as at home: no switches, no sparks, windows open, go outside, and phone from outside.

Source: [Fluvius: gas odour](https://www.fluvius.be/nl/gasgeur) · [National Crisis Center: evacuating](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/evacuate)

### Children

- Agree in advance who will take the children outside.
- Young children breathe faster than adults and absorb CO more quickly. If CO is suspected, bring them into the fresh air immediately.

Source: [National Crisis Center: evacuating](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/evacuate) · [Poison Centre: CO poisoning](https://www.antigifcentrum.be/Toxinfos/co-vergiftiging/)

### Vulnerable people

- Agree who will help members of the household or neighbours with reduced mobility, and who will warn them.
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing people who smell gas: go to a place where you can no longer smell gas and ask neighbours or bystanders to call. If the internet is working, also fill in the Fluvius contact form for deaf and hard-of-hearing people.
- Pregnant women, older people and people with cardiovascular disease, thyroid problems, asthma or anaemia are particularly sensitive to CO.

### Pets and animals

- Take your pet with you when you leave the building. If that is not possible, tell the emergency services where it is and how it reacts to strangers.
- If pets have symptoms at the same time as people, think of CO.

## Afterwards

- Have your gas appliances and chimney checked regularly by a qualified professional. For questions where there is no urgent danger you can contact a certified installer.
- After CO poisoning, nervous system symptoms can still appear after some time, such as irritability, memory problems or abnormal movements. These often disappear, but not always.

## Do not

- Do not operate any switch or electrical appliance.
- Do not phone indoors with your mobile if the leak seems to be coming from your home.
- Do not use a fire, candle or lighter and do not smoke.
- Do not put out a gas fire at a leak.
- Do not go back inside as long as the leak has not been sealed.
- Do not store propane or butane bottles in a cellar, storeroom or boiler room.
- Do not use a gas appliance with a yellow flame or soot.
