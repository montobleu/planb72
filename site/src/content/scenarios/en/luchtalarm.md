## Recognise

**The Belgian situation.** The National Crisis Center describes an international armed conflict as a risk with a very low probability. The scenario it examined takes place on NATO territory outside Belgium. On that page the Crisis Center gives no steps of its own for an air attack. The steps on this card therefore come from the official guides of Germany, Sweden, Ukraine and Poland.

**How you are warned.** In Belgium the authorities warn you via radio and TV and via police loudspeaker vans driving through the streets. If the network is working, also via BE-Alert: text message, email or phone call. In a major emergency everyone with a mobile phone in the affected zone receives an Alert-SMS, even without registering. With a battery-powered or wind-up radio you can also listen to the media during a power cut.

**Alarm signals abroad.** Every country has its own siren signal. Know the signal of the country where you are staying.

- **Sweden:** an air raid alert is short blasts of 2 seconds on and 2 seconds off, for 2 minutes. The all-clear is one long, continuous tone of 30 seconds.
- **Poland:** the start of the alert is a wavering siren tone of 3 minutes. The end of the alert is a steady tone of 3 minutes. When the alert sounds, turn on the radio or TV.

**What the danger is.** In an explosion the greatest danger comes from the blast wave and from flying debris and glass, not only from the impact itself. Thick walls and earth protect against the blast wave, fragments and radiation.

## Prepare

- **Find your shelter locations.** Choose two or three places close to home, work and school. Walk the route in advance. Know who has the key.
- **Choose your place at home.** See which room has the fewest outside walls, windows and doors: a cellar without windows, or a hallway, storeroom or walk-in cupboard in the middle of the home.
- **Have an emergency bag ready** and put it by the door. The Swedish MCF lists: water in bottles or cans, food that keeps at room temperature, hygiene products and toilet paper, medication and a first aid kit, warm clothing, identity card, cash and bank cards, keys, mobile phone with charger or power bank, and a torch. Ukraine adds: spare batteries, blankets or thermal blankets, a battery-powered radio and a fire extinguisher. Plan for a stay of at least two days.
- **Keep a stock at home** for at least 3 days, as the National Crisis Center recommends: water, long-life food, a radio, a torch, medication.
- **Reinforce windows** with strong adhesive film. If possible, block them with sandbags or sturdy furniture.
- **Make a plan** with your family: who does what, where you will meet, how you will reach each other. Agree where you will find each other again if you cannot reach each other. Also agree with your neighbours how you will reach each other without phone or internet.
- **Practise.** Establish the route to your shelter in advance and follow it when the alert sounds.

## During

When the alert sounds, go immediately to the nearest shelter.

**Ranking of shelter locations, from best to least good:**

1. **An official shelter**, where one exists. In Sweden, Poland and Ukraine these are marked with a sign.
2. **Underground:** a cellar without windows, an underground car park, a metro station, a tunnel or an underpass. A cellar protects less than a real shelter.
3. **An interior room in a building:** without windows, with as few outside walls, windows and doors as possible. Think of a hallway, a storeroom, a walk-in cupboard or a stairwell without much glass, close to a load-bearing interior wall.
4. **The two-wall rule:** make sure there are at least two solid walls between you and the outside. If the outside wall is damaged, the second wall stops debris and glass. The Ukrainian guide warns: this rule does not give complete safety. Go to a real shelter as quickly as possible.
5. **A bathroom** only as a last resort, and only if it has no window and no gas water heater or gas boiler.

### At home

- Go to your shelter location. Avoid the top floor and the attic.
- Stay away from windows, balconies, mirrors, glass cabinets and the kitchen.
- Do not stand opposite a window.
- Shut off the gas, electricity and water and close windows and vents before you leave, if there is time.
- If you cannot leave your room on your own, let your neighbours know.
- If you live in a flat without a cellar: choose the hallway or corridor in the middle of the home, or the stairwell if it has no large windows. Stay there close to an interior wall, away from windows and the front door.

### Outdoors or on the move

**Best option:** go into the nearest building. Choose a public building such as a town hall, school or museum, an underground car park or a metro station. Stay well away from open entrances, windows and doors there.

**No building nearby:** look for a tunnel, underpass or wall. Even a simple hollow in the ground protects better than staying in open terrain.

**If you hear an explosion or a whistling projectile:**

- Drop to the ground immediately, preferably in a hollow, ditch or behind a high kerb.
- Cover your head with your hands or a bag.
- Lie flat. The Ukrainian guide also mentions: cover your ears with your palms and open your mouth.
- Wait for the first salvo to pass. Then go to a deep space close to an exit.

**Drones.** If you see or hear a drone, go to a shelter immediately. If there is no shelter nearby and a drone is dropping explosives, move in a zigzag and change direction every 7 to 10 metres. Stay away from government buildings and strategic and military sites.

### In the car

- In a drone attack: get out immediately, move away from the road and find cover.
- On public transport: ask the driver to stop immediately, get out, move away from the road and lie down.
- Do not shelter next to or behind vehicles. They are often a target.

### At work or at school

- Find the shelter locations near your work and your children's school in advance.
- Take the stairs, not the lift.
- In Ukraine schools have their own shelter for pupils and staff, with at least 2 litres of drinking water per person per day.

### Children

- Talk to children in a way that suits their age. Be honest, but avoid harsh details.
- Explain their task during an alert and practise it together. A child who knows their role feels safer.
- Stay close, listen and show support. Your calm helps the child.
- Teach children never to pick up "interesting" objects. If they see something suspicious, they go back the same way and tell an adult.

### Vulnerable people

- Help older people, sick people and people with a disability. Not everyone knows that there is danger. Give them the most important information and explain what they need to do.
- Make a plan together in advance. Help them pack their emergency bag.
- Make sure there is enough medication and there are batteries for medical devices, such as hearing aids and glucose meters.
- If someone cannot leave on their own, report this to the emergency services: on site, or via 112 if you can get through.

### Pets and animals

- Have ready for your pet: medical papers and proof of vaccination, a pet carrier or cage, collar or harness, a muzzle, and food, water and medication for a few days.
- Have your animal microchipped and put your contact details on the collar.
- Take water, food and bags for droppings with you into the shelter.
- If you have farm animals: have food and water ready for 14 days. If you cannot evacuate them, tell the emergency services which animals are staying behind.

### In the shelter

- Do not smoke, do not make noise and do not light candles without permission. Move as little as possible.
- Follow the announcements on the radio.
- Do not phone to ask for information. If the network is working, use text messages to relieve the network.
- Do not leave the shelter before the official end of the alert. Only leave earlier if the shelter itself is damaged.
- After a salvo, stay in shelter for at least 10 minutes. The shelling may start again.
- See who near you needs help.

## Afterwards

**After an explosion nearby.**

- Your own safety comes first.
- Treat the surroundings as a danger zone. Leave immediately and help others if it is safe to do so. Take the stairs, not the lift.
- Keep your distance from damaged buildings and dust clouds: about half of the original height of the building.
- Watch out for exposed cables, broken water pipes, a smell of gas or hissing. Do not use matches or a lighter and leave immediately.
- Expect further collapses.
- Do not enter rooms full of smoke. Smoke gases are deadly. Close doors and windows to the burning room.
- Do not pull people out from under the rubble yourself. Call for help, keep the person still and calm and follow the instructions of the rescuers. Do not dig in ruins yourself.
- If you are trapped under rubble yourself: call for help, carefully free your limbs and do not touch anything that is supporting the rubble.
- Call 112 in an emergency. Say where, what and who is calling, and wait for questions. Keep calls short.
- If you cannot get through: have someone else call or try another phone. If calling does not work at all, point directly at a bystander to fetch help and tell them where it is, what has happened, how many casualties there are and what state they are in. Only leave a casualty alone if there is no other option. See also Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.

**Unexploded ammunition, suspicious objects and debris.**

- Stop. Do not go any closer.
- Do not touch the object. Do not move it, do not pick it up and do not look at it from close by.
- Go back the same way to a safe distance.
- Warn the people around you and alert the emergency services.
- Do not use a walkie-talkie within 15 metres of the object.
- Do not mark the spot yourself. Do not move obstacles on a road or path.
- Never touch a crashed drone or its debris. Report the location.
- In Belgium the Belgian Defence bomb disposal service DOVO clears ammunition and suspicious packages. DOVO receives an average of ten calls a day, mainly for ammunition from the two world wars.

**Information.**

- Check information in several official sources. Do not spread unconfirmed reports.
- Do not believe reports about the collapse or surrender of the country.
- Do not film or photograph soldiers, military vehicles or important buildings such as bridges and stations, and do not share images of them.
- Do not share images of impacts or of missiles in the air. Also do not share where and when something hit, not even in comments.

## Do not

- Do not shelter in doorways, under arches, on stairs on the outside of a building or with a lot of glass, or in the cellar of a prefabricated building.
- Do not shelter next to or behind cars or kiosks, and not near petrol stations, military vehicles or government buildings.
- Do not use a lift.
- Do not stand and watch the attack.
- Do not go outside before the official end of the alert.
- Do not touch ammunition, suspicious objects or drone wreckage.
- Do not film or share impacts, troops or defensive actions.
- Do not phone to ask for information.
