## Recognise

Phone and internet can fail in different ways. Each form calls for a different approach.

**Overloaded network**

- During exceptional events the mobile networks quickly become overloaded. Calls then fail or drop out, while messages sometimes still get through.

**Failure due to a power cut**

- According to the FPS Economy, mobile masts last one to two hours without power. Mobile internet (4G and 5G) drops out quickly, probably straight away.
- A landline that runs via the internet only works if the router, the fibre or cable connection and the handset itself have power. In the past the traditional landline kept working for a few more hours. That is no longer the case.
- The FPS Economy considers it unlikely that your landline will still work. If you still hear a dial tone, you can call the emergency services.
- If you have enough signal, you can call 112 via any operator. The emergency numbers 100 and 101 are then not guaranteed.
- Your own mobile phone also needs a charged battery.

**Fault in or attack on the digital infrastructure**

- Phone, internet and mobile network can also fail because of a technical defect, a solar storm, a tornado or an attack on critical infrastructure.
- During a prolonged telecommunications outage, services that depend on it also stop working, such as paying by card.

## Prepare

**Arrangements with your family and your neighbours**

- Agree with your family where you will meet if you cannot reach each other by phone or internet. Write the address down in your emergency plan.
- Choose a contact person outside your own region. Everyone lets that person know that they are safe.
- Agree how you can reach each other and who needs extra help.
- Agree with your neighbours how you will reach each other without phone or internet, and who has useful equipment, such as a battery-powered radio.
- Also keep your emergency plan on paper. You will not have a plan on your phone or computer if the power or internet fails.
- Make a paper list of important phone numbers: family, friends, utility companies, your GP and your insurer.

**Where to ask for help if you cannot call**

- Ask your municipal administration what arrangements your municipality has made for when calling is no longer possible. Mayors and governors have prepared emergency measures, for example an information and reception point for the public.
- In Germany the BBK recommends asking your municipality where you can report an emergency if the phone network fails.
- In Luxembourg the government recommends knowing in advance where the nearest fire station, police station and hospital are, near your home and your work. The map in the app shows them, with the Nearest button.

**Receiving information without internet**

- Keep a battery-powered or wind-up radio ready. Choose a set that receives FM and preferably also DAB+. An FM radio also works when power and internet fail.
- Look up the frequency of your regional station now and tune your radio to it.
- Register with BE-Alert. You will then receive a text message, a voice call or an email. Registering and receiving messages is free. These messages only get through as long as the mobile network or the internet works.
- Subscribe in advance to the channels of your municipality, province, police zone and fire service zone. You can also only follow these as long as there is internet.

**Power for your devices**

- Keep phones, laptops and cordless phones charged, or have charged spare batteries.
- Have a charged power bank. A solar charger can also help.

**112 in other forms**

- Install the 112 BE app and register. With the app you choose the fire service, ambulance or police with one button. The app passes on your location using satellite positioning and has a chat function for deaf and hard-of-hearing people and people with a speech impairment. Like calling, it only works if there is a network.
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing people can also request urgent medical help and police assistance by text message. Separate four-digit numbers apply for this. You can get them through the associations for deaf and hard-of-hearing people or through the emergency services.

**Money and payments**

- Keep some cash at home. If the network fails, you may not be able to pay by card or withdraw money. The Crisis Center recommends enough for basic needs for about 72 hours. Denk vooruit (the Netherlands) mentions 70 euros per adult and 30 euros per child.

## During

### At home

**Following the news**

- Listen to the battery-powered or wind-up radio, or the car radio. It also works when the mobile network and internet fail.
- The authorities can also inform you with police loudspeaker vans driving through the streets, and with information boards, leaflets and door-to-door communication.
- If you do not have a radio yourself, go to neighbours who have one. Pass on the information from the authorities to neighbours who are isolated.
- If the network works, information also comes via BE-Alert, TV and online media, the websites of the municipality, province and Crisis Center, and social media.
- Be critical of what you read or hear. During an emergency, incorrect or misleading information circulates. Do not forward anything that does not come from an official source.

**Neighbours and family**

- Look out for neighbours or family who need extra help. If you cannot get through, go and see them.
- If you cannot reach your family, go to the meeting point in your emergency plan.

**Asking for help when someone's life is in danger**

1. Call 112 for an ambulance and the fire service, or 101 for urgent police assistance. Calling 112 is free throughout Europe. With a mobile phone it works without an active subscription, but only with a SIM card.
2. If the network works, you can also use the 112 BE app. It has a chat function for people who are deaf or hard of hearing or cannot speak.

**If you cannot get through**

- Try another device, landline or mobile, or have neighbours or bystanders call. Another operator may help. Move a few metres or turn around and try again.
- If you can still make calls but 112 cannot be reached, try 101. If the emergency call centres fail, temporary numbers per province may be active (see the guide Emergency call). Usually they are not active. Only use them in a real emergency.
- You may also call 112 from a place other than the incident. Tell the operator if so.
- If calling does not work at all: ask for help in your neighbourhood and follow the information from the authorities on the radio. The municipality may open an information and reception point.
- In Germany the authorities recommend going, if possible, to the nearest police station or hospital. In the Netherlands, during the nationwide 112 outage of 2019, the authorities sent people to the nearest fire station or police station, and to the hospital for medical emergencies. The map in the app shows them, with the Nearest button.

**Keeping the network free**

- Avoid unnecessary calls and unnecessary internet use. This keeps the network available for important calls.
- Only phone if it is really necessary and preferably send a text message. In major emergencies the Crisis Center recommends text messages or messaging apps instead of phoning.
- An outage in itself is not an emergency. Do not call the emergency numbers to ask what is going on.

**Battery**

- Keep your mobile phone and power bank charged. Remember that the phone network may also be disrupted even if your device works.

### Outdoors or on the move

- If you cannot reach your family, go to the meeting point in your emergency plan.
- If you have to leave, let your family and your contact person outside the area know as soon as you can where you are going and when you expect to arrive.
- Help neighbours with reduced mobility or little support, for example by warning them.

### At work or at school

- Discuss with colleagues what you will do if there is an emergency at work, and how you will reach each other if phone and internet fail.
- Check whether your children's school is prepared for an emergency.

### Children

- Agree with children where you will meet if you cannot phone each other. Practise the plan regularly.
- Share out tasks in advance, for example who collects the children.

### Vulnerable people

- A personal alarm and other home services can fail if the phone or power goes off. Ask your care service in advance how they can be reached during a long outage.
- Agree with neighbours who will go and check on them if phoning does not work.
- Deaf, hard of hearing or speech-impaired: register in advance in the 112 BE app. It has a chat function, but only works if there is a network. Also request the text message numbers for emergency help.

## Afterwards

- Wait a while before you phone again or go online. This gives the system time to stabilise.
- Let your contact person and your family know that you are safe.
- Check with vulnerable relatives and neighbours that everything is all right.
- Recharge your devices and power bank and restock your emergency kit.
- Update your paper list of phone numbers and your emergency plan if something did not work.

## Do not

- Do not call 112, 101 or other emergency numbers to ask for information about the outage. During earlier power failures this overloaded the emergency numbers and real emergency calls were answered later.
- Do not call 112 just because your mobile phone shows the message "network not available, emergency calls only".
- Do not use alternative emergency numbers for something that is not a real emergency.
- Do not forward unconfirmed messages.
- Do not make unnecessary calls or stream while the network is overloaded.
