## Recognise

**Overloaded network**

- During exceptional events the mobile networks quickly become overloaded. Calls fail or drop out; messages sometimes still get through.

**Failure due to a power cut**

- Mobile masts last one to two hours without power; mobile internet (4G and 5G) probably drops out straight away.
- A landline via the internet only works if the router, connection and handset have power. It is unlikely that your landline will still work; if you hear a dial tone, you can call the emergency services.
- With enough signal you can call 112 via any operator. 100 and 101 are then not guaranteed.

**Fault in or attack on the digital infrastructure**

- A technical defect, a solar storm, a tornado or an attack on critical infrastructure can also cut phone and internet. Services that depend on them, such as paying by card, then stop working too.

## Prepare

**Arrangements with your family and your neighbours**

- Agree a meeting point with your family for when you cannot reach each other, and agree who needs extra help.
- Choose a contact person outside your region whom everyone lets know that they are safe.
- Agree with your neighbours how you will reach each other without phone or internet, and who has useful equipment, such as a battery-powered radio.
- Keep your emergency plan and a list of important phone numbers on paper: family, friends, utility companies, GP and insurer.

**Where to ask for help if you cannot call**

- Ask your municipal administration what the municipality provides when calling is no longer possible, for example an information and reception point. The BBK (Germany) recommends the same for reporting an emergency.
- Know where the nearest fire station, police and hospital are, near your home and your work (Luxembourg). The map in the app shows them, with the Nearest button.

**Receiving information without internet**

- Keep a battery-powered or wind-up radio ready, with FM and preferably DAB+, and tune it to your regional station now (see the Communication and alerting guide).
- Register with BE-Alert for free (text message, voice call or email) and follow the channels of the municipality, province, police zone and fire service zone. They only reach you as long as the mobile network or internet works.

**Power for your devices**

- Keep phones and laptops charged, with spare batteries and a charged power bank. A solar charger can also help.

**112 in other forms**

- Install and register the 112 BE app. It passes on your location and has a chat function for deaf and hard-of-hearing people and people with a speech impairment, but like calling it only works with a network.
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing people can also ask for help by text message, via separate four-digit numbers (see the Emergency call guide).

**Money and payments**

- Keep cash at home, enough for basic needs for about 72 hours (Crisis Center). Denk vooruit (the Netherlands) mentions 70 euros per adult and 30 euros per child.

## During

### At home

**Following the news**

- Listen to the battery-powered or wind-up radio, or the car radio. Also watch for police loudspeaker vans, information boards, leaflets and door-to-door communication.
- If you do not have a radio, go to neighbours who have one, and pass the information on to those who are isolated.
- If the network works, information also comes via BE-Alert, TV, websites and social media. Do not forward anything that does not come from an official source.

**Neighbours and family**

- Look out for neighbours or family who need extra help. If you cannot get through, go and see them.
- If you cannot reach your family, go to the meeting point in your emergency plan.

**Asking for help when someone's life is in danger**

- Call 112 for an ambulance and the fire service, or 101 for urgent police assistance. Calling 112 is free throughout Europe, with a mobile phone even without an active subscription, but only with a SIM card. If the network works, the 112 BE app is also an option.
- If you cannot get through, try another device or another place, or have neighbours or bystanders call. If 112 cannot be reached, try 101. If calling does not work at all, ask for help in your neighbourhood, follow the radio and go to the municipality's information and reception point if there is one. See Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call, and the Emergency call guide.

**Keeping the network free**

- Only phone if it is really necessary and preferably send a text or a message. Avoid unnecessary internet use.
- An outage in itself is not an emergency. Do not call the emergency numbers to ask what is going on.
- Keep your mobile phone and power bank charged.

### Outdoors or on the move

- If you have to leave, let your family and contact person know as soon as you can where you are going and when you will arrive.
- Warn neighbours with reduced mobility or little support.

### At work or at school

- Discuss with colleagues how you will reach each other if phone and internet fail.
- Check whether your children's school is prepared for an emergency.

### Children

- Agree a meeting point with children for when you cannot phone each other, and practise it regularly.
- Share out tasks in advance, for example who collects the children.

### Vulnerable people

- A personal alarm and other home services can fail. Ask your care service in advance how they can be reached during a long outage.
- Agree with neighbours who will go and check on them if phoning does not work.
- Deaf, hard of hearing or speech-impaired: register in advance in the 112 BE app and request the text message numbers for emergency help.

## Afterwards

- Wait a while before you phone again or go online, so that the system can stabilise.
- Let your contact person and family know that you are safe, and check with vulnerable relatives and neighbours that everything is all right.
- Recharge your devices and power bank and restock your emergency kit.
- Update your list of phone numbers and your emergency plan if something did not work.

## Do not

- Do not call 112, 101 or other emergency numbers for information about the outage. During earlier power failures this overloaded the emergency numbers.
- Do not call 112 just because your mobile phone shows "network not available, emergency calls only".
- Do not forward unconfirmed messages.
- Do not make unnecessary calls or stream while the network is overloaded.
