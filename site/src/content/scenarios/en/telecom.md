## Recognise

Phone and internet can fail in different ways. Each form calls for a different approach.

**Overloaded network**

- During exceptional events the mobile networks quickly become overloaded. Calls then fail or drop out, while messages sometimes still get through.

**Failure due to a power cut**

- A landline that runs via the internet only works if the router, the fibre or cable connection and the handset itself have power. In the past the traditional landline kept working for a few more hours. That is no longer the case.
- The mobile network has only partial backup power, and only for a short time. During a longer power cut the landline and mobile network fail after a short time.
- Your own mobile phone also needs a charged battery.

**Fault in or attack on the digital infrastructure**

- Phone, internet and mobile network can also fail because of a technical defect, a solar storm, a tornado or an attack on critical infrastructure.
- During a prolonged telecommunications outage, services that depend on it also stop working, such as paying by card.

## Prepare

**Arrangements with your family**

- Agree with your family where you will meet if you cannot reach each other by phone or internet. Write the address down in your emergency plan.
- Choose a contact person outside your own region. Everyone lets that person know that they are safe.
- Agree how you can reach each other and who needs extra help.
- Also keep your emergency plan on paper. You will not have a plan on your phone or computer if the power or internet fails.
- Make a paper list of important phone numbers: family, friends, utility companies, your GP and your insurer.

**Receiving information without internet**

- Keep a battery-powered or wind-up radio ready. Choose a set that receives FM and preferably also DAB+. An FM radio also works when power and internet fail.
- Look up the frequency of your regional station now and tune your radio to it.
- Register with BE-Alert. You will then receive a text message, a voice call or an email. Registering and receiving messages is free.
- Subscribe in advance to the channels of your municipality, province, police zone and fire service zone.

**Power for your devices**

- Keep phones, laptops and cordless phones charged, or have charged spare batteries.
- Have a charged power bank. A solar charger can also help.

**112 in other forms**

- Install the 112 BE app and register. With the app you choose the fire service, ambulance or police with one button. The app passes on your location using satellite positioning and has a chat function for deaf and hard-of-hearing people and people with a speech impairment.
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing people can also request urgent medical help and police assistance by text message. Separate four-digit numbers apply for this. You can get them through the associations for deaf and hard-of-hearing people or through the emergency services.

**Money and payments**

- Keep some cash at home. If the network fails, you may not be able to pay by card or withdraw money. The Crisis Center recommends enough for basic needs for about 72 hours. Denk vooruit (the Netherlands) mentions 70 euros per adult and 30 euros per child.

## During

### At home

**Asking for help when someone's life is in danger**

Try in this order:

1. Call 112 for an ambulance and the fire service, or 101 for urgent police assistance. Calling 112 is free throughout Europe.
2. Use the 112 BE app. It has a chat function for people who are deaf or hard of hearing or cannot speak.
3. If the emergency numbers cannot be reached, there are alternative numbers. Only use them in a real emergency.
4. Ask your municipality in advance where you can make an emergency call if the phone network fails.

**Keeping the network free**

- Avoid unnecessary calls and unnecessary internet use. This keeps the network available for important calls.
- Only phone if it is really necessary and preferably send a text message. In major emergencies the Crisis Center recommends text messages or WhatsApp instead of phoning.
- An outage in itself is not an emergency. Do not call the emergency numbers to ask what is going on.

**Following the news**

- In an emergency the authorities use several channels at the same time: BE-Alert, radio, TV and online media, the websites of the municipality, province and Crisis Center, social media and police loudspeaker vans driving through the streets.
- If the internet is not working, listen to the battery-powered radio.
- Be critical of what you read or hear. During an emergency, incorrect or misleading information circulates. Do not forward anything that does not come from an official source.

**Battery**

- Keep your mobile phone and power bank charged. Remember that the phone network may also be disrupted even if your device works.

### Outdoors or on the move

- If you cannot reach your family, go to the meeting point in your emergency plan.
- If you have to leave, let your family and your contact person outside the area know as soon as you can where you are going and when you expect to arrive.
- Help neighbours with reduced mobility or little support, for example by warning them.

### In the car

- Listen to the car radio for news and official announcements.

### At work or at school

- Discuss with colleagues what you will do if there is an emergency at work, and how you will reach each other if phone and internet fail.
- Check whether your children's school is prepared for an emergency.

### Children

- Agree with children where you will meet if you cannot phone each other. Practise the plan regularly.
- Share out tasks in advance, for example who collects the children.

### Vulnerable people

- A personal alarm and other home services can fail if the phone or power goes off. Ask your care service how they can be reached during a long outage.
- Agree with neighbours who will go and check on them if phoning does not work.
- Deaf, hard of hearing or speech-impaired: register in advance in the 112 BE app. It has a chat function. Also request the text message numbers for emergency help.

## Afterwards

- Wait a while before you phone again or go online. This gives the system time to stabilise.
- Let your contact person and your family know that you are safe.
- Recharge your devices and power bank and restock your emergency kit.
- Update your paper list of phone numbers and your emergency plan if something did not work.

## Do not

- Do not call 112, 101 or other emergency numbers to ask for information about the outage. During earlier power failures this overloaded the emergency numbers and real emergency calls were answered later.
- Do not use alternative emergency numbers for something that is not a real emergency.
- Do not forward unconfirmed messages.
- Do not make unnecessary calls or stream while the network is overloaded.
