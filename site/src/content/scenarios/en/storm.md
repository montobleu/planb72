## Recognise

**What is a storm**

- A storm has an average wind speed of at least 75 km/h (9 Beaufort). A severe storm starts at 89 km/h, a very severe storm at 103 km/h and a hurricane at 117 km/h.
- Tornadoes and downbursts do not fall under this definition.

**RMI colour codes for wind**

The RMI issues warnings per province. The thresholds depend on the season: from 15 April to 15 November the trees are in leaf and branches break off more easily, so lower thresholds apply.

| Code | Gusts | In the leaf season | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yellow | 80 to 100 km/h | 70 to 90 km/h | Be vigilant. |
| Orange | widespread 101 to 130 km/h | 91 to 120 km/h | Be prepared and follow the advice. |
| Red | widespread more than 130 km/h | more than 120 km/h | Take action and strictly follow the advice. |

- Code yellow can come up to 48 hours in advance, code orange up to 24 hours in advance, code red at the earliest 12 hours in advance. When there is uncertainty, a warning may come at shorter notice.
- The RMI also issues codes for thunderstorms, based on rain, gusts and hail. Code orange for thunderstorms means, among other things, widespread severe gusts or hail of 3 to 5 cm, code red extreme gusts or hail larger than 5 cm.
- The text accompanying the warning is more important than the colour. Always read it.
- Based on the codes, the municipality or province can close off areas or cancel events.

Source: [National Crisis Center: wind and storm](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/wind) · [RMI: explanation of the warnings](https://www.meteo.be/en/weather/warnings/info-warnings)

## Prepare

**Your home**

- Check that no roof tiles are loose.
- Clean the gutters every year.
- Have the chimney checked regularly. A chimney can collapse in a storm.
- Prune or fell trees that could fall on your house.
- Fit a central surge protector to protect your electrical installation against lightning strikes. A plug-in surge protector is an alternative.

**Before the storm arrives**

- Follow the weather forecast via the radio, TV, or the RMI website or app.
- Secure loose objects or bring them inside: garden furniture, trampolines, flowerpots, bins.
- Close windows and doors, especially on the windward side, and secure shutters.
- Move your car away from trees and fences, out of the wind.
- Bring pets inside.
- Have an emergency kit ready with a torch, battery-powered radio, charged power bank, water and food. A storm can cause a power cut.
- Take your mobile phone and a charger with you on every journey. Put a blanket, a warm coat and a bottle of water in the car.

## During

### At home

**Stay indoors**

- Stay indoors and close windows and doors. Indoors is safest.
- Stay away from windows, doors and outside walls. Watch out for flying debris.
- Listen to the weather forecast and follow the recommendations. If the power fails, use a battery-powered radio or the car radio.

**During a thunderstorm**

- Switch off electronic devices and unplug them.
- Do not use appliances that are plugged into the mains and avoid running water.

**Asking for help: whom do you alert?**

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Someone's life is in danger, or there is a risk of fire | Call 112 or have someone call. If you cannot get through, see below. |
| You need the fire service, but no one's life is in danger (roof tiles blown loose, a tree about to fall) | If the network works: submit a request via the online service 1722.be or your fire service zone's form. Only call 1722 if that number has been activated. |
| Damage without danger, for which you do not need the fire service (fallen tree in the garden, damaged garden shed) | Call in a professional, such as a roofer, or repair it yourself. |

- Do not call 112 for ordinary storm damage. In storms and severe thunderstorms the fire service becomes overloaded. The 1722.be online service is always available, but only via the internet.
- If the network works, report damage or unusual phenomena via the RMI app. This helps the RMI to warn others.

If you cannot get through, have someone else call or ask for help nearby, and follow the information from the authorities on the radio. See Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.

**If the power goes off**

- See the card Power cut. Use a torch instead of candles.

### Outdoors or on the move

**During a storm**

- In code red, only go outside if it is really necessary. In that case take water, food, a blanket, extra clothing and a charged mobile phone with you, and let someone know where you are going and by which route.
- Stay out of woods and parks and away from open water, because branches and trees may fall.
- Never shelter under a tree in strong winds.

**During a thunderstorm**

- Best option: go into a building, away from windows. Good option: a car with a roof.
- Stay away from metal structures, trees, high points and hills, fences, the edges of woods and open fields.
- Do not shelter under a tree. When lightning strikes, the sap in the trunk can vaporise and split the trunk open. Branches and wood can then fly up to 400 metres.
- Do not go swimming. Water conducts well: a strike in the water can be fatal up to 100 metres away.
- Do not walk during a lightning strike. Stand still and crouch down.

### In the car

- Postpone your journey if you can.
- Do not park near trees.
- Do not drive on flooded roads. According to Ready.gov, a few centimetres of fast-flowing water can knock you over and about 30 cm of water can carry a vehicle away.
- During a thunderstorm a car with a roof is a safe shelter. If your car is struck, the metal bodywork conducts the current via the roof and sides to the ground. You do not need to touch a post or crash barrier.

### At work or at school

- Follow the measures of the municipality or province. In code orange or red, areas may be closed off and activities cancelled.
- Agree in advance with colleagues what you will do in an emergency at work. Check whether your children's school is prepared.
- Know sturdy buildings near your work, school and the places where your children play, where you can shelter during a thunderstorm.

Source: [National Crisis Center: wind and storm](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/wind) · [National Crisis Center: make a plan](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan) · [Ready.gov: thunderstorms](https://www.ready.gov/thunderstorms-lightning)

### Children

- Keep children indoors during the storm and away from windows.
- Do not let children play in parks, woods or near trees during the storm.
- Teach children not to swim during a thunderstorm and not to shelter under a tree.

Source: [National Crisis Center: wind and storm](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/wind) · [National Crisis Center: lightning](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/lightning) · [Brandweer Nederland: thunderstorms](https://www.brandweer.nl/onderwerpen/onweer-donder-en-bliksem/)

### Vulnerable people

- Agree with neighbours how you will help each other, and how you will reach each other without phone or internet. Check whether neighbours who live alone or have reduced mobility need help.
- If you depend on electrically powered medical equipment, bear in mind that the power may fail. See the card Power cut.

Source: [National Crisis Center: make a plan](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan) · [National Crisis Center: plan in your neighbourhood](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/plan-your-neighbourhood-association-or-club) · [National Crisis Center: wind and storm](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/wind)

### Pets and animals

- Bring pets inside before the storm arrives.
- Make sure animals outdoors have a sheltered place, or take them with you. Provide food and water for at least 72 hours.
- Animals that live outdoors need a sturdy, waterproof and windproof shelter. Trees, shrubs and hedges are not enough.

## Afterwards

**Safety**

- Do not touch power lines lying on the ground. Also do not drive through water in which fallen cables may be lying.
- Watch out for fallen branches, fallen trees and loose roof tiles. Report them.
- Only go outside when the authorities and the weather forecast say it is safe. Watch out for possible flooding after heavy rain.

**Damage**

- If you need help from the fire service and no one's life is in danger: if the network works, request it via 1722.be, your zone's form or 1722 if that number is active. Keep 112 free for situations where someone's life is in danger.
- Otherwise call in a professional or repair it yourself, for example for a fallen tree, a damaged garden shed or hail damage to your car.
- Take photos of the damage for your insurance claim.
- Contact your insurer as soon as possible.
- Check whether the Flemish Disaster Fund (Vlaams Rampenfonds) covers your situation. This is only possible after a recognised disaster and in exceptional cases. Report the damage to your insurer first.

## Do not

- Do not shelter under a tree.
- Do not park near trees.
- Do not drive on flooded roads.
- Do not swim during a thunderstorm.
- Do not walk during a lightning strike.
- Do not touch fallen power lines.
- Do not call 112 for ordinary storm damage.
