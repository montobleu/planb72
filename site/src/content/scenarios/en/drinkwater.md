## Recognise

**How you hear about it.** Your water company announces a problem with a notice through your letterbox and on its website. In an emergency the authorities also inform you via radio, TV and police loudspeaker vans. If the network works, also via BE-Alert, websites and social media.

**Types of message.** The Walloon water company SWDE uses three colours for the notice through your letterbox:

- **Orange:** the water is not drinkable, but you may use it if you boil it first.
- **Red:** the water is temporarily not drinkable, not even after boiling. You may not use it for drinking.
- **Green:** the incident is over. The water is drinkable again.

Other water companies may use different words. Read the message from your water company carefully and follow what it says.

**Why a boil-water advice.** A water company issues a boil-water advice when it finds intestinal bacteria such as E. coli or enterococci in the water. This points to contamination with human or animal faeces. It is a precautionary measure. Healthy people usually do not become ill from these bacteria, but other bacteria, viruses and parasites from faeces can cause gastrointestinal illness.

**Chemical contamination.** If the water is contaminated with fuel, toxic substances or radioactive substances, boiling or disinfecting does not make it safe. In that case use only other water, such as bottled water.

**No water.** The supply can also be cut off or the pressure can drop.

**How long.** A boil-water advice stays in force until tests show that the water is no longer contaminated. No fixed duration can be given in advance.

## Prepare

### How much water

- Allow 3 litres per person per day, for drinking and cooking. An adult drinks 1.5 to 2 litres. More if you can, for washing and the toilet.
- Keep a stock for at least 3 days, preferably longer.
- Allow more for pregnant women, sick people, children, women who are breastfeeding, pets and in hot weather. In great heat the need can double.
- Also set aside water that is not for drinking, for washing and cleaning.

### Which water and how to store it

**Best option: bottled water from the shop.** This is the safest to store. Keep it sealed in its original packaging. Store it in a cool, dark place. Check the best-before date: it is often only one year.

**Good option: bottles or jerrycans you fill yourself.**

- Use clean, empty bottles or a jerrycan, preferably food-grade.
- Choose a sturdy, unbreakable container (not glass) with a tight-fitting cap and a narrow neck.
- Never use a container that has held toxic substances, such as bleach or pesticides.
- Wash the container with washing-up liquid and rinse well. Then disinfect it: 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach (5 to 9 per cent sodium hypochlorite) in about 1 litre of water, shake well so that all inside surfaces get wet, wait at least 30 seconds, pour it out and let it dry.
- Fill with clean water and close tightly.
- Write "drinking water" and the date on the container. Replace water you filled yourself every 6 months.
- Store between about 10 and 21 °C, out of the sun and away from petrol and pesticides.
- Do not touch the water or the inside of the container with your hands. Always use a clean scoop.

### Information without a network

- Keep a battery-powered or wind-up radio ready, preferably with DAB+ and FM.
- Put the customer service number of your water company on your paper list of important phone numbers.

### Save water, always

- Take a shower instead of a bath.
- Collect rainwater and use it for the garden.
- Use leftover water for the plants.
- Have leaks repaired quickly.
- Use drinking water only where it is needed.

## During

### At home

**With a boil-water advice (orange).**

Use bottled water or boiled water for drinking, washing up, brushing teeth, washing and preparing food, washing hands, making ice cubes and making up formula. If your water company gives different advice, follow that.

How to boil water:

1. If the water is cloudy, filter it through a clean cloth, kitchen paper or a coffee filter. Or let it settle and skim off the clear water.
2. Bring the water to the boil and let it boil vigorously for at least 1 minute. If your water company gives a duration, follow that.
3. Let it cool.
4. Store it in clean, tightly closed containers or bottles.
5. If it tastes flat, pour it back and forth a few times from one container to another, or let it stand for a few hours.

**Water not drinkable, not even boiled (red).**

- Use only bottled water or water from your stock.
- The water company sets up distribution points. If the internet works, the SWDE says in the message on its website where they are. If the phone works, the customer service also knows.

**Chemical or radioactive contamination.** Boiling and disinfecting do not help. Use bottled water or another source. Follow the advice of the health services.

**No water from the tap.**

- Use your stock. First drink the water you know for certain is safe.
- The authorities may have bottled water distributed. Listen to the radio and watch for notices in your letterbox and loudspeaker vans.
- Do not ration drinking water unless the authorities ask you to. Drink what you need today and look for water for tomorrow.
- Limit your exertion and stay cool, so that you need less water.
- Do not drink alcohol or caffeine instead of water. They make you lose more water.
- Keep drinking water separate from water for washing and cleaning.

### Emergency solutions if you have no safe water

Use these only if you have no bottled water or stock left. If you cannot treat water, wait as long as possible before drinking doubtful water, but do not let yourself become dehydrated.

- **Boiling** is the safest way to kill germs.
- **Bleach.** Use ordinary, unscented bleach. First check the label: if there are instructions for disinfecting water, follow them. For bleach with 5 to 9 per cent sodium hypochlorite: 2 drops (0.1 ml) per litre of water. For bleach with 1 per cent: 10 drops (0.5 ml) per litre. If the water is cloudy, coloured or very cold, use double the amount. Stir well and wait at least 30 minutes before drinking. Belgian bleach may have a different strength: always read the label.
- **Tablets or drops.** Follow the instructions on the packaging. Chlorine dioxide kills the parasite Cryptosporidium if you use it correctly. Iodine and chlorine tablets do not kill this parasite. Anyone who is pregnant or has thyroid problems should preferably not drink water disinfected with iodine. No one should drink such water for longer than a few weeks.
- **Filters.** Portable filters do not remove viruses. For bacteria the pore size must be 0.3 micron or smaller, for parasites 1 micron or smaller. After filtering, also disinfect the water with a disinfectant.
- **Sunlight.** Only works with clear water. Fill clean, transparent plastic bottles and lay them on their side in the sun: 6 hours in sunshine, 2 days when cloudy. A dark surface underneath helps.
- **Distilling** also removes heavy metals, salts and most chemicals. Fill a pot half full of water, hang a cup from the handle of the lid so that it does not touch the water, and boil for 20 minutes. The water that drips into the cup is distilled.

### Outdoors or on the move

- Only drink water from streams or ponds after treating it. Filter it first and then boil it, or use one of the emergency solutions above.

### Children

- Never make up formula with water that is not safe. Use bottled water or boiled and cooled water.
- For young children a gastrointestinal infection can be serious.
- Children sometimes need more water.
- Include baby food and nappies in your stock.

### Vulnerable people

- Older people, pregnant women and people with a weakened immune system can become seriously ill from contaminated water. Give them bottled water or boiled water.
- Sick people, pregnant women and women who are breastfeeding need more water.

### Pets and animals

- Include extra water for pets in your stock, with food, water and bowls for 72 hours.

## Afterwards

- Wait for the message from your water company that the water is drinkable again. With the SWDE this is a green notice through your letterbox.
- If the water is brown or cloudy after the disruption, let the tap run for a few minutes.
- If the water pressure is low, it will recover gradually.
- Keep saving water. Do not waste it.
- Replenish your stock.

## Do not

- Do not drink tap water with a red message, not even boiled.
- Do not assume that boiling helps against chemical contamination.
- Never use water from the heating system or the water heater for drinking, cooking or washing yourself.
- Do not use a container that has held chemicals.
- Do not touch stored water with your hands.
- Do not ration drinking water unless the authorities ask you to.
- Do not use soft drinks with caffeine or alcohol as a substitute for water.
