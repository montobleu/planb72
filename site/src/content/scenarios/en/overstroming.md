## Recognise

**Types of flooding**

- **River flooding**: a river or stream bursts its banks after prolonged or heavy rain. The water usually rises gradually.
- **Surface water**: the sewers are overloaded or the ground can no longer absorb the rain. This is usually a slow process that leaves time to get to safety.
- **Flash flood**: very fast, dangerous, with a strong current and debris swept along.

**Warnings**

- The RMI issues colour codes for rain. Code yellow for example for 25 to 50 litres per m² in 24 hours, code orange for 51 to 100 litres, code red for more than 100 litres per m² in 24 hours or for major flooding with more heavy rain on the way. Code orange can come up to 24 hours in advance, code red at the earliest 12 hours in advance.
- For storm surges the RMI issues a code based on the water level in Ostend and Antwerp.
- In case of danger the authorities warn you via radio, TV and police loudspeaker vans, and if the network works also via BE-Alert, websites and social media.
- If the internet works, Waterinfo.be (Flanders) shows water levels, the rainfall radar and flood reports. In Wallonia hydrometrie.wallonie.be shows the water levels and flow rates of the rivers.

## Prepare

**Know your risk**

- Use the water test (watertoets) on waterinfo.be to check whether your home is in a flood-prone area.
- Know where to shut off the gas, electricity and water.
- Find the floor or the attic that stays dry the longest. Also check what escape route you have if the front door is blocked, for example a roof window or a ladder.
- Agree with whom you can stay if you have to leave your home. Make arrangements with neighbours about who helps whom and how you will reach each other without phone or internet. Agree with your family where you will find each other again.

**Protect your home against water from outside**

- Watertight barriers for windows and doors.
- Raise air vents or make them closable, and make the facade watertight.
- A flood defence around the building: an earth bank or a low wall.
- Have empty bags ready that you can fill with sand. The fire service or the municipality's technical department often hands out sandbags.
- Keep gutters and ditches clean and clear.

**Protect your home against water from the sewers**

- A non-return valve on every drain pipe, so that sewage does not flow back into the house or the rainwater tank.
- Drain rainwater and waste water separately as far as possible.
- Protect drains, for example with an inflatable plug.
- A pump or sump with a long discharge hose.

**Have ready at home**

- Boots, buckets and floor squeegees.
- Material to raise furniture and appliances, such as bricks or concrete blocks.
- Batteries and emergency lighting. No candles.
- A battery-powered or wind-up radio, preferably with DAB+ and FM, and a paper list of important phone numbers.
- Important papers and valuables stored high up.
- A heating oil tank that is secured and has its openings sealed.
- An emergency kit for 72 hours in a dry place. Cellars and garages flood more quickly. Add dry, warm blankets and towels: hypothermia is a risk.

**Health**

- Have your doctor check that your tetanus vaccination is up to date.
- Keep a supply of medicines for several days and a first aid kit high up and within reach.
- If your home dates from before 1998, have it checked for asbestos. A flood can damage such material.

## During

### At home

**First: is anyone's life in danger?**

- Someone's life is in danger: call 112 or have someone call. If you cannot get through, ask for help nearby and follow the information from the authorities on the radio. See Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.
- You need help from the fire service but no one's life is in danger, for example water in the cellar or in the street: if the network works, submit a request via 1722.be or your fire service zone's form, or call 1722 if that number is active. Keep 112 free.
- Damage but no help from the fire service needed: call in a professional or repair it yourself.

**Keeping water out**

- Use sandbags to build small dams in front of cellar openings and outside doors.
- Fit a watertight barrier in the doorway and seal it with silicone.

**Protecting your belongings**

- Take up carpets and put furniture at a safe height.
- Take valuables and important papers, such as contracts and invoices, to a higher floor.
- Secure loose objects in the garden so that they do not float away.

**Utilities**

- If necessary, shut off the main gas and water valves and the electricity.
- Switch off the power in rooms that may flood, before the water arrives. Anyone entering a flooded room where the power is still on risks an electric shock.
- If sockets, switches, the fuse box or appliances are already under water, do not touch them and do not enter that room. There may be a danger to life.
- In flooded areas the network operators often switch off the power themselves, for the duration of the flood and the clean-up.

**If the water rises in the house**

- Best option if the authorities ask you to: evacuate in good time.
- If you cannot or may not leave: go to the highest floor.
- Only go onto the roof if it is really necessary, and signal for help from there. This is what the American Ready.gov advises.
- Do not climb into a closed attic that you can no longer get out of.

**Evacuating**

- Follow the advice of the emergency services and wait for their instructions via the radio or police loudspeaker vans. Sometimes staying inside is safer.
- Help warn others that they need to evacuate.
- Take your identity card, money, water, mobile phone and charger, keys, a list of phone numbers, essential medicines, hygiene products, spare clothing and boots.
- Shut off the gas, water and electricity. Close windows and doors. The police keep watch over the homes.
- If you have no family or friends to go to, you will be given shelter in a reception centre run by the municipality or province, for example a sports hall or function room. A contact person on site provides the necessary information there.
- Let your family and a contact person outside the area know where you are. If the network works, send a text or message rather than phoning.
- Only return home when the authorities say it is safe.

### Outdoors or on the move

- Limit your journeys and avoid dangerous areas.
- Do not walk, swim or drive through the water. According to Ready.gov, 15 centimetres (6 inches) of flowing water can knock you over.
- Stay off bridges over fast-flowing water.
- If you do have to walk through a flooded street, use a stick to feel the ground. Manhole covers and sections of road surface may have been washed away.
- Floodwater can be contaminated and hide debris. Fallen power lines can electrify the water.

Source: [National Crisis Center: fluvial flooding](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/fluvial-riverine-flood) · [Ready.gov: floods](https://www.ready.gov/floods) · [Denk vooruit: flood](https://www.denkvooruit.nl/risicos/risicos-in-nederland/overstroming)

### In the car

- Move your car to a safe place in advance, and not into your garage.
- Never drive through a flooded road and never around a barrier. According to Ready.gov, 30 centimetres (1 foot) of water can carry a vehicle away.
- If you are trapped in the car, stay in it. If the water rises inside the car, climb onto the roof.

Source: [National Crisis Center: pluvial flooding](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/surface-water-flooding) · [Ready.gov: floods](https://www.ready.gov/floods)

### At work or at school

- Follow the instructions of the emergency services and of the people in charge on site.
- Agree in advance with colleagues what you will do in an emergency at work, and check whether your children's school is prepared.

Source: [National Crisis Center: make a plan](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan) · [National Crisis Center: fluvial flooding](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/fluvial-riverine-flood)

### Children

- Take baby food, nappies, the child's health record book, spare clothing and some toys with you.
- Keep children away from floodwater and from the clean-up. Children should not help with clearing up.
- Do not let children play in a flooded garden.

Source: [National Crisis Center: emergency kit](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [Vivalis](https://www.vivalis.brussels/sites/default/files/2026-07/Overstromingen%20en%20gezondheidsrisico%27s.pdf) · [Ready.gov: floods](https://www.ready.gov/floods)

### Vulnerable people

- Older people, children, pregnant women, people with a chronic illness or a disability and people who live alone are most at risk.
- If you depend on electricity for your health, plan in advance what you will do if the power is cut off.
- Agree who helps whom. Help neighbours with reduced mobility, for example with a lift to a safe area.
- If you are evacuated, report your medical condition immediately.
- If you lose your essential medicines, contact your doctor or pharmacist.
- If your home nursing cannot reach you, contact your municipality or the OCMW (public social welfare centre).

### Pets and animals

- Plan in advance how you will bring animals to a dry place.
- Take your pet with you if possible. Ask in advance whether family or friends can take it in, and keep a pet carrier ready. Small pets are sometimes welcome in a reception centre. Follow the instructions.
- If you cannot take your animal with you, tell the emergency services where it is and how it reacts to strangers. Leave water and food for at least 24 hours, newspaper for droppings and an item of clothing with your scent.

## Afterwards

**Returning safely**

- Only return when the authorities say it is safe.
- Do not touch wet electrical equipment and do not stand in water. Switch off the electricity if it is safe to do so.
- Be careful with the electrical installation.
- Only run a generator or petrol-powered appliance outdoors, away from windows.

**Cleaning and disinfecting**

- Keep children, vulnerable people and people with asthma away.
- Wear gloves, boots, safety glasses, disposable clothing and preferably an FFP2 or FFP3 mask.
- Clean with water and detergent, rinse with clean water and then disinfect with 15 ml of bleach per litre of water. Leave it to act for 10 to 15 minutes and rinse well. Undiluted bleach is corrosive to skin and mucous membranes.
- Work from clean to dirty. Wash your hands regularly.
- Throw away material that cannot be repaired: rugs, fitted carpet, cardboard and textiles.
- Preferably wash clothing that came into contact with floodwater at 60 °C.
- Leave the work to a professional if there is heating oil contamination or if asbestos may be involved.

**Drying and mould**

- Ventilate and heat the rooms at the same time, so that the moisture can escape.
- Dry walls and floors quickly. Leave at least 10 cm of space between furniture and the wall, even if the wall seems dry.
- Watch for mould stains and a mouldy smell. Call in a professional if the mould covers more than 3 m² or comes back after cleaning.

**Drinking water and food**

- Follow the advice of the authorities about tap water. Drink bottled water until the quality of the tap water has been restored.
- Throw away food that came into contact with floodwater or was not stored properly. This also applies to packaging that is not watertight, such as cardboard, and to jars with screw or clip lids.
- Clean kitchen surfaces and utensils with 15 ml of bleach per litre of water and preferably wash the dishes in a hot dishwasher of at least 70 °C.
- Do not eat anything from a flooded garden. Have water from a well or rainwater tank and the soil analysed before you use them again.

**Health**

- In the days and weeks afterwards, watch for fever, severe tiredness, coughing or breathing problems, wounds or skin rashes, nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea, and sleep problems or anxiety. If in doubt, contact your GP. Outside office hours, call 1733.
- Stress and tiredness after such an event are normal. If they persist, talk about it with a healthcare professional.

**Damage and insurance**

- Take photos of the damage for your insurance claim.
- Contact your insurer as soon as possible. According to the VMM, flood damage is covered by fire insurance, even if the flood is not recognised as a disaster.
- In Flanders the Flemish Disaster Fund (Vlaams Rampenfonds) can in exceptional cases compensate part of the damage, only after a disaster recognised by the Flemish Government. Report the damage to your insurer first. Report the damage to the Disaster Fund within 60 days of the date of the damage.

## Do not

- Do not shelter in cellars, underground car parks, tunnels or underpasses.
- Do not walk, swim or drive through flowing water, and do not drive around a barrier.
- Do not touch electrical equipment that is or was under water, and do not enter a flooded room where the power may still be on.
- Do not use candles as emergency lighting.
- Do not drink tap water or eat food that came into contact with the water until it has been declared safe.
- Do not leave your car in the garage.
