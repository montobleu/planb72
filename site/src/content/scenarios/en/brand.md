## Recognise

- In Belgium about 20,000 homes catch fire every year. Every year dozens of people die in a house fire. About half of those who die are older than 65.
- Most deaths are caused by toxic smoke, not by the flames.
- Modern furnishings give off toxic smoke very quickly. Sometimes you have only 3 minutes to get out. According to Ready.gov, a small flame can become a large fire in less than 30 seconds and a home is completely on fire after about five minutes.
- When you are asleep, you cannot smell anything. Without a smoke alarm the chance of waking up in time at night is very small.
- The three main causes of house fires are smoking, cooking and electrical appliances.
- Warm smoke rises. Low to the ground you can see the most and the air stays fresh the longest.

## Prepare

**Smoke alarms**

- Smoke alarms are compulsory in every home in Flanders, Wallonia and Brussels. Check which rules apply in your region.
- Fit a smoke alarm in every room where a fire can start, as close to the centre of the ceiling as possible. Not in the kitchen, bathroom or garage, because of false alarms. A heat alarm can be used in the kitchen.
- Choose an alarm with a CE mark complying with standard EN 14604, preferably with a 10-year battery that cannot be replaced.
- Interlinked alarms all go off when one of them goes off. Otherwise you hear an alarm less well behind a closed door.
- For deaf and hard-of-hearing people there are alarms with light signals or a vibrating pad.
- Dust the alarm every month and press the test button regularly. Replace the whole device after 10 years. Never paint over it.

**Escape plan**

- Decide on your quickest and safest escape route, usually the front door, and a second route, such as a back door or window, in case the first is blocked.
- Keep escape routes free of shopping bags, toys and boxes. Do not charge an electric bike or scooter in the hallway or stairwell.
- Hang the key by the door.
- Keep a phone and a torch by your bed, and keep your glasses or hearing aid within reach.
- Agree on an assembly point at the front of the building or in a place the fire service can see.
- Share out the tasks: who fetches the children, who helps anyone with reduced mobility, who calls 112. Include your pets in the plan as well.
- Practise the plan with everyone in the house, also with the internal doors closed. Ready.gov recommends practising twice a year, also in the dark.
- You can call on a fire prevention adviser free of charge.

**Doors closed**

- Close the internal doors at night. An ordinary internal door keeps smoke out for up to 10 minutes longer. Behind a closed door it can be up to 500 °C cooler.

**Fire-fighting equipment**

- Hang a fire blanket within reach in the kitchen. Practise beforehand how to use it.
- Keep a fire extinguisher within reach in the kitchen. Read the instructions before you need it and check regularly that it still works and has not expired.

**Preventing fire**

- Stay with your pots and pans when you cook. Keep tea towels, cookery books and loose clothing away from the hob. Use a deep fryer instead of a pan of oil.
- Only charge devices when you are at home, during the day, on a solid, non-combustible surface and in a room with a smoke alarm. Use the original charger.
- Do not plug too many appliances into one socket and never connect extension leads to each other.
- Only use the tumble dryer and washing machine during the day and when you are at home. Clean the tumble dryer filter after every use.
- Do not smoke in bed, when you are drowsy or when using medical oxygen.
- Do not leave candles burning unattended. LED candles are a safe alternative.
- Have the chimney swept every year and the heating serviced regularly.
- A fuse box with circuit breakers and a residual current device protects against electrocution and short circuits.

## During

### At home

**You hear a smoke alarm or see smoke**

1. Go outside immediately by the agreed escape route. Do not take anything with you.
2. Warn the others in the house and in the building on the way.
3. Close all doors behind you.
4. If there is smoke, stay low. Crawl under the smoke to the exit.
5. Feel the door handle and the door before you open a door. If it is hot or smoke is coming round the door, keep it closed and use your second escape route. If you open a door, do it slowly and be ready to close it again immediately.
6. Go to the assembly point at the front of the building.
7. Call 112 from outside. Say who you are, what is going on, where it is and whether anyone is injured. If you are not calling from the scene of the fire, tell the operator.
8. Never go back inside, not even for something you have forgotten.

**If you cannot get through**

- Have neighbours or bystanders call, or try another phone. Move a few metres and try again.
- If you can still make calls but 112 cannot be reached, try 101.
- If calling does not work at all: ask for help nearby. See Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.
- In the Netherlands, during the national 112 outage of 2019, the government sent people to the nearest fire station or police station. The map in the app (Nearest button) shows these.

**Your escape route is blocked**

1. Go to a room without smoke and close the door. Where possible, close other doors between you and the fire.
2. Seal gaps and air vents with a cloth or tape.
3. Open a window and make yourself visible, for example with a light-coloured cloth or a torch, so that the fire service or the neighbours know you are there.
4. Call 112 and say where you are.

**Putting it out yourself: only if it is safe**

- Your own safety comes first. Only put out a small fire that has just started, and only if you know how to use the fire extinguisher or the fire blanket. If that does not work straight away, get out and call 112.
- **Burning pan or deep fryer**: never put it out with water.
  - Best option: put a lid or a baking tray on the pan and turn off the heat.
  - Good option: fire blanket. Take the blanket, wrap your hands in it, lay it calmly over the pan without sudden movements, make sure no more air can get in and switch off the appliance or pull out the plug.
  - If that does not work: go outside, close the door and call 112.

**Clothing on fire**

- Stop, drop to the ground and roll. If that is not possible, smother the flames with a blanket or towel.

**Burns**

- Cool the burn for about 20 minutes under lukewarm running water of about 20 °C. Cool only the wound, so that the casualty does not become hypothermic.
- Do not put anything on the wound.
- See a doctor if the wound is larger than a 2 euro coin. Call the emergency services for large or severe blisters, or if the skin is brown or black. If you cannot get through, see above. See also the first aid card Burns.

### Outdoors or on the move

- If you see smoke or fire in another building, call 112. Say where it is and what you see. If you cannot get through, have someone else call or ask for help nearby (see At home, If you cannot get through).
- If you cannot reach someone inside, do not go in. Tell the fire service where that person is: via 112, or as soon as they arrive.

### At work or at school

- Follow the evacuation plan and the instructions of the people in charge. Leave the building immediately by a safe route in case of fire, smoke or an alarm.
- Know the quickest way out.

Source: [National Crisis Center: evacuating](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/evacuate)

### Children

- Teach children that fire is a tool and not a toy.
- Keep matches and lighters out of reach and out of sight, preferably in a locked cupboard.
- Never leave children alone near a hob or burning candles.
- Teach children not to hide from the fire service.
- Agree who will take the children outside, and practise the escape plan together.

Source: [Ready.gov: home fires](https://www.ready.gov/home-fires) · [Speel niet met vuur: escape plan](https://speelnietmetvuur.be/vluchtplan/)

### Vulnerable people

- Older people are most at risk: about half of those who die in house fires are older than 65.
- At night, keep a phone with the emergency numbers in it, a torch, glasses and hearing aid by the bed.
- If you use a rollator or wheelchair, check that all exits are wide enough.
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing people: choose alarms with a light signal or vibrating pad. For blind and partially sighted people there are alarms with a sound signal.
- If you cannot get out on your own: stay in a room without smoke, close the door, open a window and make sure the neighbours or the emergency services know you are there. Keep a phone at hand.
- Do not smoke while using medical oxygen.
- Use an electric blanket as the instructions say, not folded, and switch it off before you go to sleep.

### Pets and animals

- Include your pets in the escape plan.
- If there are still animals inside, tell the fire service immediately. Do not go back in yourself.

Source: [Speel niet met vuur: escape plan](https://speelnietmetvuur.be/vluchtplan/) · [Ready.gov: home fires](https://www.ready.gov/home-fires)

## Afterwards

- Only go back inside when the fire service says the home is safe.
- Do not reconnect the gas, water and electricity yourself.
- Make a list of the damaged belongings before you throw anything away, and keep the receipts for costs caused by the fire.
- If you need temporary shelter, food or medicines, ask an aid organisation such as the Red Cross for help.

Source: [Ready.gov: home fires](https://www.ready.gov/home-fires)

## Do not

- Never throw water on a burning pan or deep fryer.
- Do not go back inside.
- Do not open a door that feels hot or has smoke coming under it.
- Do not try to put out the fire yourself if you do not know how.
- Do not hide from the fire service.
- Do not connect extension leads to each other and do not leave devices charging at night or unattended.
- Do not paint over a smoke alarm or cover its openings.
