## Recognise

**How you are warned.** The authorities warn you via BE-Alert (text message, email or phone call), radio and TV, social media, the official websites and an information number. They can also deploy vehicles with loudspeakers. People who have no landline or mobile phone receive the information through those other channels. In a major emergency the authorities send an Alert-SMS to everyone who is in the affected zone with a mobile phone. You do not need to be registered for this.

**What can happen.** In a nuclear emergency radioactive substances can be released, including radioactive iodine. An emergency can begin with a threat phase, before anything is released. Iodine is also not always released.

**Which measures the authorities can take.** Depending on the situation, the authorities ask for one or more of these measures:

- sheltering: the best way to protect yourself;
- decontaminating yourself if you were outside;
- taking stable iodine;
- protecting the food chain;
- exceptionally: evacuating a particular zone.

## Prepare

- **Know your zone.** Check on nucleairrisico.be whether you live, work or go to school in a nuclear emergency planning zone.
- **Register with BE-Alert.** Also add the address of your work, your children's school or a vulnerable family member.
- **Follow the official channels** of your municipality, your governor and the National Crisis Center on social media.
- **Make an emergency plan** for your family, for example with the template of the National Crisis Center (crisiscentrum.be, "Make a plan").
- **Make an emergency kit** for at least 3 days: drinking water (3 to 10 litres per person per day, of which 1.5 to 2 litres for drinking), long-life food that needs no water, gas or electricity, a tin opener, a battery-powered or wind-up radio, a torch, your essential medication and a charged power bank. Keep it in a dry place.
- **Choose your shelter room:** a central room on the ground floor, away from windows.

**Iodine tablets at home.**

- If you live within about 20 km of a nuclear installation (10 km around IRE in Fleurus), keep iodine tablets at home.
- Throughout Belgium a box is recommended for families with children and young people up to 18, and for pregnant women and women who are breastfeeding.
- Adults aged 18 to 40 are less sensitive. They are advised to take iodine only if the authorities expressly say so.
- For people older than 40 the tablets are usually less suitable: the risk of thyroid cancer decreases with age, and the risk of side effects increases. People older than 40 who live close to an installation should still collect tablets from the pharmacist. Discuss their use with your GP.
- You collect the tablets free of charge from the pharmacy. Residents of the zones can collect them with their eID.
- Keep the package leaflet in the box. If you are unsure about allergies or an illness, ask your doctor or pharmacist.

## During

**What sheltering means exactly.** You go into the nearest building and stay there until you are told that it is safe. Walls and ceilings stop radiation and radioactive particles better than windows. A building protects better than a car.

### At home

1. Stay inside. Do not go outside.
2. Close all windows and doors.
3. Also close other openings: the chimney air inlet, an open fireplace and the cooker hood.
4. Switch off ventilation, heating and air conditioning.
5. Seal the bottom of doors with damp cloths. Make sure some air still circulates, so that you do not feel unwell.
6. Go to a central room on the ground floor, away from windows.
7. Bring pets inside.
8. Follow the instructions of the authorities via radio, TV, websites and social media.
9. Only phone in a medical emergency (112). Unnecessary calls overload the network and hinder the emergency services. Warn others by text message, WhatsApp or Signal.

**Do you have a cellar?** The Belgian sources speak of a central room on the ground floor. The American Ready.gov names a cellar or the middle of a large building as the best protected place, with as many walls as possible between you and the outside. Brick or concrete buildings and underground car parks also offer good protection.

**How long.** In principle sheltering lasts at most 24 hours. If the release lasts longer, radioactive particles can gradually get in through gaps and vents. The authorities may then order the evacuation of certain zones. Ready.gov advises staying in the best protected place for the first 24 hours and only leaving earlier in case of immediate danger, such as fire, a gas leak or a serious injury.

**Eating and drinking while sheltering.** Food and drink that were indoors are safe. You can use closed packaging from outside if you first wipe it with a damp cloth. Put that cloth in a plastic bag, away from people and animals. Do not eat anything from the vegetable garden or anything that was left uncovered outside.

### Outdoors or on the move

- Go into the nearest building as quickly as possible and close windows and doors.
- If you were outside when the cloud passed, go in through a room you do not use. Decontaminate yourself there before you join the others:
  1. Take off your clothes. Do this calmly, so that dust does not come loose. Do not touch your eyes, nose and mouth.
  2. Put the clothes in a plastic bag and put the bag outside.
  3. Take a shower and wash with soap.
  4. If you cannot shower, wipe your skin and hair with a damp wipe or a wet cloth. Do not use household cleaning wipes on your skin. Hand gel does not protect against radioactive substances.
  5. Put on clean clothes and drink enough.
- According to Ready.gov, just taking off the outer layer of clothing can remove up to 90 per cent of the radioactive dust.
- The authorities may set up a reception centre for decontamination and a medical check.

### In the car

- If you are close to home, work or a public building, go inside immediately.
- If that is not possible, park in a safe place or drive away from the installation, at right angles to the wind direction.
- Switch off the engine when you are stationary, close the windows and switch off the ventilation and air conditioning.
- Drive calmly and follow the traffic rules.
- Listen to the radio.
- Stay where you are until the authorities say you may leave. Follow the police, diversions and road closures.

### At work or at school

- The advice to shelter also applies at work. Stay inside.
- Leave children at school or childcare. They follow the same recommendations there. Schools have an internal emergency plan and keep the children under supervision.
- Do not go to collect them. You would cause traffic jams, hinder the emergency services and expose yourself and your children to the radioactive cloud.
- The school will let you know through its own channels how and when you can collect your child.
- If there is an evacuation, the local authorities organise the transport of the children to assembly points, unless otherwise announced.

### Children

- Children and young people up to 18 are the most sensitive to radioactive iodine. A box of iodine tablets is recommended for them.
- Sheltering is the best protection at any age, even if you have no tablets.

### Vulnerable people

- Pregnant women and women who are breastfeeding belong to the group for whom iodine tablets are recommended. Only take them if the authorities say so.
- People older than 40 do not in principle take iodine, unless the authorities expressly recommend it. Ask your doctor for advice in advance.
- If you are ill or injured, follow the instructions about where you can get medical care. Call 112 in a medical emergency.

### Pets and animals

- Bring pets and assistance dogs inside.
- An animal that was outside after the cloud passed should be gently brushed and washed with soap and water, if possible.
- The authorities may impose a grazing ban. Dairy cows and other grazing animals must then be kept indoors, so that their milk and meat do not become contaminated.
- In an evacuation, people come before animals. With your own transport you may take your pet with you if possible. Pets are usually not allowed on public transport.
- Livestock are only evacuated if there is too little housing space, the zone is small enough and it can be done without risk of contamination.

### Iodine tablets

- Never take iodine tablets on your own initiative. Only take them if the authorities expressly say so.
- The authorities decide when. The advice may apply to certain zones or groups.
- Stable iodine only helps if radioactive iodine is released. It saturates the thyroid gland, so that it no longer absorbs radioactive iodine.
- Iodine does not protect against other radioactive substances. Sheltering remains necessary.
- Sheltering comes before iodine, especially if you have no tablets at home.
- The amount depends on your age. Follow the package leaflet.
- One dose is often enough. The protection lasts at least 24 hours.

### Evacuation

- Evacuation is the most exceptional measure. Never evacuate on your own initiative.
- Evacuation does not take place while the cloud is passing. It can take place as a precaution, before the release reaches the zone, or later, after sheltering.
- If you have to leave: stay calm, shut off the water, gas and electricity and take the minimum with you: identity card, money, a few bottles of water, mobile phone and charger.
- Follow the designated routes and assembly points. Who goes, where to and how will be announced at the time.
- The police cordon off a safety zone. You may no longer enter an evacuated zone.

## Afterwards

- Only go outside when the authorities announce that it is safe.
- If you were evacuated, only return when the local authorities say so.
- Medical advice may follow. Follow the media and the official channels.
- The authorities may take measures for the food chain:
  - restricting or banning the use of fresh crops, including from your vegetable garden;
  - banning the use of drinking water and surface water;
  - advising against watering crops with rainwater;
  - banning milk from non-commercial sources, for example from your own animals;
  - imposing a grazing ban.
- Do not eat anything from your garden until the authorities say it is safe.
- Also look after your mental health. Talk to someone if you are upset.

## Do not

- Never take iodine tablets on your own initiative.
- Never evacuate on your own initiative.
- Do not collect your children from school or childcare.
- Do not phone unless necessary.
- Do not go outside to look or to do your shopping.
- Do not leave ventilation or air conditioning on.
- Do not eat anything from the vegetable garden or anything that was left uncovered outside.
- Do not water your garden with rainwater as long as the authorities advise against it.
