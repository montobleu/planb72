## Recognise

**How you are warned.** In a chemical accident the authorities warn you via BE-Alert (text message, email or phone call), radio and TV, social media and official websites. Sometimes vehicles with loudspeakers drive around. In a major emergency the authorities also send an Alert-SMS to everyone who is in the risk zone with a mobile phone at that moment. You do not need to be registered for this.

**Signs that you have come into contact with a substance.** Watch for:

- unexplained fumes, a strange smell or a strange taste;
- irritation of the eyes, skin or airways;
- nausea, vomiting, shaking, sweating, confusion, breathing problems or fever;
- other people nearby with the same symptoms.

Symptoms can also appear only after days or weeks. Some toxic substances have no smell, so no smell does not mean there is no danger. Large numbers of dead insects or birds can also be a sign.

A layperson usually cannot judge how dangerous a substance is. So always follow the instructions of the authorities.

## Prepare

- **Know your neighbourhood.** Check on seveso.be whether you live, work or go to school near a Seveso company. Seveso companies work with hazardous substances.
- **Register with BE-Alert.** As well as your home address, add other addresses: your work, your children's school or childcare, the address of a vulnerable family member.
- **Choose your shelter room.** The Belgian authorities recommend a central room on the ground floor, away from windows and outside doors. Walls and ceilings protect better than windows. Choose a room with as few windows and doors to the outside as possible.
- **Know how to switch off the ventilation.** Find out in advance how to stop mechanical ventilation, air conditioning and the air intake of the heating.
- **Have material ready to seal gaps.** The American Ready.gov recommends having plastic sheeting, strong tape and scissors ready to cover doors, windows and vents. Wet towels can also be used.
- **Make an emergency kit** with water, food, a battery-powered or wind-up radio, a torch, medication and a charged power bank.
- **Make arrangements** with the people you live with: who does what, and how you reach each other without phoning.

**Difference between sources about the floor.** The Belgian authorities advise a central room on the ground floor. The Bezirksamt Pankow in Berlin and Ready.gov recommend a room on a higher floor or above ground in the case of gases, because most gases are heavier than air and sink. In Belgium, follow the Belgian advice. If the authorities give a different instruction for a particular substance via BE-Alert, follow that instruction.

## During

Sheltering is the best protection. Sheltering is temporary: from a few hours up to at most 24 hours. If the situation remains unsafe after a few hours, the authorities may still order an evacuation.

### At home

1. Stay inside. If you are outside, go into the nearest building.
2. Close all windows and doors. Also close the ventilation grilles in the window frames.
3. Switch off ventilation, air conditioning and any other system that brings in outside air.
4. Go to a central room on the ground floor, away from windows and outside doors.
5. Seal gaps under doors and around windows with wet towels, plastic sheeting or tape if you have that material.
6. Warn the people you live with and the other residents of the building.
7. Let passers-by who are in danger come in temporarily.
8. Turn on the radio or TV and follow the instructions of the authorities.
9. Do not light candles. They use up oxygen.
10. Do not eat or drink anything you suspect is contaminated.

**Can you still smell the substance indoors?** Then breathe shallowly through a cloth or handkerchief. Use a face mask if you have one. If gases or fumes are released inside the building itself, for example through a leak inside, leave the building.

**Phoning.** Only call 112 in an emergency. Warn others by text message, WhatsApp, Signal or social media, so that the phone network stays free for the emergency services.

### Outdoors or on the move

1. Walk away from the accident, preferably at right angles to the wind direction.
2. Breathe through a cloth, or at least through a handkerchief.
3. Go into the nearest closed building. Ask to be let in and follow the same steps there as at home.

Ready.gov recommends moving upwind of the source. The Belgian and German authorities recommend walking away at right angles to the wind. Follow the Belgian advice.

**Were you in contact with a suspicious substance?**

- When you go inside, take off your outer clothing and shoes. Put them in a rubbish bag, tie it shut and put it outside the living area.
- Clothing that you would have to pull off over your head is better cut off, so that the substance does not pass over your face.
- Take out your glasses or contact lenses.
- First wash your hands, then your face, hair, nose and ears thoroughly with soap and water.
- Rinse your eyes with water.
- Take a shower and put on clean clothes.
- Then see a doctor if it is safe to do so. For questions about poisoning, call the Poison Centre on 070 245 245.

### In the car

- Close the windows and switch off the ventilation, air conditioning and heating.
- Listen to the radio and follow the instructions of the authorities and emergency services.
- Go to the nearest closed building, unless the authorities say otherwise. Ask to be let in.
- If there is no building nearby, stay in the vehicle with the ventilation off.
- If you have to stop, stay where you are until the authorities announce that it is safe. Follow diversions and the instructions of the police.

### At work or at school

- The advice to shelter also applies at work. Do not go home.
- Do not collect your children from school or childcare. The staff keep them inside according to the school's emergency plan. Anyone who goes anyway causes traffic jams, hinders the emergency services and puts themselves and the children in danger.
- The school or childcare will let you know through its own channels when and how you can collect your child.
- If you are near the school yourself, shelter where you are.

### Children

- Children at school follow the same recommendations as at home.
- At home: take children with you to the shelter room.
- Add the address of the school and childcare in BE-Alert, so that you receive alerts for that location.

### Vulnerable people

- See whether you can help someone who has reduced mobility or has few people around them, without going outside into the cloud yourself. Warn them with a message.
- In your emergency plan, arrange helpers near older people, people with a disability and people with specific needs.
- Register the address of a vulnerable family member with BE-Alert.

### Pets and animals

- Bring pets inside if you can do so without risk to yourself. Your own safety and that of others comes first.
- Take them with you to the shelter room.

### If the authorities ask you to evacuate

- Only leave when the authorities or the emergency services ask you to. Staying can sometimes be safer.
- Then leave immediately. If possible, do not go through the contaminated zone.
- Take the minimum with you: identity card, money, bottles of water, mobile phone and charger, keys, a list of phone numbers, your essential medication and a toilet bag.
- Switch off electrical appliances. The fridge and freezer can stay on. Close windows and doors.
- Go to family or friends outside the zone or to the reception centre set up by the municipality or province.

## Afterwards

- Wait for the official announcement that the danger is over before you open windows or go outside.
- If you were evacuated, only return when the authorities say it is safe.
- A chemical accident can contaminate drinking water and food: agriculture, livestock and vegetables from your garden. Follow the instructions of the authorities before you use them.
- If you were in contact with the substance and could not see a doctor straight away, have yourself checked as soon as it is safe.
- Symptoms can appear later. See a doctor if you still develop symptoms days or weeks later, and say that you were near the accident.

## Do not

- Do not go to look at the accident.
- Do not collect your children from school or childcare.
- Do not evacuate on your own initiative.
- Do not open windows and do not go outside before the all-clear.
- Do not leave ventilation or air conditioning on.
- Do not phone unless necessary.
- Do not eat or drink anything that was outside or may be contaminated.
