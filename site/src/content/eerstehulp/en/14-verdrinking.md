## Recognise

Drowning is when breathing is impaired after submersion or immersion in water or another liquid, completely or partially. Because of the lack of oxygen, the heart slows down and eventually stops. The casualty may still be in the water, may have got themselves to safety or may have been pulled out of the water.

What to look out for:

- breathing difficulties caused by water, mud or weed in the airways;
- vomiting;
- unconsciousness or no normal breathing;
- a head or spinal injury after a dive or fall into shallow water;
- hypothermia: the water is almost always colder than the body (see topic 10);
- after the rescue, pneumonia or brain injury may still develop, even if things seem fine at first.

In Europe, drowning is the fourth leading cause of death in children aged 5 to 14 years. People older than 50 years drown most often.

## What to do

### The rescue

1. Shout for help and have someone call 112.
2. Try to rescue without going into the water yourself. Use rescue equipment that is available on site, throw something that floats, or hold out a long object.
3. Do not go into deep water if you are not a strong swimmer. Only go into the water if you are trained in rescue, and then take rescue equipment with you. A drowning person who is conscious may grab hold of the rescuer in panic.
4. According to the ERC, a trained rescuer who reaches an unresponsive drowning person in the water immediately gives 5 rescue breaths before bringing the person to the side.
5. Lift the casualty out of the water horizontally. This avoids a drop in blood pressure.

### On the side

1. Check for a response.
2. Open the airway and check the breathing. If you suspect a spinal injury, do not tilt the head back.
3. Call 112 if this has not yet been done.
4. Unconscious and not breathing normally:
   - First give 5 rescue breaths. After drowning, the outcome is better if the casualty is given rescue breaths first.
   - Then start normal CPR: 30 chest compressions and 2 rescue breaths (see topic 2).
   - Have someone fetch an AED. Dry the chest before you attach the pads, and follow the instructions of the device.
   - Continue until the emergency services take over. Bear in mind a possible spinal injury.
5. Unconscious but breathing normally: place the casualty in the recovery position. If you suspect a spinal injury, leave them lying as long as they are breathing spontaneously. Check the breathing every minute.
6. Conscious and breathing normally: move the casualty horizontally, if necessary to a warmer environment. Remove the wet clothing completely, dry them and cover them warmly. Measure the body temperature. Do not rub the casualty to warm them.
7. If the casualty vomits, turn them onto their side so that they do not choke.

### Children and babies

Follow the same steps. Give a baby rescue breaths over the mouth and nose together, an older child mouth-to-mouth. Press with two thumbs on a baby, with one or two hands on a child (see topic 2).

## If help is a long time coming

- After drowning, there is a chance that the person responds to CPR, even without a defibrillator. The IFRC names drowning and hypothermia as exceptions to the rule that, in an area without emergency services, you can stop if CPR has had no effect after 20 minutes. With a child who has drowned or is hypothermic, there is a chance of saving them with CPR alone.
- Rescue breaths are especially important here, because the casualty has not been getting oxygen. Give them if you can.
- Take turns with other rescuers every 1 to 2 minutes without interrupting CPR.
- If the casualty has to be transported, continue CPR without interruption on a firm surface during transport.
- Protect the casualty from further cooling: remove wet clothing, dry them, cover them, insulate them from the ground, shelter them from the wind. If the casualty is hypothermic and in cardiac arrest, continue CPR without interruption until they have been rewarmed (see topic 10).
- A casualty who seems well again must also be examined. Meanwhile, monitor them continuously: breathing, level of consciousness and temperature. Pneumonia or brain injury may develop later.
- After a dive into shallow water: move the casualty as little as possible and keep the head in line with the back (see topic 9).

## Call 112 if

- someone is in difficulty in the water: have someone call immediately;
- someone has been pulled out of the water: always call 112;
- the casualty is unconscious or not breathing normally;
- you suspect a spinal injury.

Every drowning casualty must be examined, even if it does not seem so serious.

Source: [Gezondheid en Wetenschap: Drowning](https://www.gezondheidenwetenschap.be/eerste-hulp/eerste-hulp-bij-verdrinking) · [ERC Guidelines 2025 for Everyone](https://www.erc.edu/media/p5ymaeej/gl2025_layperson_book_ipdf-v11-e.pdf)

## Do not

- Do not go into the water yourself if you are not trained for it.
- Do not go into deep water if you are not a strong swimmer.
- Do not tilt the head back if you suspect a spinal injury.
- Do not rub the casualty to warm them.
- Do not lift the casualty out of the water upright, but horizontally.
- Do not let a casualty who seems well go home without being examined.
- Do not attach the AED pads to a wet chest.

Prevention: always keep an eye on children near water.
