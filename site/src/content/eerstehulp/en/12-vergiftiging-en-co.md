## Recognise

Poisoning can be caused by swallowing, inhaling, injecting or skin contact. Clues are an empty medicine packet, an opened bottle of cleaning product, spilled chemicals, a strange smell, or an enclosed space. The person may also tell you themselves.

Possible signs. They depend on the product and may appear immediately or only later:

- vomiting or retching, abdominal cramps;
- very large or very small pupils;
- burns around or in the mouth and throat; breath that smells of chemicals such as petrol or paint thinner;
- stinging eyes, nose or mouth;
- headache, dizziness, fainting, drowsiness or confusion;
- breathing difficulties, pink froth at the mouth;
- heart rhythm disturbances or cardiac arrest, signs of shock;
- pale, red or blue skin, clammy skin, heavy sweating, a burning sensation on the skin;
- a high body temperature, seizures, unconsciousness.

Poisoning can look like other conditions, such as a seizure, a stroke or a blood sugar problem.

### Carbon monoxide (CO)

CO is colourless, odourless and tasteless. You do not notice it. The symptoms are often mistaken for flu, food poisoning or simply feeling unwell: headache, nausea and dizziness. In serious cases, CO leads to unconsciousness or death. If you find someone unconscious in an enclosed space, think of CO straight away. About half of the cases happen in the bathroom, often because of poor ventilation. The risk increases in the cold months, when windows are more often kept closed.

## What to do

### Swallowed product or medicine

1. Make sure the scene is safe. Ventilate the room and close the packaging, because some products give off harmful fumes.
2. Put on gloves that are resistant to the product. Move any remains to a safe place if this can be done safely.
3. If the network is working, call the Poison Centre on 070 245 245. Follow the advice strictly. Do not wait until there are symptoms. If you cannot get through, follow the steps below and get help as in Call 112 if.
4. Say which product it is, how much and when, or for how long the person was exposed to it. Keep the packaging at hand. Only touch it if it is safe to do so. If it is not safe, wait for the emergency services.
5. If the person is conscious and there is still product in the mouth, have them rinse their mouth with water and spit it out.
6. Help the person into a comfortable position, on the left side if possible. Monitor the breathing and the level of consciousness.
7. If the person vomits while lying down, turn them onto their side so that they do not choke.
8. Unconscious but breathing normally: recovery position, preferably on the left side. Call 112 or have someone call. If you cannot get through, see Call 112 if.
9. Unconscious and not breathing normally: call 112 or have someone call, and perform CPR. If there is a harmful product in or around the mouth, give chest compressions only, without mouth-to-mouth rescue breaths.
10. Collect bottles, packaging and labels and give them to the emergency services.
11. Treat a swallowed button battery as poisoning.
12. Afterwards, take off your gloves and wash or disinfect your hands.

### Inhaled gas or fumes

1. Do not simply walk into an enclosed space. If you cannot reach the casualty safely, call 112 immediately or have someone call, and wait at a safe distance. If you cannot get through, have someone get help (see Call 112 if).
2. Open windows and doors fully, preferably from the outside.
3. Some gases are explosive. Do not switch electrical appliances on or off. Put out open flames and pilot lights if possible. Do not let anyone smoke.
4. Keep everyone at a safe distance and out of the direction in which the fumes are blowing.
5. Only bring the casualty outside if you do not put yourself in danger by doing so.
6. If the network is working, call the Poison Centre on 070 245 245, with the packaging at hand. Follow the advice of the Poison Centre and of 112 strictly.
7. If the casualty has difficulty breathing, help them into a comfortable position. If they are unconscious, place them in the recovery position.
8. CPR in poisoning by inhalation: give chest compressions only, no mouth-to-mouth rescue breaths.
9. If you do not know the product, do not treat it as an ordinary household product. Call 112 or have someone call.

### CO poisoning

1. Ensure your own safety first. Do not simply walk into the room. A wet handkerchief over your mouth does not protect you: you will still breathe in CO.
2. Call 112 immediately or have someone call. Say that you suspect CO poisoning and whether the casualty is conscious. If you cannot get through, see Call 112 if.
3. Ventilate the room by opening windows and doors from the outside.
4. Switch off the appliances that can produce CO.
5. Get the casualty out of the room.
6. If the casualty is not breathing, perform CPR as quickly as possible. With CO, mouth-to-mouth rescue breathing is safe.
7. If they are still breathing, place them in the recovery position, so that they cannot choke on vomit.

### Product on the skin or in the eyes

Rinse with clean running water. If the network is working, call the Poison Centre (see topic 7).

## If help is a long time coming

- Keep exposure as small as possible. Stay with the casualty in fresh air, away from the source, and keep the room ventilated.
- Place the casualty on their left side and monitor the breathing and level of consciousness continuously. Symptoms may only appear later.
- Write down what happened: which product, how much, by which route and at what time. Keep packaging and labels. That information helps the emergency responders give the right care.
- Do not induce vomiting. Do not give milk, water or activated charcoal, unless the Poison Centre explicitly says so.
- If the network is working, you can also call the doctor on duty: 1733.
- Prevent new cases: never leave an engine running in an enclosed space such as a garage. This applies to all combustion engines. Keep ventilation grilles clear and ventilate regularly. A CO alarm warns of raised concentrations, but does not prevent CO.

## Call 112 if

- the casualty is unconscious;
- the casualty has breathing difficulties or is not breathing;
- the casualty has a seizure;
- the condition deteriorates quickly and seriously;
- you find someone unconscious in an enclosed space;
- you suspect CO and there are symptoms;
- you cannot reach the casualty safely;
- you do not know the product.

If the network is working, call the Poison Centre (070 245 245) for any possible poisoning or exposure, even if the product, the quantity or the circumstances are not clear, for an overdose of drugs, pills or medicines, and when medicines have been taken incorrectly, including during breastfeeding.

**If you cannot get through**

- Only leave the casualty alone if there is no other option. Point directly at a bystander to get help and tell them where it is, what happened, how many casualties there are and what condition they are in. Also say which product it is, how the person was exposed to it and when.
- Stay with the casualty, in a safe place and in fresh air. Monitor the breathing and the level of consciousness.
- Collect bottles, packaging and labels, if this can be done safely, and give them to the emergency responders.
- If you are giving CPR and are alone without a phone, see topic 2 (If you cannot get through).
- See also topic 1 (If you cannot get through: getting help without a phone) and Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.

## Do not

- Do not make the casualty vomit. That can damage the throat.
- Do not give milk, water or activated charcoal, unless the Poison Centre says so.
- Do not try to dilute a swallowed corrosive product.
- Do not wait for symptoms before calling the Poison Centre.
- Do not enter an enclosed space to rescue an unconscious casualty if it puts you in danger.
- Do not rely on a wet handkerchief against CO.
- Do not switch electrical appliances on or off and do not smoke near gas.
- Do not give mouth-to-mouth rescue breaths in poisoning by inhaled fumes or with a harmful product around the mouth. With CO it is allowed.
