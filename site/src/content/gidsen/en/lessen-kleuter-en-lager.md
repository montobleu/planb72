This guide gives activities for each level that work with paper, pencil and whatever is in the house. They follow the domains of the curricula: language, mathematics, world studies and nature (in Flanders science and technology, geography and history), arts education and physical education, supplemented with practical skills for the situation. How to fit them into a day and a week is explained in the guide on home schooling without electricity.

The Flemish minimum goals (minimumdoelen) set out what a child must be able to do at the end of nursery school, the 4th and the 6th year. If you are not sure what suits a school year, look in your child's exercise books and textbooks, or in the goals you printed in advance.

Use materials that can be found anywhere: pebbles, sticks, dried beans, buttons, packaging, a calendar. Read aloud every day, at every level.

## Nursery children (2.5 to 6 years)

Nursery children learn by playing, talking and repeating. Work in short, playful moments, not at the table with tasks.

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Language | read aloud every day and talk about the pictures; recite rhymes and songs; name objects; guess which word begins with which sound; recognise and copy their own name; retell a story with cuddly toys |
| Arithmetic | count everything: stairs, plates, buttons; sort by colour, shape or size; compare more and less; lay out patterns (red, blue, red, blue); look for shapes in the house; recognise the dots on a die |
| World and nature | name the weather of the day; day and night, the seasons; collect and compare leaves, stones and shells; let seeds sprout on a wet cloth; floating and sinking in a bowl of water |
| Practical | washing hands before eating and after the toilet; getting dressed; laying the table; washing vegetables; knowing that in an emergency you fetch an adult straight away |
| Movement | walking, jumping, climbing, crawling, dancing; rolling and catching a ball; obstacle course |
| Music and art | singing, clapping to the rhythm, drumming on a pot; drawing, colouring, cutting, sticking, modelling clay |

## First stage of primary school (1st and 2nd year, 6 to 8 years)

Children learn to read, write and do arithmetic up to 100. Repetition matters: a quarter of an hour every day is better than an hour once a week.

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Reading | read aloud every day, including labels, calendars and packaging; write words and short sentences on cards and play with them; read aloud to a younger child or a cuddly toy |
| Writing | letters and words in a lined exercise book; one sentence a day in a diary; a shopping list; a card for someone |
| Telling | telling what you did; retelling a story in the right order; making up a story together |
| Arithmetic | counting and grouping in 10s with beans or pebbles; adding and subtracting up to 20, then up to 100; splitting numbers; the 2, 5 and 10 times tables as a song; arithmetic with dice and cards |
| Measuring and time | reading the clock to the hour and half hour; the days of the week and the months; measuring with a ruler, with feet or with hands; weighing with a home-made balance from a coat hanger and two small bags |
| World and nature | a weather calendar with drawings; drawing and naming plants and animals nearby; planting a seed and recording its growth; drawing their own house and street |
| Practical | washing hands properly; asking an adult for help; rinsing a small wound with water and covering it; cooling a simple burn; helping prepare a small meal, such as a sandwich or fruit |
| Movement and music | playing outside, skipping, hopscotch; songs with actions; home-made instruments |

## Second stage of primary school (3rd and 4th year, 8 to 10 years)

Children read independently, write short texts and do arithmetic up to 1,000 and beyond. They can already work on a task alone for longer.

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Reading | silent reading for half an hour every day; telling what they read; answering questions about a text; reading aloud to younger children |
| Writing | a diary or a family logbook; a letter to someone they miss; a report on a small experiment; dictation of sentences from a book |
| Language | looking up words in a dictionary; parsing sentences into subject and verb; practising spelling with words from the day |
| Arithmetic | all the multiplication tables; division with and without remainder; calculating with money, including coins; fractions with an apple or a pancake; written column arithmetic |
| Measuring and time | the clock to the minute; distances in metres and kilometres; volume in litres with measuring jugs; weight; making a daily plan with times |
| World and nature | measuring the weather with a home-made thermometer and rain gauge; the wind direction with a weather vane; the life cycle of a plant or an animal; the family history on a timeline |
| Map | drawing a plan of the house and the street; finding the four compass points using the sun; reading a map of the neighbourhood with an adult |
| Practical | practising alerting the emergency services correctly as a game: who, what, where; stopping bleeding by pressing; counting and recording supplies; helping prepare a simple meal under supervision |
| Movement and music | relay races, ball games, dancing; learning a song with several verses; copying rhythms |

**Your own weather station.** The RMI (Royal Meteorological Institute) describes how children can make one with what is in the house:

- a thermometer from a bottle, a straw and coloured water;
- a rain gauge from a bottle, a funnel and a scale in centimetres, which you read and empty every morning;
- a weather vane from a cardboard arrow, a straw and a stick;
- a wind gauge from a protractor with a table tennis ball on a string of 30 cm.

Record at the same time every day. After a few weeks you will see patterns.

Source: [RMI: build your own weather station with Meteoz](https://www.meteo.be/uploads/media/5bfc1774280f6/publication3-weerstation.pdf?v1=) · [Rode Kruis-Vlaanderen: first aid learning pathway](https://jeugd.rodekruis.be/storage/jrk/2025-leerlijn-eerste-hulp-rode-kruis-vlaanderen.pdf)

## Third stage of primary school (5th and 6th year, 10 to 12 years)

Children can plan a project lasting a few days, look up information in books and explain a subject to others. In Flanders, French is compulsory from the 5th year. In the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, modern languages are part of the Tronc commun.

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Reading | one book a week; writing a summary; looking up information in an atlas, encyclopaedia or printed articles |
| Writing | a project on a subject; a report; a poem; a small newspaper for the family or the reception centre |
| Speaking | giving the family a short explanation of the project; interviewing a grandparent about the old days |
| Second language | ten words every day; naming objects in the house in the other language; acting out a simple conversation |
| Mathematics | decimals, percentages and fractions with recipes and supplies; perimeter and area of the room; scale on a map; a bar chart of the weather measurements |
| Science | experiments with water, light and shadow; the sundial; recognising the types of cloud; estimating wind force |
| History and geography | a timeline of the family or the commune; Belgium and the neighbouring countries on a map; the provinces and the rivers |
| Map and compass | reading a topographic map: scale, legend, contour lines; finding north with a compass; planning a simple route and walking it with an adult |
| First aid | the four steps of first aid; checking whether someone is conscious and breathing; tilting the head and lifting the chin; helping with choking by back blows; an introduction to the recovery position and resuscitation |
| Practical | planning a meal and helping to cook; measuring out water for a day; a small budget with the supplies |
| Movement and music | sport with rules: football, dodgeball; making up a dance or a song; drama |

**Weather and clouds.** The RMI distinguishes ten cloud genera in three layers. Nimbostratus is the typical rain cloud, cumulonimbus the thundercloud. Let children draw the clouds every day and forecast the next day's weather, and check the following day whether they were right. On the RMI's Beaufort scale, wind force 6 is strong (39 to 49 km/h) and wind force 8 is gale (62 to 74 km/h).

**Map.** The NGI map at a scale of 1:25 000 means that 1 cm on the map is 250 m in reality. The legend in the map margin explains the symbols. Use the compass in the app or an ordinary compass. See the guide on navigation without GPS.

## First aid at a child's level

The Red Cross builds up first aid by age. Teach children what suits their age, and practise it as a game. What to do for each injury is in the app under First aid.

| Level | What a child can learn |
| --- | --- |
| Nursery children | washing hands; knowing that in an emergency you fetch an adult straight away |
| 6 to 8 years | fetching help from an adult; making sure you are safe yourself; rinsing an ordinary wound and covering it; cooling a small burn with water for 10 to 20 minutes; comforting someone; knowing the emergency number 112 |
| 8 to 10 years | alerting the emergency services correctly: who you are, what has happened, where; stopping bleeding by pressing firmly |
| 10 to 12 years | the steps of first aid; checking whether someone responds and is breathing; opening the airway; helping with choking; an introduction to the recovery position and resuscitation |
| 12 to 14 years | being able to do the recovery position and resuscitation themselves; abdominal thrusts for choking; caring for a wound with little water |

Also teach children that calling does not always work. Then fetch an adult, go to the neighbours or to a place where there are people.

Source: [Rode Kruis-Vlaanderen: first aid learning pathway](https://jeugd.rodekruis.be/storage/jrk/2025-leerlijn-eerste-hulp-rode-kruis-vlaanderen.pdf) · [Belgian Red Cross: Outils pédagogiques pour le primaire](https://enseignement.croix-rouge.be/outils/primaire/)

## Cooking with children

Cooking together is arithmetic, reading and practical skill all at once: reading a recipe, measuring quantities, keeping track of time.

- Let young children wash vegetables, stir or lay the table. Older children measure, cut with a suitable knife under supervision and follow a recipe.
- Explain what is allowed, what is dangerous and why.
- Never leave a child alone near a fire, a cooker or anything on the fire. Agree which adult is keeping watch.
- Turn pan handles towards the back. Keep hot drinks, knives, matches and lighters out of reach.
- Wash hands before cooking and after touching raw meat or eggs.

Cooking without electricity, on a campfire or a gas stove, calls for extra care. See the guide on food without electricity.

Source: [Kind en Gezin: safe in the kitchen](https://www.kindengezin.be/nl/thema/veiligheid/veilig-wonen/veilig-de-keuken)

## Moving every day

Children and young people from 6 to 17 years should move lightly for most of the day, and on top of that vigorously for an average of 60 minutes a day. For sitting activities, prefer reading, drawing or puzzles. Movement can also be a lesson: counting while skipping, reciting the times tables with every jump, walking the compass points on a drawn compass rose.

Source: [Gezond Leven: how much children and young people should move](https://www.gezondleven.be/themas/beweging-sedentair-gedrag/beweegadvies-volgens-leeftijd/beweegadvies-voor-kinderen-en-jongeren/hoeveel-bewegen-kinderen-en-jongeren-best-en-wat-met-stilzitten)
