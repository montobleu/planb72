## What exists in Belgium

Belgium has no network of public air-raid shelters. The sheltering page of the National Crisis Center contains no list or map of public shelters. The Belgian sheltering advice is: go inside the nearest accessible building, your own home, your workplace or another building, and stay there until the danger is over. If you are on the road, go into the nearest accessible building. If you cannot reach a safe building, for example on foot in a rural area or a nature area, move further away from the emergency until you come across a safe and accessible building, and call 112.

If you cannot get through: try another device or have neighbours or bystanders call. If calling does not work at all, ask for help in your neighbourhood or, if you can, go to the nearest police station, fire station or hospital (the map in the app shows them, with the Nearest button). In a major outage the municipality may open an information and reception point.

What does exist in Belgium:

- **Reception centres in an evacuation.** In larger emergencies, the municipality or province provides a place to receive evacuees, for example a sports hall or function hall. On site, a contact person provides information. That is accommodation after an evacuation, not protection against an explosion.
- **An information and reception point of the municipality.** For load shedding (a planned power cut), mayors and governors have prepared emergency measures, for example an information and reception point for the population. Ask your municipal administration in advance which solutions your municipality has. The map in the app (Nearest button) shows the town halls.
- **Emergency plans of schools and communities.** Schools, companies, hospitals, sports centres and other places where many people gather are encouraged to have an internal emergency plan and to practise it. Within the nuclear emergency planning zones, they are also asked to provide iodine tablets for staff and visitors.
- **Let others shelter with you.** The Crisis Center explicitly asks you to let people who need a place shelter with you.

## What the possible shelters on the map are

Because there is no official list, PlanB72 shows places from OpenStreetMap, the open world map maintained by volunteers. These are places that can offer protection during an air attack: underground car parks, metro stations, tunnels, underpasses and cellars. Each place gets the label: **"Possible shelter. Not officially designated. Access and condition unknown."**

The count for Belgium on 17 November 2026:

| Type of place | Number | How it appears in OpenStreetMap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pedestrian tunnel, underpass | 2,551 | Footpath tagged as tunnel, at least 10 metres |
| Historic bunker | 2,517 | military=bunker or historic=bunker |
| Railway tunnel | 592 | Railway tagged as tunnel, at least 50 metres |
| Underground car park | 573 | Car park with parking=underground or location=underground |
| Road tunnel | 449 | Road tagged as tunnel, at least 50 metres |
| Metro station | 83 | Station tagged as metro |
| Other underground station | 82 | Tram or railway station below ground |
| Building with underground floors | 60 | Building with building:levels:underground |
| **Total** | **6,907** | |

Of those 6,907 places, 59 have opening hours filled in and 632 have access filled in (for example public, private or customers only). For the others, you do not know whether you can get in.

## Per type of place

### Underground car park

- An underground car park protects well against explosions and air attacks. Thick walls and earth stop blast waves and debris. Stay well away from open entrances.
- If you are outside during an air threat, an underground car park is a first choice.
- An underground car park counts as a simple shelter, usable during short periods of shelling.

### Metro station and underground station

- An underground metro station protects well during an air attack.
- In an emergency, the metro serves first and foremost as transport to a safe place. So do not count on staying in the station: you may be taken to another place.
- In Belgium, no station has been officially designated as a shelter.

### Pedestrian tunnel and underpass

- Tunnels and underpasses offer some protection.
- Avoid underpasses in high water and heavy rain.

### Road and railway tunnel

Tunnels offer some protection. In a fire in a tunnel, the opposite applies: drive out if you can, and if that is not possible, leave your vehicle and walk against the direction of the smoke to an emergency exit. On the PlanB72 map, road and railway tunnels therefore get a warning: traffic, smoke, trains and live rails are a risk. Do not walk on the tracks.

### Building with underground floors

Cellars under public buildings can be a place of refuge. Contact the owners of underground spaces (shops, gyms, warehouses) in advance and note down their telephone number.

### Historic bunker

OpenStreetMap says that the tag military=bunker is only intended for bunkers that are fully operational or still in military use, but it is also used for civilian bunkers. In practice, many old bunkers and forts in Belgium are on the map. PlanB72 therefore puts them in a separate class with the warning: often closed, overgrown or unsafe.

## When an underground place is dangerous

A place below ground protects against one threat and is life-threatening in another.

| Threat | Underground | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air attack, explosion | Good choice | Earth and concrete stop the blast wave and debris |
| Radioactive substances | Gives protection; in a nuclear accident the Belgian authorities ask for a central room on the ground floor of the nearest building | Thick walls and earth shield against radiation |
| Flood, cloudburst | Never | You become trapped; risk of electrocution |
| Gases and vapours | No | Most gases are heavier than air and collect in cellars and low places |
| Fire, smoke | No, if there is only one exit | Smoke fills the space; walk away from the smoke to an emergency exit |

Other risks:

- **Locked.** A shelter may be closed when the siren sounds. A car park may be locked at night.
- **Full.** Note down two or three addresses in case the first place is full.
- **Poorly maintained.** Cellars may be leaking, damp or poorly ventilated, or contain pipes and hazardous substances. Underpasses and garages are usually not suitable to withstand bombing, and staying for more than a few hours is difficult without chairs, beds, heating and electricity.
- **No second exit.** The safest shelters have several exits.

## How reliable the map is

- **Not official.** No place on the PlanB72 map has been designated as a shelter by the Belgian authorities.
- **Incomplete.** OpenStreetMap contains what volunteers have mapped. A car park or underpass that has not been mapped is missing.
- **Access unknown.** For most places it is not known whether they are public, when they are open and whether you are allowed in during an emergency. In OpenStreetMap, access=private means that the public has no access, access=customers that only customers may enter.
- **Condition unknown.** The map says nothing about the state of a bunker, the ventilation of a car park or the solidity of a tunnel.
- **No right of access.** Belgium has no arrangements for public shelters. A place on the map gives you no right to enter where restrictions apply.

So use the map as a starting point to explore your neighbourhood in advance, not as a promise that a place is open and safe.

## Exploring your neighbourhood in advance

1. **Look for places close to home, school and work.**
2. **Keep the distance short,** preferably 500 to 800 metres on foot. During an air-raid alert there is little time: the safest place to shelter is often where you are.
3. **Note down two or three addresses**, in case the first place is closed or full.
4. **Walk the route in advance**, so that you know how long it really takes.
5. **Check for yourself whether the place is usable.** Is it open, accessible, dry, ventilated, free of fuel or gas cylinders.
6. **Contact the owner or manager** of a cellar, car park or shop with underground space and note down their telephone number.
7. **Ask your employer** what to do in an emergency at work.
8. **Put the addresses on paper** with your emergency plan, together with a paper list of important telephone numbers. The internet and mobile networks can go down during a power cut.

### What to do if a place is closed

1. Try to reach the owner and ask them to open the door.
2. Quickly look for another place nearby: metro, ground floors and cellars of buildings, underground car parks, underpasses.
3. If you are outside without shelter and hear explosions: lie down on the ground at once and cover your head with your hands. A kerb, ditch, concrete bin or staircase can give protection.

### Improving the map

If you know a car park or underpass, you can add the opening hours and access in OpenStreetMap. The next version of the PlanB72 map will include this. Other people's private cellars do not belong on the map.

## How other countries organise it

The Netherlands and Germany also have no working network of public air-raid shelters; both countries are working on it. Sweden, Finland and Switzerland do have a large network of shelters, and Poland and Lithuania have an official map of shelters. No such official list exists in Belgium. That is why PlanB72 only shows possible shelters.

## What to take to a public shelter

- Only take what is quickly to hand. Have ready at home what you need to leave quickly.
- Always keep your identity document, essential medication, mobile and charger or power bank, some cash and your bank card with you.
- Take water in bottles or cans and food you can eat at room temperature.
- If you have time: warm clothing and toiletries.
