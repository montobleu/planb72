Young people in secondary school can largely learn independently, if they have a plan, materials and someone who looks over their work now and then. Let them make their own daily and weekly plan. Also give them real responsibility in the family or in the group: that motivates them and is a learning experience in itself.

In Flanders, the attainment targets (eindtermen) are built around sixteen key competences. The most important ones to focus on when not everything is possible are Dutch, mathematics and sciences, other languages, and the basic literacy (basisgeletterdheid) that every pupil must reach by the end of the first stage. In the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, the Tronc commun runs until the 3rd year of secondary school. Also work on life skills: solving problems, working together, making decisions, knowledge about disasters, health and hygiene, and creative subjects.

## How to go about it

- **A fixed study time.** Two to three blocks a day, with breaks and movement in between.
- **A plan on paper.** For each week: which subjects, which tasks, when they must be finished. Let the young person draw it up themselves and check it together at the end of the week.
- **Work with what there is.** Textbooks, exercise books and course notes from school, printed material, a dictionary, an atlas.
- **Learning by explaining.** Let young people explain a subject to a younger child or to the family. Whoever explains notices what they do not yet understand themselves.
- **Projects.** A project of one or two weeks related to the situation: water, energy, the weather, the history of the commune.
- **Together with people their own age.** In a reception centre or in the neighbourhood, young people can study together.

## First stage of secondary school (1st and 2nd year, 12 to 14 years)

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Dutch | read every day, including youth novels and informative texts; write a summary; a letter or an argumentative text; spelling and grammar using their own texts |
| Other languages | vocabulary by theme; a diary in French or English; acting out dialogues; translating a text with a dictionary |
| Mathematics | fractions, percentages and ratios with recipes and supplies; equations with one unknown; geometry with compasses and a set square; the area and volume of a room or a water barrel |
| Science | the water cycle; building a simple filter of sand and gravel as an experiment, not for drinking; recording plant growth; the human body and hygiene |
| People and society | a timeline of a period in history; the map of Belgium and Europe; how a commune and the emergency services are organised |
| First aid | the recovery position; resuscitation and chest compressions; abdominal thrusts for choking; caring for a wound with little water |
| Practical | planning a meal and cooking for the family; a simple budget with the supplies; a repair with needle and thread |
| Movement and music | sport every day; learning a song or an instrument; drama or a reading-aloud session for younger children |

## Second stage of secondary school (3rd and 4th year, 14 to 16 years)

In the second stage a pupil chooses a course of study. So above all, follow the textbooks and course notes of that course. In addition:

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Dutch and languages | one book a week and writing a review of it; a presentation for the family; an interview with someone from the neighbourhood, written out |
| Mathematics | functions and graphs with their own measurements, such as temperature or water use per day; statistics: mean and spread; geometry on the map |
| Science | electricity with a battery, wire and a small bulb; energy: how much water does a gas canister boil; the chemistry of cooking and storing food; the biology of nutrition and disease |
| People and society | causes and consequences of a historical crisis; the state structure of Belgium; the economics of a household budget |
| Map and compass | reading a topographic map: scale, contour lines, legend; planning and walking a route with map and compass; using the magnetic declination in the map margin |
| First aid | revising resuscitation and the recovery position; the AED during resuscitation, if there is one |
| Practical | keeping a logbook for the family; planning supplies, water and fuel for a week; simple repairs |
| Movement and music | sport, including with younger children; writing their own song |

## Third stage of secondary school (5th and 6th year, 16 to 18 years)

Young people in the third stage are preparing for higher education or work. They can largely study independently with their course notes.

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Languages | reading and discussing literature; writing an essay; a formal letter or a report; speaking and debating on a proposition |
| Mathematics and science | following the subjects of their course of study with the textbooks; making and correcting exercises themselves; applications to the situation, such as the volume of a rain barrel or the force of the wind |
| People and society | citizenship and rights; how a crisis is managed; the history of earlier emergencies in Belgium |
| Weather | types of cloud and forecasting the weather; estimating wind force on the Beaufort scale; their own weather diary with graphs |
| Leadership | organising a games session or study hour for younger children; taking on a task in the reception centre |
| Practical | planning a weekly menu and a food supply; a simple budget; a CV and a job application letter on paper |
| Movement and music | their own training schedule; music, drama or a project that shows something to others |

## Map, compass and weather: what you need to know

- **Scale.** On a topographic map at 1:25 000, 1 cm equals 250 m. At 1:50 000, 1 cm equals 500 m.
- **Contour lines.** They connect points at the same height. If they are close together, the slope is steep. The index contours are drawn thicker.
- **Legend and declination.** The map margin explains the symbols and gives the magnetic declination: the difference between north on the map and north on the compass.
- **Coordinates.** Belgian topographic maps have a grid in Lambert 2008 and, on the 1:25 000 and 1:50 000 maps, also a UTM grid. See the guide on navigation without GPS to read and pass on coordinates, and use the compass in the app.
- **Clouds.** There are low, medium-level and high clouds. Cumulonimbus is the thundercloud.
- **Wind.** Wind force is expressed on the Beaufort scale from 0 (calm) to 12 (hurricane). See the guide on weather and terrain for what to do in a storm.

## Adults

For adults too, learning gives structure and calm. And what you learn, you can pass on.

| Domain | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| Reading and writing | reading every day; reading aloud to children or older people; a diary or logbook; letters that you send later |
| Learning to read | anyone who finds reading or writing difficult can practise with children's books, together with a child or a neighbour; reading aloud also helps those who find reading difficult themselves |
| Languages | learning a language from someone in the neighbourhood or in the reception centre, and teaching your language in return |
| Arithmetic | calculating supplies and water; a budget; planning distances and times |
| Practical skills | practising first aid with others; cooking on another heat source; sewing and mending; gardening and saving seeds; map and compass; reading the weather |
| Passing on | explaining what you know from your job or hobby to others: a lesson, a workshop, a demonstration |
| Movement | 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate-intensity activity, muscle-strengthening exercises twice a week, and regularly breaking up sitting time |
| Culture | singing together, making music, a reading group, a storytelling evening |
