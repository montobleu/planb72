Water is more important than food. Without water from the tap you cannot drink, cannot cook, cannot wash your hands and cannot flush the toilet. This guide follows the order in which you need it: first know how much, then store it, then find water when your supply runs out, and only as a last step make water safe yourself.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [BBK: Vorsorgen für Krisen und Katastrophen](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/Buergerinformationen/Ratgeber/BBK-Vorsorgen-fuer-Krisen-und-Katastrophen.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=44)

## How much water per person per day

Count on 3 litres per person per day, for drinking and cooking. Of this, an adult drinks 1.5 to 2 litres every day. Set aside more if you can, up to 10 litres per person per day, to wash yourself and flush the toilet. Keep a supply for at least 3 days, preferably longer.

These need more water:

- Children, breastfeeding women and sick people.
- Someone with a medical condition.
- Everyone in hot weather or during heavy exertion. In very high temperatures, the need can double.
- Your pets. See the guide on animals.

Do not ration drinking water unless the authorities ask you to. Drink what you need today and look for more for tomorrow. Move little and stay cool, so that your body needs less. Do not drink carbonated or caffeinated drinks instead of water: caffeine and alcohol make the body lose fluid.

## Storing water

### Best choice: bought bottled water

Bottled water from the shop is the safest to store: less chance of contamination with bacteria and a longer shelf life. Keep it in its original, sealed packaging.

- Store it in a cool, dark place.
- Check the best-before date. It is often only one year.
- In a sealed bottle, mineral water keeps almost indefinitely. Manufacturers usually give glass bottles a best-before date of two years and PET bottles one of a year, because PET is less airtight.
- Do not keep PET bottles in full sun or in the car. A substance from the bottle can then get into the water and affect its taste and smell. This substance is harmless to health.

### Good choice: tap water in your own containers

This is how you store water yourself:

1. Buy containers that are suitable for food.
2. Wash them thoroughly with washing-up liquid.
3. Disinfect them before you fill them, with a solution of one teaspoon of unscented liquid household bleach to about a litre of water.
4. Fill them with water from the public mains.
5. Replace water you stored yourself every six months.

Use clean containers or buckets with a tight-fitting lid and keep them in a cool, dark place. A bucket with a wide neck is easy to clean. A tap on the container prevents hands and cups from getting into the water.

### Extra: bottles in the freezer

Put plastic bottles of water in the freezer. During a power cut they act as ice packs. When the ice has melted, you can drink the water. Do not fill the bottles to the brim, or they may burst.

### Where in the house

- Choose a dry, cool and dark place, such as a shed or hall cupboard. Cellars and garages are cool, but more prone to flooding.
- In a flat, you can put together an emergency kit with the residents of the building, for example in a cellar or another shared space.

### In advance or at the last moment

If an interruption is expected, also have some empty, clean bottles or a jerrycan ready and set aside extra non-drinking water to wash yourself and clean. Collect rainwater for the garden, so that you do not waste drinking water.

## Order of use

1. First drink water that you know is not contaminated.
2. When that runs out, use questionable water (cloudy water from the tap, water from streams or ponds) only after you have treated it.
3. If you cannot treat it, put off drinking questionable water for as long as possible, but do not become dehydrated.

Treat water of uncertain quality for everything that goes in your mouth: drinking, washing or preparing food, washing up, brushing teeth and making ice cubes. Also do not make baby formula with unsafe water and do not wash your hands with it.

Source: [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water) · [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html)

## Finding water in and around the house

### In the house

| Source | Usable for | What you need to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water heater with storage tank (boiler) | drinking, cooking, hygiene | Only if the tank is part of your drinking water system and not of the central heating. A tankless (instantaneous) water heater does not provide this. |
| Water in the pipes | drinking, cooking | After closing the main stopcock you can let the water run out of the pipes (see below). First make sure the water source is not contaminated. |
| Melted ice cubes | drinking | Only if the ice was made from uncontaminated water. Otherwise treat it first. |
| Bottles in the freezer | drinking | Drinkable after melting. |
| Drinks and tinned food in the house | drinking | Sealed packaged drinks and the liquid from tinned vegetables or fruit, preferably packed in water. Caffeine and alcohol in large amounts make you lose fluid. Use nothing from packaging that has been under floodwater. |

**Water from the pipes**

1. Check that the water source is not contaminated.
2. Close the main stopcock of the home.
3. Let air into the pipes by opening the tap at the highest point of the home.
4. Draw off water at the tap at the lowest point.

In a flat, the main stopcock is often not inside your home. How much water you get then depends on your floor and on how many others are drawing water from the pipes at the same time.

**Water from the water heater**

1. Switch off the energy supply to the appliance. For an electric appliance, use the correct fuse in the fuse box. For a gas appliance, turn the knob to the pilot setting.
2. Close the water supply to the appliance.
3. Let air in, through the pressure relief valve or by opening a hot tap in the house.
4. Put a container under the drain valve at the bottom of the tank and draw off what you need.

The water can be very hot. Wear gloves and safety glasses.

### Not for drinking

| Source | Why not | Usable for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radiators and boiler of the central heating | never use for drinking, cooking or hygiene | nothing |
| Toilet bowl and cistern | unsafe to drink, even after treatment | flushing the toilet, cleaning |
| Swimming pool, water feature, hot tub | not for drinking | sanitation (swimming pool, water feature) |
| Waterbed | unsafe to drink | sanitation |
| Floodwater | unsafe, even after treatment | nothing |
| Water with floating debris, a strange smell or colour | unsafe, even after treatment | nothing |
| Water that may be contaminated with fuel, heavy metals or toxic substances | boiling or disinfecting does not make it safe | nothing |

### Around the house

- **Rainwater.** Do not drink collected rainwater: it is not clean enough. Use it for washing, cleaning, flushing the toilet and watering the garden, so that you save drinking water. Pets that go outside can drink rainwater.
- **Streams, rivers, ponds, lakes, springs.** Only after treating or filtering. During a prolonged power cut, avoid contact with water from ditches and canals, because waste water is then treated less well.
- **Water from the authorities.** In case of a shortage, the mayor, the governor or the minister can have bottled water distributed. Municipalities can also set up water tanks. Bring your own clean containers for this. Where and when water is distributed will be announced on the radio and through the other channels of the authorities, such as police loudspeaker vans, information boards, leaflets or door-to-door visits.

## Purifying water: overview of the methods

No single method does everything. A combination is often the best solution. The ordinary household methods remove dirt and pathogens, but not chemical contamination. Only distilling removes most chemicals.

| Method | Kills bacteria and viruses | Parasites such as Cryptosporidium | Removes chemicals, salts, heavy metals | Needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boiling | yes | yes | no | fuel, pot |
| Chlorine (bleach) | yes, most | Cryptosporidium is resistant | no | unscented bleach, dropper |
| Chlorine dioxide tablets | yes | yes, if you use them exactly according to the instructions | no | tablets |
| Chlorine or iodine tablets | yes | no, not against Cryptosporidium | no | tablets |
| Portable filter | bacteria (pores 0.3 micron or smaller), not viruses | yes (pores 1 micron or smaller) | no | filter |
| Solar disinfection (SODIS) | yes | not mentioned | no | clear plastic bottles, sun |
| UV device | yes, for small amounts of clear water | not mentioned | no | device, batteries |
| Distilling | yes | yes | yes, most | fuel, pot, cup |

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf)

## Step 0: first make cloudy water clear

Almost every method works better, or only, on clear water.

- **Let it settle.** Let cloudy water stand so that larger particles sink to the bottom. Even after settling, you still have to treat it. Storing it for two days reduces the contamination further.
- **Strain it.** Pour it through a coffee filter, kitchen paper or several layers of clean cloth, or let it settle and skim off the clear water.
- **Aerate it.** This makes iron and manganese settle out and improves smell and taste: shake a half-full container vigorously for about five minutes and then let it stand for 30 minutes.

Source: [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf) · [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water)

## Method 1: boiling (best choice)

Boiling is the safest method. It does use a lot of fuel.

**How long.** Let the water boil vigorously for at least 5 minutes. If your water supply company has issued a boil water advice, follow that. You will hear it on the radio; if the network works, also via BE-Alert and the official websites.

**Method**

1. First make cloudy water clear (see step 0).
2. Bring the water to the boil in a large pot or kettle until it bubbles vigorously.
3. Let it boil for at least 5 minutes, or for as long as the boil water advice says. Some water will evaporate.
4. Let it cool.
5. Store it in clean, disinfected containers with a tight-fitting lid.

**Improving the taste.** Boiled water tastes flat. Pour it back and forth a few times between two clean containers and let it stand for a few hours, shake the cooled water vigorously in a closed container, or add a pinch of salt per litre.

**With an official boil water advice.** The boil water advice tells you what you need boiled water for. With the boil water advice from De Watergroep in Roeselare (March 2026) it was as follows: first let all taps run for at least 2 minutes, then boil water for drinking, preparing food, caring for wounds and brushing teeth. Washing, washing up, flushing the toilet and cleaning could be done with unboiled tap water. It remained better to sterilise baby bottles.

**If it is not possible.** If you have no fuel or no way to cook, go to chlorine (method 2).

## Method 2: chlorine from household bleach

You can disinfect water with ordinary liquid household bleach (sodium hypochlorite).

**Which bleach**

- Unscented. No scented bleach, no colour-safe bleach, no bleach with added cleaners.
- First follow the instructions for drinking water on the label, if there are any.
- If there are none, look for the percentage of sodium hypochlorite on the label and use the table below.

**How much**

| Strength of the bleach | Per litre of clear water | Per 5 litres of clear water (five times the amount per litre) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 to 9 per cent sodium hypochlorite | 2 drops (0.1 ml) | 10 drops |
| 1 per cent sodium hypochlorite | 10 drops (0.5 ml) | 50 drops |

If the water is cloudy, coloured or very cold: use double the amount.

**Method**

1. Make the water as clear as possible (step 0).
2. Add the correct amount.
3. Stir well.
4. Let it stand for at least 30 minutes.
5. Smell it. The water should smell slightly of chlorine. If it does not, add the same amount again and wait 15 minutes. If it still does not smell of chlorine, throw it away and find another source.
6. Store it in clean, disinfected containers with a tight-fitting lid.

**Limits**

- Chlorine kills most viruses and bacteria. Some parasites, especially Cryptosporidium, are resistant to it.
- Chlorine does not help against chemical contamination.
- Chlorine products differ in strength. Always use them according to the manufacturer's instructions and keep them in their original packaging. You cannot tell how much you need by looking at the product.

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf)

## Method 3: tablets and drops

Follow the instructions on the packaging. Each product is made for a specific amount of water.

| Product | Kills | Does not kill | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chlorine dioxide | viruses, bacteria, Cryptosporidium (if you use it exactly according to the instructions) | | |
| Chlorine tablets | viruses, bacteria | Cryptosporidium | |
| Iodine and iodine tablets | viruses, bacteria | Cryptosporidium | Do not use if you are pregnant, have thyroid problems or are hypersensitive to iodine. No one should drink water treated with iodine for more than a few weeks in a row. |

There are also products that make particles settle out and disinfect the water with chlorine at the same time.

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf)

## Method 4: filtering

**Portable filters**

- Choose a filter with an absolute pore size of 1 micron or smaller to stop parasites such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium.
- Choose 0.3 micron or smaller to stop bacteria.
- Portable filters do not stop viruses. So disinfect the filtered water afterwards with chlorine, iodine or chlorine dioxide.

**Other filters**

- **Ceramic filters (candle filters).** The water runs slowly through a ceramic candle. Some are treated with silver, which kills bacteria; in that case the water does not need to be boiled after filtering. Regularly brush the dirt off the candle under running water. Ceramic filters keep for a long time, so you can get them in advance.
- **Membrane filters.** Can be very effective, some also against smaller organisms such as viruses. Follow the manufacturer's instructions; they often need to be cleaned.
- **Sand filters.** A container with layers of sand and gravel that the water flows through.

Assume that a filter does not stop viruses unless the manufacturer explicitly says otherwise, and disinfect after filtering.

**No filter in the house.** A cloth, coffee filter or kitchen paper makes water clearer, but does not make it safe (step 0). Boil or disinfect afterwards.

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf)

## Method 5: solar disinfection (SODIS)

The UV radiation of the sun kills pathogens in water.

**Method**

1. Make the water clear: filter it, or let it settle and skim off the clear water.
2. Fill clean, transparent plastic bottles of one or two litres with the clear water.
3. Lay the bottles on their side in full sun. A dark surface helps.
4. Leave them for as long as needed (see below).
5. Let the water cool and shake it vigorously before use.

**How long.** Leave the bottles in the sun for at least 24 hours, or 48 hours when it is cloudy. These are the times for sunny countries around the equator. In Belgium the sun is weaker: so preferably leave them longer. How long it really takes depends on the transparency of the bottle, the strength of the sun and the clarity of the water.

**When not.** With cloudy water that you cannot make clear. With chemical contamination.

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf)

## Method 6: UV device

UV devices only work on small amounts of clear water. Filter cloudy water first. Follow the instructions of the device. A UV device needs electricity or batteries; see the guide on light and energy.

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html)

## Method 7: distilling

Boiling and chlorine kill most microbes. Distilling also removes microbes that are resistant to them, as well as heavy metals, salts and most other chemicals. You boil the water and collect only the vapour that turns back into liquid.

**Method**

1. Fill a pot halfway with water.
2. Tie a cup to the handle of the lid, so that the cup hangs upright when you put the lid upside down on the pot. The cup must not hang in the water.
3. Put the lid upside down on the pot.
4. Boil the water for 20 minutes.
5. The water that drips from the lid into the cup is distilled.

This gives you little water for a lot of fuel. It is the only household method that removes most chemicals.

Source: [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water)

## What does not help against chemical contamination

- Water with fuel, heavy metals, toxic chemicals or radioactive substances does not become safe by boiling, disinfecting or filtering. Use bottled water or another source and, if the network works, contact the health authorities.
- Only distilling removes most chemicals (see method 7).

In an accident involving hazardous substances or a nuclear incident, follow the instructions of the authorities on the use of tap water. These come via radio, TV and police loudspeaker vans, among other channels. See the scenario cards and the guides on sheltering and nuclear emergencies.

Source: [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf) · [Regional Water Providers Consortium: Emergency Water Sources](https://regionalh2o.org/node/39) · [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water) · [National Crisis Center: Information during an emergency](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/stay-informed/get-informed-emergency-situation)

## After the interruption

- If brown or cloudy water comes out of the tap, let it run for a few minutes.
- If little water comes out of the tap, the pressure still has to recover. It increases slowly.
- Keep using water sparingly. Do not waste it.
- After a flood: listen to the recommendations of the authorities on the use of tap water, for example on a battery radio.
- A boil water advice remains valid until checks show that the water is no longer contaminated.

## Per person and per situation

- **Babies.** Do not make baby formula with unsafe water. Preferably use ready-to-feed formula; if you do have to make up powder, use bottled water, or boiled water as a last resort. Keep sterilising baby bottles, also during a boil water advice. See also the guide on food without electricity.
- **Pregnant women and people with thyroid problems.** No water treated with iodine.
- **Older people.** For people over 65, count on 2 litres of drinks per day instead of 1.5 litres (see the guide on emergency supplies).
- **Sick people and people with young children.** Need more water. Contaminated drinking water can have serious consequences for young children, older people, pregnant women and people with a weakened immune system.
- **High up in a block of flats.** During a power cut, no more water may come out of the tap even on the third floor, because pumps stop working. So build up a supply here in advance.
- **On the road or in the car.** Keep bottles of water in the glove compartment or the boot, extra in summer.
