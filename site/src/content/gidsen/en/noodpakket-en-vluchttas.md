The National Crisis Center divides the emergency kit into two parts: a backpack or bag that you can take with you quickly, and a supply for at home for when you have to shelter or are without electricity, water, telephone and internet for a while. This guide covers the backpack and the smaller kits for the car and for work. The supply at home is covered in the guide on the emergency supply.

There is no standard emergency kit. Take into account babies or young children, pets, medication you use every day and other personal needs. You do not need to buy an expensive kit: you already have many of the items at home. Add to it step by step.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf)

## The grab bag: basic list per person

The Belgian list for the backpack, from the brochure and the website of the Crisis Center.

**Money and documents**

- Copies of important documents such as your identity card, driving licence and insurance papers. The website also mentions copies of insurance contracts and of all identity cards.
- Your emergency plan or a list of important telephone numbers: family and friends, emergency numbers, your gas, water and electricity supplier, the monitoring centre of your alarm system, your GP, your insurer.
- Pen and paper.
- A little cash (see the section on cash).

**Food and drink**

- One or two small bottles of water, about one litre per person.
- A few biscuits, energy bars or nuts.

The Crisis Center advises against putting tinned food in the backpack, because it must not become too heavy. If you evacuate, you go to friends, family or a reception centre where food is provided.

**Health and safety**

- A small first aid kit: bandages, plasters, compresses, scissors, tweezers, safety pins, painkillers, disinfectant.
- Essential medication for at least three days (72 hours).
- Personal care products: wet wipes, disinfecting gel, toothpaste, toothbrush, toilet paper, tissues, sanitary towels.
- A multi-purpose pocket knife.
- A whistle to let the emergency services know where you are.
- A charged power bank and mobile phone charger.

Source: [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf) · [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home)

## The bag itself and where to keep it

- **Which bag.** A sturdy backpack (Crisis Center). Denk vooruit recommends waterproof packaging, such as a backpack. Ready.gov: put the items in airtight plastic bags and everything together in one or two portable containers, such as plastic tubs or a duffel bag.
- **Where.** In a central, easily accessible place in the house (Crisis Center). Hang the house keys near the door. Make sure everyone in the house knows where the bag is and who takes it (Ready.gov).
- **How many bags.** Ready.gov talks about a bag that you can carry if you leave on foot or by public transport, and extra items for longer distances if you have a car.
- **Checking.** Denk vooruit: every six months, including expiry dates. Ready.gov: review what your family needs every year.

No bag ready and immediate danger: the Crisis Center says that in that case you should not lose time gathering documents or belongings. Evacuate as quickly as possible.

## Documents: which ones and how to store them

**Which ones**

The Crisis Center asks for copies of identity cards, driving licence and insurance papers. The BBK gives a more detailed breakdown. Keep documents that:

| Type | Examples (BBK, Ready.gov) |
| --- | --- |
| prove your identity | birth certificate, identity card, photo ID of each family member |
| prove ownership | title deed, purchase contracts |
| prove financial entitlements | insurance policies, income, tax returns, housing costs |
| prove qualifications | diplomas, certificates |
| prove rights | powers of attorney, court decisions |
| contain important personal information | will, medical reports, vaccination booklet, details of your doctor, health insurance, list of passwords |
| have emotional value | a selection of photos you do not want to lose |

**How to store them**

1. Keep the originals in a fireproof or waterproof container (BBK).
2. Ask the institution that issues the document whether you need the original or a certified copy (BBK).
3. Put copies in the grab bag (Crisis Center).
4. Make a digital copy and store it in the cloud, on a portable hard drive or on a USB stick (BBK, Ready.gov).
5. Be careful about sharing financial details such as account numbers (Ready.gov).

The emergency plan of the Crisis Center should also be on paper, with a copy held by your contact persons. See the guide on the family emergency plan.

## Cash

During a power cut, payment terminals and cash machines do not work (OFF ON brochure of the Crisis Center, BBK).

| Source | How much |
| --- | --- |
| National Crisis Center | a little cash to buy basic necessities if you can no longer pay online or withdraw money; provide roughly enough for 72 hours, but be careful with large amounts at home |
| Denk vooruit (Netherlands) | for 72 hours, 70 euros per adult and 30 euros per child |
| MCF (Sweden) | about 1,000 Swedish kronor per adult, preferably in different denominations |
| Ready.gov | a small amount at home in a safe place, in small notes |

The BBK puts cash both in the grab bag and in the supply at home. The MCF recommends paying with cash from time to time and having other means of payment as well.

## Children

**In the bag, per child (Crisis Center)**

- The health or vaccination booklet.
- Nappies.
- Spare clothing.
- Special baby food, if needed.
- A few toys. The brochure recommends letting the child choose a small toy or cuddly toy themselves, or something else that makes them feel safe or calm.

**Additions**

- Formula powder, disposable nappies and baby wipes, the medicines your children need (Crisis Center, plan for those who need extra help).
- Bottles and nappy rash cream; books, games and puzzles (Ready.gov).

Involve children in putting together the emergency kit (Crisis Center).

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf) · [National Crisis Center: A plan for those who need extra help](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/people-who-need-additional-help) · [Ready.gov: Build A Kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit)

## Older people

Ready.gov gives these additions for older people:

- Prescription medicines for several days.
- A list of all medicines, the dosage and any allergies.
- Extra glasses, contact lenses, hearing aids and batteries.
- A reserve supply of oxygen, if you use it.
- A list with the type and serial number of medical devices, with any operating instructions.
- Copies of insurance cards.
- Contact details of doctors, family or friends who should be notified if you are injured.
- Food, water, a collar with name tag and medical records for a service or assistance dog.

The Crisis Center recommends asking your GP or pharmacist for a medication list and putting it with your important documents. In the case of dementia, Ready.gov recommends taking a pillow and blanket or other familiar objects.

Source: [Ready.gov: People with Disabilities](https://www.ready.gov/disability) · [Ready.gov: Older Adults](https://www.ready.gov/older-adults) · [National Crisis Center: A plan for those who need extra help](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/people-who-need-additional-help)

## People with a disability

Add to the basic list what is essential for you, for example medicines or aids (Crisis Center). The BBK mentions spare batteries, rechargeable batteries and chargers for technical aids, mobility aids, a medication plan and a supply of medicines, food and water for an assistance animal, a list of emergency contacts and an emergency card with information about illnesses, allergies, aids and how you communicate.

Per disability, according to Ready.gov:

| Situation | Extra in the kit |
| --- | --- |
| Deaf or hard of hearing | extra batteries for the hearing aid; pen and paper; a battery-powered lantern so that you can sign or lip-read in the dark; a weather radio with a text display and light signal |
| Blind or partially sighted | emergency supplies marked in braille or large print; a list of the items on a USB stick or as an audio file; communication devices such as a braille or deafblind device |
| Speech disability | a plan for taking or replacing your communication device; laminated cards with phrases or pictograms |
| Intellectual or developmental disability | charged devices with videos and activities; spare chargers; sheets and rope or a small pop-up tent to limit stimuli; noise-cancelling headphones; familiar snacks |
| Mobility disability | if possible a lightweight manual wheelchair as a backup to an electric one; an extra battery for an electric wheelchair or other aids, always charged; a puncture repair kit or tyre sealant and possibly an inner tube; a portable pump; an extra cane or walker; an extra seat cushion if you need it for your skin or balance |

The Crisis Center recommends keeping aids such as a white cane in a fixed place, so that you can find them quickly, and having an alternative if needed.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan for those who need extra help](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/people-who-need-additional-help) · [BBK: Vorsorgen für Krisen und Katastrophen](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/Buergerinformationen/Ratgeber/BBK-Vorsorgen-fuer-Krisen-und-Katastrophen.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=44) · [Ready.gov: People with Disabilities](https://www.ready.gov/disability)

## Pets

**With the bag, per animal (Crisis Center)**

- Medicines or a medication list.
- Pet passport, vaccination booklet and contact details of your vet.
- A recent photo of your pet. The brochure asks for a photo of you together with your animal.
- Two food and water bowls and some food.
- Poo bags.
- A few toys.
- Collar or harness and lead.
- Near the backpack: a transport cage or travel basket suited to your animal. For exotic animals: make sure you can transport them safely and take the necessary equipment.

**Additions (Ready.gov)**

- A spare lead, collar and name tag.
- Copies of registration and other documents in a waterproof container and digitally.
- Cat litter and litter tray, newspapers, kitchen roll, bin bags.
- Familiar items such as a blanket.
- Two kits: a large one for at home and a light one for when you have to evacuate.

Diereninrampen.nl, to which the Dutch information booklet refers, uses 50 ml per kilogram of body weight per day as a general guideline for drinking water. More in the guide on animals.

## In the car

In emergencies you may be stuck in traffic for a long time. The Crisis Center recommends having in the car:

- Small bottles of water and non-perishable food, for example cereal bars, in the glove compartment or the boot.
- First aid kit with instructions.
- A torch, preferably wind-up; otherwise spare batteries.
- Jump leads.
- A GPS device or road maps.
- Accident report form.
- High-visibility vests for all passengers.
- A warning triangle.
- Games to keep children occupied.

In winter: blankets, gloves, warm clothing and boots, an ice scraper. During a cold wave, also a small bottle of water and hot drinks for when you are stuck.

In summer: sunglasses, extra small bottles of water, a sunshade.

Ready.gov adds sand for winter and recommends keeping the tank full. The BBK says that a car with a full tank keeps you mobile if the fuel supply fails. The MCF recommends a mobile phone charger for the car.

## At work, at school and in buildings with many people

An emergency can strike when you are not at home. The Crisis Center says: stay where you are until you know for sure that it is safe to go home.

**For yourself.** The Crisis Center recommends a small emergency supply at work, for example a bottle of water, a warm jumper and a blanket. Ready.gov recommends preparing to shelter at work for at least 24 hours, with food, water, medicines and comfortable walking shoes in a bag you can grab and go.

**For the person in charge of a school, company, shop, hospital, crèche or sports hall.** The Crisis Center asks for extra attention to:

- A basic supply of bottled water and long-life food.
- A basic medicine cabinet.
- Iodine tablets. An organisation can collect a supply free of charge from the pharmacy in advance.
- A few torches, candles and lighters or matches.
- A battery-powered radio.
- A few blankets.

An internal emergency plan is useful and sometimes mandatory. Keep it up to date and practise it regularly.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit on location](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-site) · [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf) · [Ready.gov: Build A Kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit)

## A kit for a whole building

In a block of flats, student accommodation or other form of shared living, you can put together an emergency kit jointly instead of per household unit, for example in a cellar or communal area (Crisis Center). Agree who has the keys and who has access to the water supply, gas tap and electricity meters.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home)

## Grab bag checklist

- [ ] Copy of identity card, driving licence, insurance.
- [ ] Emergency plan or list of telephone numbers on paper.
- [ ] Pen and paper.
- [ ] Cash for about 72 hours, in small denominations.
- [ ] About one litre of water per person.
- [ ] Biscuits, energy bars or nuts.
- [ ] Small first aid kit.
- [ ] Medication for at least three days and a medication list.
- [ ] Personal care products and sanitary towels.
- [ ] Pocket knife and whistle.
- [ ] Charged power bank and mobile phone charger.
- [ ] Warm clothing, rainwear, sturdy shoes, spare clothing.
- [ ] Per child: health booklet, nappies, clothing, baby food, toy or cuddly toy.
- [ ] Per pet: papers, photo, food, bowls, lead, transport cage.
- [ ] Aids and spare batteries for those who need them.
- [ ] House keys in a fixed place by the door.
- [ ] Check in the diary, every six months.
