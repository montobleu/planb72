The emergency kit consists of two parts: a backpack or bag that you can take with you quickly, and a supply for at home for when you have to shelter or are without electricity, water, telephone and internet for a while. This guide covers the backpack and the smaller kits for the car and for work. The supply at home is covered in the guide on the emergency supply.

There is no standard emergency kit. Take into account babies or young children, pets, medication you use every day and other personal needs. You do not need to buy an expensive kit: you already have many of the items at home. Add to it step by step.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf)

## The grab bag: basic list per person

**Money and documents**

- Copies of important documents: the identity cards of everyone in the household, your driving licence and your insurance contracts.
- Your emergency plan or a list of important telephone numbers, on paper: family and friends, emergency numbers, your gas, water and electricity supplier, the monitoring centre of your alarm system, your GP, your insurer. Write down the address of your contacts too. That way you can find each other again, even if your mobile is flat or the network is not working. Also put the meeting place you agreed with your household in your emergency plan.
- Pen and paper.
- A little cash (see the section on cash).

**Food and drink**

- One or two small bottles of water, about one litre per person.
- A few biscuits, energy bars or nuts.

Do not put tinned food in the backpack: it must not become too heavy. If you evacuate, you go to friends, family or a reception centre where food is provided.

**Health and safety**

- A small first aid kit: bandages, plasters, compresses, scissors, tweezers, safety pins, painkillers, disinfectant.
- Essential medication for at least three days (72 hours).
- Personal care products: wet wipes, disinfecting gel, toothpaste, toothbrush, toilet paper, tissues, sanitary towels.
- A multi-purpose pocket knife.
- A whistle to let the emergency services know where you are.
- A charged power bank and mobile phone charger. Do not count on always being able to call: during a power cut, the internet and mobile networks can also fail.

## What else you can add

Choose what suits your situation.

- Warm clothing, rainwear and sturdy shoes: you do not know how long you will be outside.
- A complete set of spare clothing.
- A sleeping bag or blanket, for a night in a reception centre.
- A reusable water bottle to refill.
- Cutlery and a tin opener.
- A torch.
- A battery-powered, solar or wind-up radio.
- A lighter or matches in a waterproof box.
- Sun cream and a hat.
- Work gloves.
- Spares of aids such as glasses or a hearing aid.
- A map of your town, village or neighbourhood and a compass, for when GPS and phone do not work.
- Spare keys for house and car.
- A dust mask, to filter contaminated air.
- A bank card in addition to cash.

## The bag itself and where to keep it

- **Which bag.** A sturdy, waterproof backpack. Put the items in airtight plastic bags.
- **Where.** In a central, easily accessible place in the house. Hang the house keys near the door. Make sure everyone in the house knows where the bag is and who takes it.
- **How many bags.** A bag that you can carry if you leave on foot or by public transport. If you have a car, you can have extra items ready for longer distances.
- **Checking.** Every six months, including expiry dates. Also check whether the contents still match what your family needs.

No bag ready and immediate danger: do not lose time gathering documents or belongings. Evacuate as quickly as possible.

## Documents: which ones and how to store them

**Which ones**

Keep documents that:

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| prove your identity | birth certificate, identity card, photo ID of each family member |
| prove ownership | title deed, purchase contracts |
| prove financial entitlements | insurance policies, income, tax returns, housing costs |
| prove qualifications | diplomas, certificates |
| prove rights | powers of attorney, court decisions |
| contain important personal information | will, medical reports, vaccination booklet, details of your doctor, health insurance, list of passwords |
| have emotional value | a selection of photos you do not want to lose |

**How to store them**

1. Keep the originals in a fireproof or waterproof container.
2. Ask the institution that issues the document whether you need the original or a certified copy.
3. Put copies in the grab bag.
4. Make a digital copy and store it in the cloud, on a portable hard drive or on a USB stick.
5. Be careful about sharing financial details such as account numbers.

Your emergency plan should also be on paper, with a copy held by your contact persons. See the guide on the family emergency plan.

## Cash

During a power cut, payment terminals and cash machines do not work.

- Keep a little cash ready to buy basic necessities if you can no longer pay electronically or withdraw money: enough for about 72 hours, for example 70 euros per adult and 30 euros per child.
- Use small notes, in different denominations.
- Do not keep large amounts at home. Keep the cash in a safe place.
- Put some in the grab bag and keep some with the supply at home.
- Pay with cash from time to time and also keep other means of payment.

## Children

**In the bag, per child**

- The health or vaccination booklet.
- Nappies.
- Spare clothing.
- Special baby food, if needed.
- A few toys. Let the child choose a small toy or cuddly toy themselves, or something else that makes them feel safe or calm.

**Additions**

- Formula powder, disposable nappies and baby wipes, the medicines your children need.
- Bottles and nappy rash cream; books, games and puzzles.

Involve children in putting together the emergency kit.

## Older people

For older people, also include:

- Prescription medicines for several days.
- A list of all medicines, the dosage and any allergies.
- Extra glasses, contact lenses, hearing aids and batteries.
- A reserve supply of oxygen, if you use it.
- A list with the type and serial number of medical devices, with any operating instructions.
- Copies of insurance cards.
- Contact details of doctors, family or friends who should be notified if you are injured, on a list in waterproof packaging.
- Food, water, a collar with name tag and medical records for a service or assistance dog.

Ask your GP or pharmacist for a medication list and put it with your important documents. In the case of dementia: take a pillow and blanket or other familiar objects.

## People with a disability

Add to the basic list what is essential for you, for example medicines or aids. Think of alternative ways to communicate with others, even if you have no phone or internet. Also think of spare batteries, rechargeable batteries and chargers for technical aids, mobility aids, a medication plan and a supply of medicines, food and water for an assistance animal, a list of emergency contacts and an emergency card with information about illnesses, allergies, aids and how you communicate.

Per disability:

| Situation | Extra in the kit |
| --- | --- |
| Deaf or hard of hearing | extra batteries for the hearing aid; pen and paper; a battery-powered lantern so that you can sign or lip-read in the dark; a weather radio with a text display and light signal |
| Blind or partially sighted | emergency supplies marked in braille or large print; a list of the items on a USB stick or as an audio file; communication devices such as a braille or deafblind device |
| Speech disability | a plan for taking or replacing your communication device; laminated cards with phrases or pictograms |
| Intellectual or developmental disability | charged devices with videos and activities; spare chargers; sheets and rope or a small pop-up tent to limit stimuli; noise-cancelling headphones; familiar snacks |
| Mobility disability | if possible a lightweight manual wheelchair as a backup to an electric one; an extra battery for an electric wheelchair or other aids, always charged; a puncture repair kit or tyre sealant and possibly an inner tube; a portable pump; an extra cane or walker; an extra seat cushion if you need it for your skin or balance |

Keep aids such as a white cane in a fixed place, so that you can find them quickly, and have an alternative if needed.

## Pets

**With the bag, per animal**

- Medicines or a medication list.
- Pet passport, vaccination booklet and contact details of your vet.
- A recent photo of you together with your animal.
- Two food and water bowls and some food.
- Poo bags.
- A few toys.
- Collar or harness and lead.
- Near the backpack: a transport cage or travel basket suited to your animal. For exotic animals: make sure you can transport them safely and take the necessary equipment.

**Also useful**

- A spare lead, collar and name tag.
- Copies of registration and other documents in a waterproof container and digitally.
- Cat litter and litter tray, newspapers, kitchen roll, bin bags.
- Familiar items such as a blanket.
- Two kits: a large one for at home and a light one for when you have to evacuate.

For drinking water, allow about 50 ml per kilogram of body weight per day. More in the guide on animals.

## In the car

In emergencies you may be stuck in traffic for a long time. Keep in the car:

- Small bottles of water and non-perishable food, for example cereal bars, in the glove compartment or the boot.
- First aid kit with instructions.
- A torch, preferably wind-up; otherwise spare batteries.
- Jump leads.
- A GPS device or road maps.
- Accident report form.
- High-visibility vests for all passengers.
- A warning triangle.
- Games to keep children occupied.

- A mobile phone charger for the car.

In winter: blankets, gloves, warm clothing and boots, an ice scraper and some sand. During a cold wave, also a small bottle of water and hot drinks for when you are stuck.

In summer: sunglasses, extra small bottles of water, a sunshade.

Keep the tank full: that way you stay mobile if the fuel supply fails.

## At work, at school and in buildings with many people

An emergency can strike when you are not at home. Stay where you are until you know for sure that it is safe to go home.

**For yourself.** Keep a small emergency supply at work, for example a bottle of water, a warm jumper and a blanket. Be prepared to stay there for at least 24 hours: put food, water, medicines and comfortable walking shoes in a bag you can grab and go.

**For the person in charge of a school, company, shop, hospital, crèche or sports hall.** Pay extra attention to:

- A basic supply of bottled water and long-life food.
- A basic medicine cabinet.
- Iodine tablets. An organisation can collect a supply free of charge from the pharmacy in advance.
- A few torches, candles and lighters or matches.
- A battery-powered radio.
- A few blankets.

An internal emergency plan is useful and sometimes mandatory. Keep it up to date and practise it regularly.

## A kit for a whole building

In a block of flats, student accommodation or other form of shared living, you can put together an emergency kit jointly instead of per household unit, for example in a cellar or communal area. Agree who has the keys and who has access to the water supply, gas tap and electricity meters.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home)

- [ ] About one litre of water per person.
- [ ] Biscuits, energy bars or nuts.
- [ ] Small first aid kit.
- [ ] Medication for at least three days and a medication list.
- [ ] Personal care products and sanitary towels.
- [ ] Pocket knife and whistle.
- [ ] Charged power bank and mobile phone charger.
- [ ] Warm clothing, rainwear, sturdy shoes, spare clothing.
- [ ] Per child: health booklet, nappies, clothing, baby food, toy or cuddly toy.
- [ ] Per pet: papers, photo, food, bowls, lead, transport cage.
- [ ] Aids and spare batteries for those who need them.
- [ ] House keys in a fixed place by the door.
- [ ] Check in the diary, every six months.
