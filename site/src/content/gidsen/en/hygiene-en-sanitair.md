Without water you can no longer shower, wash your hands or flush the toilet. After a disaster, too little water and contaminated water are usually the first and most important causes of illness. Hygiene is therefore not a luxury. This guide gives the options per topic, from best choice to emergency solution.

Source: [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf)

## What to get in beforehand

- Toilet paper.
- Wet wipes and disinfecting gel.
- Tissues.
- Sanitary towels and tampons.
- Nappies and baby wipes.
- Soap, toothpaste and toothbrushes.
- Bin bags, including large, strong ones to hang in the toilet.
- Cat litter, sawdust, compost litter or shredded paper.
- A bucket with a lid.
- A camping toilet with spare bags or fluid.
- Unscented household bleach.
- Rubber or vinyl gloves, household gloves.
- Kitchen roll, disinfectant and detergent.
- Disposable cutlery and tableware.
- Dry shampoo and hand-cleaning paste.

## First: is the sewer still working

There are two different situations.

**1. No water comes out of the tap, but the sewer works.** Then also set water aside for flushing the toilet. If you pour a bucket of water into the bowl, count on 3 to 5 litres per person per day; an ordinary flush uses much more. Water that is not drinkable, such as water from the cistern, a swimming pool or a waterbed, can be used for the toilet (see the guide on water).

**2. The sewer no longer drains.** During a prolonged power cut, the sewage pumping stations pump less. The toilet then sometimes does not flush properly, and an overfull sewer can push wastewater into your house through the toilet, floor drain or sink. In that case:

- Pour as little water as possible down the sink and floor drains.
- Flush the toilet as little as possible, or use a chemical toilet or a bucket with a bin bag.
- To stop wastewater flowing back: stretch a towel over the toilet bowl, close the seat and lid and put something heavy such as a sandbag on the lid. Or use an inflatable pipe plug. Push a strong cloth into floor drains and put something heavy on top. This applies to the lowest points of the house.

## Emergency toilet: the variants

| Variant | When |
| --- | --- |
| Camping toilet or chemical toilet with spare bags or fluid | best choice if you have one |
| Bag in the existing toilet bowl | the toilet is usable, but does not flush or must not be flushed |
| Bucket with bin bag and lid | no usable toilet |
| Urine and faeces kept separate | to limit odour |
| Latrine in the ground | only outside built-up areas, not in the city |

## The bucket method, step by step

1. **Line the toilet bowl or bucket.** Use a bucket with a tight-fitting lid and line it, or the toilet bowl, with two strong bin bags. If the toilet bowl is usable but the sewer pipes are broken, pull the bag over the bowl.
2. **Put absorbent material in the bottom.** Cat litter, sawdust, ash from the fireplace or shredded newspaper.
3. **Cover faeces after each use** with compost litter or sawdust.
4. **Disinfect after each use.** Pour a solution of ten parts water and one part bleach over the contents, to limit odour.
5. **Close the lid** when the toilet is not in use, against insects and animals and to prevent accidents.
6. **Urinate separately if possible.** You may urinate in the toilet, even if you cannot flush it. To limit odour, preferably do not mix urine and faeces.
7. **Toilet paper** goes in a bin or bucket with a lid.
8. **Close the bag.** At the end of each day, tie the bag tightly shut with a tie, put it in a second bag and close it again. Bags with handles are not moisture-proof without extra sealing.
9. **Store the bags** in a sheltered place, such as a garage, cellar or outbuilding, where people cannot reach them and which is protected against rats and vermin. A wheelie bin for residual waste or garden waste protects against rats.
10. **Wash your hands** after using the toilet and after handling the bags. Provide a place next to the emergency toilet where you can wash your hands.

**Location.** Place the emergency toilet far from where you eat or prepare food. Supervise young children who use it.

**Where to dispose of it.** The municipality will announce where you can dispose of the waste. In an emergency, the authorities may announce that the bags can go out with the ordinary waste. So wait for that announcement. Follow it on the radio; the authorities can also inform you with police loudspeaker vans, information boards, leaflets or door-to-door visits.

## Outdoors: latrines

These options are for people outside built-up areas, or for larger groups. In the city, latrines in the ground, burning or burying are not advisable: the risk of vermin and contact with the excrement is too great.

**Where**

- At least 30 metres from surface water such as lakes, rivers and streams.
- At least 30 metres lower than or away from a drinking water source, dwelling or campsite.

**A simple latrine**

1. Dig a hole at least 90 centimetres deep. The bottom must be at least 30 centimetres above the groundwater or a hard layer. If that is not possible, the spot is unsuitable.
2. After each use, throw soil, lime, mulch or ash into the latrine, against odour and against flies, mosquitoes and rodents.
3. Cover the latrine between uses, for example with a board.

**For groups**

For the first days of a disaster with many displaced people, these are the options:

- **Shallow trench latrine.** Trenches about 0.2 to 0.3 metres wide, 1.5 metres deep and 4 metres long, with a screen around them. Users squat over the trench and cover their excrement with soil. Make some trenches narrower for children and older people. Cover everything at least once a day and close the trench when the contents come to within 0.3 metres of the surface.
- **Family latrine.** A simple hole with footrests, a screen and a place to wash hands.
- **Defecation field.** Only as a temporary emergency solution, in strips that you use one per day, starting at the far end. Never on the bank of water that may be used as drinking water, and not on fields with crops.

## Washing your hands

**When**

Always wash your hands with soap and water at these four moments:

1. After using the toilet.
2. Before you prepare food.
3. Before you eat, breastfeed or feed children.
4. After cleaning a child's bottom.

Also wash them before and after caring for someone who is vomiting or has diarrhoea; before and after treating a wound; after changing nappies; after blowing your nose, coughing or sneezing; after touching animals, animal feed or animal waste; after touching rubbish.

**How**

1. Wet your hands with clean running water, turn off the tap and apply soap.
2. Rub well, including the backs of your hands, between your fingers and under your nails.
3. Scrub for at least 20 seconds.
4. Rinse well under clean running water.
5. Dry with a clean towel or let them air dry.

**Without running water**

| Option | When |
| --- | --- |
| Soap and water from a container with a tap | if there is water but no mains supply |
| Hand gel with at least 60 per cent alcohol | if there is no soap and water |
| Wet wipes and disinfecting wipes | to save water |
| Hand-cleaning paste | to save water |

Soap and water remain the best choice in most situations. If you have no soap, ash will also do. Use only safe water for washing your hands; unsafe water does not get your hands clean.

## Washing and cleaning with little water

- Shower less often. For your personal hygiene, use a little water and a flannel.
- Reuse water as much as possible.
- During longer water shortages, use disposable tableware and cutlery, so that you do not have to wash up.
- Use products that need little or no water, such as dry shampoo and hand-cleaning paste.
- Use wet and disinfecting wipes to clean your hands, and kitchen roll or wet cleaning cloths for cleaning.
- Wear household gloves.
- Clean surfaces regularly with a disinfectant, for example a solution of one part liquid bleach to ten parts water.
- For basic hygiene, count on 2 to 6 litres per person per day.

- Keep toothpaste and a toothbrush in your emergency kit and grab bag.

## Waste

Waste quickly becomes a health problem: flies, rats and other scavengers are attracted to it, pools of water near waste let mosquitoes breed, heaps of waste can catch fire, the smoke from burning plastic or chemical waste is harmful, sharp objects are dangerous, and rain washes waste into water sources.

What to do:

- Do not let waste pile up indoors, because of fire and hygiene.
- Store bags containing excrement separately, well sealed, in a place where people cannot reach them and protected against rats and vermin.
- Wait for the municipality's announcement about where and how you can dispose of waste. Without a network, it will come via the radio and the other channels of the authorities.
- Do not burn or bury excrement in the city.

## Preventing diarrhoea

The measures are in this and other guides. In summary:

1. **Wash your hands** at the four set moments, and after caring for someone with diarrhoea.
2. **Safe water.** Drink and cook only with water that is safe or has been treated. See the guide on water.
3. **Keep water clean after treatment.** Store it in clean, covered containers in a cool, dark place, preferably with a tap so that hands and cups do not touch the water.
4. **Safe food.** Throw away what has been left unrefrigerated for too long, what has come into contact with floodwater, and what smells or looks abnormal. See the guide on food without electricity.
5. **Excrement away from water and food.** The emergency toilet far from food and drink, a latrine far from water sources.
6. **Pets.** Clean up their excrement with bags, newspapers and kitchen roll and use bleach for their sanitation needs.

If someone gets diarrhoea: see the first aid pages. If it is severe or lasts a long time, find a doctor. If the network is not working, see Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.

## Per person and per place

- **Flat.** High up in a building, the water may stop during a power cut and the toilet will no longer flush. The bucket or bag method also works in a small home. Store the bags in a cellar or communal area if you do not have a garage.
- **Lowest floor and cellar.** Wastewater can flow back there; seal off the toilet and floor drains as described above.
- **Children.** Supervise them when they use the emergency toilet. For trench latrines, make narrower trenches for children.
- **Older people and people with limited mobility.** For trench latrines, make narrower trenches for older people.
- **Sick people.** Wash your hands before and after caring for someone who is vomiting or has diarrhoea.
