Games with cards, a drawn board or pencil and paper need no electricity and little space. They suit every age, you can play them with two or with ten, and children practise without noticing: counting, planning, waiting their turn, coping with losing. The rules below are the usual basic rules, in our own words. Most games have many house rules. Agree before you start which rules apply.

What you need is given for each game. A pack of cards, a few dice, paper and pencils belong in the emergency kit: they weigh almost nothing.

Source: [UNICEF: Learning through play](https://www.unicef.org/southsudan/media/5371/file/UNICEF-Lego-Foundation-Learning-through-Play-2018.pdf.pdf)

## Making cards if you have none

- **Playing cards.** Cut 52 equal pieces of card, for example from a box. On each card write a value (2 to 10, jack, queen, king, ace) and one of the four suits: hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades. Draw the suit in a corner. The back must be the same on all cards.
- **Pairs cards.** Draw or stick the same picture twice each time on small pieces of card. Start with 8 pairs for nursery children.
- **Die.** Write the numbers 1 to 6 on six slips of paper and draw one blind from a cup. Put the slip back after each turn.
- **Counters and pieces.** Buttons, bottle tops, pebbles, coins or pieces of card in two colours.

## Card games

**Pairs (Memory)** (1 to 6 players, from 3 years)

1. Lay out all the cards face down in rows. With an ordinary pack, use pairs of the same value and colour, for example the two red sevens.
2. The player whose turn it is turns over two cards.
3. If they match, the player takes them and has another go.
4. If they do not match, they turn them back over and it is the next player's turn.
5. Whoever has the most pairs at the end wins.

For nursery children: start with 6 to 8 pairs and play together instead of against each other.

**War** (2 players, from 4 years)

1. Deal out all the cards. Each player keeps their pile face down.
2. Both players turn their top card face up at the same time. The higher card wins both cards. Ace is highest, 2 lowest.
3. If they are equal, it is war: each player lays one card face down and one card face up. The highest face-up card wins everything.
4. Cards you win go under your pile. Whoever has all the cards wins. Feel free to agree a time limit: then whoever has the most cards wins.

This way young children practise comparing numbers.

**Donkey** (3 to 13 players, from 5 years)

1. Take four matching cards for each player, for example four aces, four kings and so on. With five players you therefore use five kinds.
2. Shuffle and give everyone four cards.
3. At the same time, everyone chooses a card they do not want and passes it face down to the left. Pick up the card from the right. Repeat this.
4. Whoever has four matching cards quietly puts a finger on their nose. Whoever sees this does the same.
5. The last person to put a finger on their nose gets a letter: D, O, N, K, E, Y. Whoever is the first to have the whole word DONKEY loses.

**Pesten, a Dutch variant of Crazy Eights** (2 to 8 players, from 6 years)

Aim: to be the first to get rid of all your cards.

1. Give everyone 7 cards. Put the rest face down as the draw pile. Turn over the top card: that is the discard pile.
2. In turn, lay a card on the discard pile of the same suit or the same value as the top card.
3. If you cannot, draw a card. If it fits, you may lay it straight away.
4. Special cards (the usual basic rules):

| Card | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | the next player draws 2 cards, unless they also lay a 2; then the player after that draws 4 |
| 7 | stay on: you lay another card |
| 8 | the next player misses a turn |
| jack | you choose the suit that must be laid; a jack may go on any card |
| ace | the direction of play reverses |
| joker | the next player draws 5 cards |

5. When you have only one card left, say "last card". If you forget and someone notices, you draw 2 cards.
6. When the draw pile runs out, shuffle the discard pile except for the top card and use it as the new draw pile.

**Thirty-one** (3 to 8 players, from 8 years)

Aim: three cards of the same suit with the highest possible total, ideally 31.

1. Use a pack without the 2 to 6 (32 cards). Ace counts 11, king, queen and jack count 10, the other cards their face value.
2. Everyone gets three cards and three lives (chips, matches or tally marks). Three cards lie face up in the middle.
3. In turn, exchange one card for a card from the middle, or all three at once.
4. If you have more than enough, knock on the table. Everyone then gets one more turn.
5. Then everyone shows their cards. You only count the cards of one suit. Three cards of the same value count 30.5.
6. Whoever scores lowest loses a life. Whoever has exactly 31 shows it straight away: then all the others lose a life.
7. Whoever has no lives left is out. The last player wins.

**Hearts** (4 players, from 10 years)

Aim: as few penalty points as possible.

1. Deal out all 52 cards: 13 per player. Ace is highest.
2. Everyone passes three cards to a neighbour. Agree in which direction.
3. Whoever has the 2 of clubs starts. You must follow the suit led if you can. If you cannot, you may lay any card.
4. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick and leads the next one.
5. Penalty points: every heart in your tricks counts 1, the queen of spades counts 13.
6. You may not lead hearts until hearts have been played on another trick.
7. Whoever takes all the hearts and the queen of spades gets 0 points and the others 26 each.
8. The game ends when someone has 100 points. Whoever then has the fewest wins.

**Patience** (1 player, from 7 years)

1. Lay out seven piles side by side: the first with 1 card, the second with 2, up to the seventh with 7. Only the top card of each pile is face up.
2. The rest is the stock. Turn over one card from it at a time.
3. On the seven piles you lay cards in descending order and alternating red and black, for example a black 9 on a red 10. You may also move a face-up sequence.
4. If a face-down card becomes uncovered at the top, turn it over. Only a king may go in an empty space.
5. Each ace goes to its own pile at the top. On it you lay cards of that suit in ascending order: 2, 3, up to the king.
6. You win when all the cards are on the four piles at the top.

## Board games to draw yourself

Draw the board on card, the back of a box, a piece of wallpaper, or outside with chalk or a stick in the sand.

**Game of the Goose** (2 to 6 players, from 4 years)

Board: a spiral or winding track of 63 numbered squares. Draw a goose on squares 5, 9, 14, 18, 23, 27, 32, 36, 41, 45, 50, 54 and 59.

1. In turn, throw two dice and move your counter forward that many squares.
2. If you land on a goose, move forward the same number of squares again.
3. Usual special squares: 6 bridge (go to 12), 19 inn (miss a turn), 31 well (wait until someone else lands there), 42 maze (back to 39), 52 prison (wait until someone else lands there), 58 death (back to the start).
4. You must land exactly on 63. If you throw too many, count back.

You can also choose the squares yourself. Let children draw the board: that is a lesson in counting and drawing at the same time.

**Ludo** (2 to 4 players, from 5 years)

This is the classic game in which you bring four counters round the board to home. It is sold under various names.

Board: a cross with, in each corner, a space for four counters of one colour. A path of 40 squares runs round the cross. From each starting square, a separate row of four squares leads to the centre: that is home.

1. You may only put a counter on the starting square when you throw a 6. After a 6 you throw again.
2. Move a counter forward by the number of dots, clockwise.
3. If you land on a square where another player's counter stands, it goes back to its starting area.
4. After one lap, your counter goes into your own row. You must land exactly in home.
5. The first to get four counters home wins.

**Nine men's morris** (2 players, from 7 years)

Board: three squares one inside the other, connected by four lines in the middle of each side. The 24 intersections are the points. Each player has 9 pieces in their own colour.

1. **Placing.** In turn, place a piece on a free point.
2. **Mill.** Three of your own pieces in a straight line form a mill. You then remove one of the other player's pieces, preferably not from a mill.
3. **Moving.** When all the pieces are placed, take turns to move a piece along a line to a free neighbouring point.
4. **Flying.** When a player has only three pieces left, they may jump to any free point.
5. Whoever has only two pieces left or can no longer move loses.

**Draughts** (2 players, from 7 years)

In Belgium and the Netherlands people play on a board of 10 by 10 squares with 20 pieces per player. Draw the board with alternating dark and light squares. The bottom left square is dark.

1. Each player places 20 pieces on the dark squares of their first four rows. White starts.
2. A piece moves one square diagonally forward to a free dark square.
3. **Capturing.** If one of the other player's pieces is diagonally next to yours with a free square behind it, jump over it and remove it. Capturing is allowed forwards and backwards, and after a capture you may continue capturing with the same piece.
4. **Capturing is compulsory.** If you can capture in more than one way, choose the route with which you capture the most pieces.
5. **King.** When a piece reaches the other player's back row, it becomes a king: put a second piece on top of it. A king moves diagonally along a whole line, as far as you like, and also captures at a distance.
6. Whoever has no pieces left or can no longer move loses.

For younger children: play on a board of 8 by 8 with 12 pieces per player, and leave out the obligation to capture the most pieces.

**Chess** can also be played with home-made pieces. Write a letter on each piece: K (king), Q (queen), R (rook), B (bishop), N (knight), and pawns without a letter. If you know the rules, pass them on to children from about 6 years.

## Pencil-and-paper games

**Noughts and crosses** (2 players, from 4 years). Draw a grid of 3 by 3. In turn, draw a cross or a nought. The first to get three in a row, straight or diagonal, wins.

**Dots and boxes** (2 to 4 players, from 6 years). Draw a grid of dots, for example 6 by 6. In turn, draw a line between two neighbouring dots. Whoever draws the fourth side of a box writes their initial in it and has another go. Whoever has the most boxes wins.

**Hangman** (2 or more players, from 6 years). One player thinks of a word and draws a dash for each letter. The others take turns to guess a letter. If it is in the word, it is written in all the right places. If it is not, draw part of a gallows or, gentler for young children, part of a snowman or a flower. If the drawing is finished before the word is guessed, the player who chose the word wins.

**Battleships** (2 players, from 7 years)

1. Each player draws two grids of 10 by 10, with the letters A to J along the top and the numbers 1 to 10 down the side: one for their own fleet, one for their shots at the other player.
2. Draw your ships hidden in your own grid, in straight lines and not touching each other: 1 ship of 5 squares, 1 of 4, 2 of 3 and 1 of 2.
3. In turn, call out a square, for example C7. The other player says "miss", "hit" or "sunk".
4. The first to sink all the other player's ships wins.

**Categories (name, place, animal)** (2 or more players, from 8 years)

1. Make columns: town, country, river, animal, plant, job, first name, and whatever you choose yourself.
2. Someone silently recites the alphabet, someone else says "stop". The letter reached at that moment applies.
3. Everyone fills in each column as fast as possible with a word beginning with that letter. Whoever is finished calls "stop" and everyone stops.
4. Points: 10 for a word that nobody else has, 5 for a word that someone else also has, 0 for nothing.

**Other games without materials or with a pencil**

- **I spy** (from 3 years): guess the object from a colour or a letter.
- **Who am I?** (from 6 years): someone thinks of a person or an animal. The others ask questions that are answered yes or no.
- **Word chain** (from 7 years): each word begins with the last letter of the previous word.
- **Draw and guess** (from 5 years): one player draws a word without letters or numbers, the others guess.
- **Word guess on paper** (from 9 years): guess a five-letter word. After each attempt, the player who chose the word says which letters are in the right place and which are in the word but in another place.

## Dice games

- **To a hundred** (from 6 years, 2 or more players). Throw one die as often as you like and add up the dots. If you throw a 1, you lose the points of that turn and it is the next player's turn. If you stop yourself, write down your points. The first to reach 100 wins.
- **Arithmetic with dice** (from 6 years). Throw two or three dice and add, subtract or multiply. Whoever says the right answer first gets a point.

## Keeping it enjoyable

- Choose a game that suits the youngest player, or play in small teams of an older and a younger child.
- Explain the rules with a practice round without a winner.
- Agree that arguments about rules are settled by one set person, or by following the rules here.
- Alternate games with a winner and games in which you play together against the game, for example laying out a patience together.
- Keep all the cards, counters and dice in a dedicated box or bag. That way nothing gets lost.
