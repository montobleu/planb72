Evacuating means that you have to leave the building or area where you are because of danger. This has to happen quickly and you do not have much time for it. That is why preparation is important. This guide follows the order: beforehand, the decision to leave, the departure, on the way, the reception centre and the return.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Evacuating](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/evacuate)

## When to evacuate and when not to

**Go outside immediately, without waiting for instructions**

- In an emergency in your own home or workplace, such as a fire: if you see smoke or fire or hear the smoke alarm or fire alarm, go outside as quickly as possible via a safe escape route.
- If you are in immediate danger.

**When ordered by the authorities or the emergency services**

- In a larger emergency in your area, such as a flood, fire, gas leak or accident with hazardous substances, a whole street or neighbourhood may have to evacuate.
- In that case, never evacuate on your own initiative. Wait for specific instructions from the authorities or emergency services. Sometimes it is safer to shelter than to go outside.
- Always follow the instructions of the emergency services and the authorities first: mayor, governor.

**How you hear about it, even without a network**

- Listen to a battery radio, a wind-up radio or the car radio.
- The police may drive through the street with loudspeaker vans.
- The authorities may also go door to door, or use information boards and leaflets.
- Follow the instructions of the emergency services on site.

**If the network works,** instructions also come via BE-Alert, TV, official websites and social media. During an evacuation the authorities can send a text message to everyone in the zone, including those who are not registered with BE-Alert.

If you are instructed to leave, leave early enough not to be trapped by severe weather.

## Preparing in advance

- Make an emergency plan for your home. Make sure everyone knows the quickest and easiest way out.
- Make arrangements with family or friends about where you can go if you can no longer stay in your home or neighbourhood.
- Agree with your family where you will meet if you cannot reach each other, for example because the phone and internet are not working.
- Make sure everyone in the household knows the contact persons, with address and phone number, and keep a list on paper.
- Think about what to do with your pets, and about their transport and care.
- Pay extra attention to family members with specific needs. If you need help to evacuate, make arrangements with people around you.
- Hang the house keys by the door. Always keep them in the same place.
- Divide the tasks, for example who takes the children.
- Keep an emergency kit for evacuation ready in a central, easily accessible place.
- Practise the plan regularly. Finding the way out in thick smoke is not easy, so practise it blindfolded too.
- Know several places you can go to, for example to friends in another municipality, in different directions.
- Know alternative routes and other means of transport to leave your area.
- Know that, depending on the disaster, your evacuation route may be on foot.

## What to take

**With a grab bag:** take the rucksack with your emergency kit. The contents are in the guide on the emergency kit and grab bag.

**Without a grab bag:** take at least this:

- Identity card.
- Money.
- A few bottles of water.
- Mobile phone and charger.
- Keys.
- A list of important telephone numbers, on paper.
- Important medicines.
- A small bag with hygiene products.

In a flood, also spare clothing and boots.

**Clothing.** Wear sturdy shoes and clothing that gives some protection: long trousers, long sleeves, a hat.

**Radio and map.** Take a battery radio or a wind-up radio, and a paper map of your area or town.

**In immediate danger**, do not waste time gathering documents or belongings. Evacuate as quickly as possible.

Make a list in advance of what you will take if you have to leave quickly and cannot return for a while.

## Leaving your home

**Check before you leave**

1. Switch off electrical appliances, such as the coffee maker, oven and iron. The fridge and freezer may stay switched on.
2. Close windows and doors.
3. If you have an alarm system, switch it on.
4. Shut off water and gas.

**If you cannot do this,** leave the home anyway as the authorities ask. On your return, you will receive support from the competent services if needed.

**In a flood**, shut off gas, electricity and water if necessary, and in any case the power to the rooms that may flood. Park your car in a safe place, not in the garage.

**A note.** Leave a note stating when you left and where you are going. That way others know where you have gone.

During the evacuation, the local police take security measures in the evacuation zone.

## Where to go

There are two options:

| Destination | What you need to know |
| --- | --- |
| Family or friends outside the affected zone | ask in advance whether they are willing to take you in; agree whether your pets may come |
| A reception centre, for example a sports hall or function hall | in larger emergencies the authorities (municipality, province) provide a reception site; the emergency services give you the information |

You receive information about where to go via the official communication channels. Without a network, these are the radio, police loudspeaker vans and the emergency services on site (see above). Choose destinations in different directions in advance, so that you have options if a road is closed.

**Your municipality's reception.** Every municipality must set out in its general emergency and intervention plan where the reception and housing centres are and who arranges transport. During a prolonged power cut, mayors can also open an information and reception point for the population. Ask your municipality in advance what arrangements exist. The map in the app (Nearest button) shows the town halls.

People who cannot evacuate by themselves receive help, and on arrival at a safe place also temporary accommodation and food.

## How to travel

**On foot.** Usually you evacuate on foot. Have a grab bag that you can carry, also if you leave by public transport.

**By bus.** You may be able to take a bus to the reception centre. The emergency services or your municipality will let you know.

**By car.** Over a long distance it can be done with your own vehicle.

- Fill up the tank when an evacuation becomes likely. Petrol stations may be closed in an emergency and cannot pump fuel during a power cut.
- Follow the recommended evacuation routes. Do not take back roads: they may be blocked.
- Take water and non-perishable food for the journey.
- Watch out for hazards such as washed-out roads or bridges and fallen power lines.
- Do not drive through flooded areas.
- Stay away from fallen electricity cables; they may carry a deadly voltage.
- Give neighbours a lift to the reception site if they need one, especially those who are less mobile or have a smaller social safety net.

**Without a car.** Think in advance about how you will leave. Discuss it with family, friends or the municipality to see what help is available. For those who need adapted transport: plan it in advance with transport companies and the municipality.

## Informing and finding family

**Without a network**

- Go to the meeting place you agreed with your family in advance.
- Leave a note at home saying when you left and where you are going.
- At the reception centre, the contact person on site gives information.

**If the network works**

- As soon as you are safe, contact your family. Say where you are going and when you expect to arrive.
- In a large-scale emergency, preferably use text messages or messaging apps. Mass calling can overload the network.
- Let your contact person outside the zone know where you are, and whether family members have become separated.

**If you have lost track of someone**

Rode Kruis-Vlaanderen has a Restoring Family Links service that traces missing persons and restores contact between family members, including after a natural disaster. You can reach the service via a form, email or phone, so only if the network works. The service does not deal with matters that fall under the police. The service recommends:

- Learning the phone numbers of family members by heart.
- Having children memorise important information, such as the names of their parents.
- Sharing your planned route with family and friends and reporting where you are at fixed times.
- Contacting the local Red Cross immediately if you lose contact with your family.

## Children

- Agree in advance who takes the children.
- Teach children the steps of the plan, with extra attention to the emergency numbers and a safe escape route.
- Keep children with their parents or caregivers and do not let them become separated. If a parent is taken away for medical care, try to let the children go with them, or note where the parent is being taken.
- If you find a child without parents, do not leave the child alone and put them in touch with a reliable child protection organisation or the emergency services. Do not try to reunite them on your own.
- Let a child take a cuddly toy or small toy of their own choosing.

How to reassure children is explained in the guide on mental resilience.

## Older people and people with a disability

- If you have difficulty walking: practise now and then whether you can leave the house quickly, even if the lift does not work or may not be used. Think about who can help you and how to contact them.
- Think of alternative ways to communicate with others, even if you have no phone or internet. Agree on this in advance with the people who help you.
- If you use a wheelchair: look at which way you can evacuate if the lift does not work. Think about a wheelchair-accessible reception site.
- Blind or partially sighted: keep your aids, such as a cane, in a place where you can find them quickly.
- Have someone register your address in BE-Alert, so that this person is also warned.
- Build a support network and agree who will help you leave the building.
- Think about how you will take your aids with you in an evacuation, or how you will replace them if they are lost.
- With dementia: do not leave the person alone; even someone who does not normally wander off may do so in an unfamiliar environment. Take a pillow, blanket or other familiar object. In the shelter, find a quiet corner away from the exits. If the person becomes agitated, respond to the emotion: for example, say that you understand the person is afraid and wants to go home, and that you are there with them.
- For those who have difficulty in an unfamiliar, busy environment: sheets and rope or a small pop-up tent to limit stimuli, and noise-cancelling headphones.

**The Crisis Center asks neighbours**, when a whole street, neighbourhood or block of flats is evacuated, to check whether others need help, especially those who are less mobile.

## Pets

**Can they come**

- You may be allowed to take small pets with you. The emergency services and the authorities say whether pets are allowed in the reception centre. Sometimes they are not.
- If you go to family or friends, agree in advance whether your animals may come, and think about transport: travel bag, small cage.
- If you have to evacuate, take your animal with you if you can. If it is not safe for you, it is not safe for your animal either. Do not leave it behind, and certainly not shut in.

**If your animal cannot or may not come**

1. Let the emergency services know that there are animals in your home. Say where the animal often hides or how it reacts to strangers.
2. Put out enough water and food for 24 hours.
3. Lay newspapers on the floor for droppings.
4. Leave an item of clothing with your scent to reassure your animal.

If you still cannot go home after 24 hours, you will usually receive adapted recommendations for your pets.

**In advance.** Get your animal used to the travel basket: leave it open in places where the animal often goes, add treats and put a familiar blanket in it. Note where your animals hide, so that you can find them quickly. Put a sticker on a window or door stating which pets you have and how many.

More in the guide on animals.

## Livestock and horses

For horses, goats, pigs and other livestock:

- Make sure all animals can be identified, large animals with a microchip or ear tag.
- Evacuate animals earlier where possible. Set a main route and a back-up route in advance.
- Arrange vehicles and trailers for each type of animal, and experienced handlers and drivers.
- Make sure the destination has feed, water, a vet and equipment.
- If evacuation is not possible, the owner must decide whether to put large animals in a barn or turn them loose outside.

In a nuclear accident in Belgium, the evacuation of livestock is only considered if the population is evacuated, there are too few stables in the zone and the zone is small enough. It only takes place after the population has been protected. See the guide on animals.

## At the reception centre

- At the reception centre, the contact person on site gives the necessary information, including about when you may go back home.
- Municipalities, provinces or the Crisis Center can open an information number to handle questions from residents. They can use the national information number 1771 for this, which can be operational within the hour, day and night. It can also handle sensitive calls about possible victims. It is only opened in an emergency and only helps if the network works.
- After a disaster, people often need: shelter, food, water and sanitation; care for injuries or chronic illnesses; understandable and accurate information; contact with loved ones; support suited to their culture or religion; and being involved in important decisions.

## How long, and returning

**How long.** It depends on the situation. You can go home as soon as it is safe. That may be after only a few hours, but it may also take several days.

**How you know you may return**

- At the reception centre: via the contact person on site.
- With family or friends: listen to the radio. If the network works, also check the municipality's website or call the information number.

**When returning**

- Ask the authorities both where you are staying and at home whether you can travel.
- Returning before debris has been cleared is dangerous.
- Let family and friends know when you are leaving and when you arrive.
- Charge your devices and have spare batteries, in case the power cut continues.
- Fill up the tank and take water and food for the journey.
- Stay away from fallen power lines and report them to the grid operator.
- Only use a generator outdoors and never connect it to your home's electrical installation.

**After a flood**

- Clean the house and disinfect the damaged rooms.
- Ventilate enough and also heat, so that the moisture can escape.
- Listen to the recommendations of the authorities about tap water.
- Take photos of the damage for your insurance and contact your insurer as soon as possible.
- Find out whether the Disaster Fund (Rampenfonds) will contribute.

## Evacuating together

If you have to evacuate with a whole street, neighbourhood or block of flats, the Crisis Center asks you to help others:

- Give a lift to the reception site.
- Help warn others that they have to evacuate. If the network works, preferably do so by text message, so that the network stays free for important calls.
- Look out especially for neighbours who are less mobile or have a smaller social safety net.
- Make an evacuation plan for the building, street or neighbourhood together with other residents.
- Make yourself available as an emergency contact for friends and family, in case they have to evacuate.

Help, but never put yourself in danger.

## Departure checklist

- [ ] Instruction from the authorities or immediate danger.
- [ ] Grab bag, or the minimum: identity card, money, water, mobile phone and charger, keys, phone list on paper, medicines, hygiene.
- [ ] Battery radio and a map of the area.
- [ ] Sturdy shoes, long clothing; in a flood, boots and spare clothing.
- [ ] Electrical appliances off; fridge and freezer on.
- [ ] Windows and doors closed, alarm on.
- [ ] Water and gas shut off, if possible.
- [ ] Pets in the travel basket, or water and food for 24 hours and emergency services informed.
- [ ] Note: when you left, where to.
- [ ] Neighbours who need help informed.
- [ ] Meeting place with the family known.
- [ ] Contact person outside the zone informed if the network works, preferably by text message.
