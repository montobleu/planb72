According to the Crisis Center, a power cut knocks out a great deal at once: lighting, fridge and freezer, electric cookers, TV and computer, central heating boilers and circulation pumps, lifts, automatic doors and garage doors, cash machines and petrol stations, and trains, trams and metros. The telephone network can also be disrupted: expect calls and the internet to stop working after a short time (see The phone: charge it, but do not rely on it). This guide is about light and about power for the appliances you really need.

Ready.gov recommends making a list in advance of everything you need that runs on electricity, and planning for each appliance with batteries or another source.

## Using candles safely

Candles are on every emergency list, but they are also the light source with the most risk.

1. Put candles in a candle holder and place it on a fireproof surface (OFF ON).
2. Never leave them burning unattended (OFF ON).
3. Put them out before you go to sleep (OFF ON).
4. Ventilate regularly, because candles consume oxygen (BBK).
5. Make sure you have a working smoke alarm (BBK) and keep something to put out a fire to hand: fire extinguisher, garden hose or fire blanket (BBK, Stromausfall).
6. Keep candles and matches or a lighter in a safe and dry place (Denk vooruit).

The BBK points out that during a power cut the fire risk increases through incorrect or unattended use of gas appliances, candles and the like.

## Batteries and power bank

- Have rechargeable batteries and spare batteries (Crisis Center brochure).
- Keep a charged power bank ready, in your emergency kit and in your grab bag (Crisis Center, Denk vooruit).
- Make sure the batteries of laptops and phones are charged, or keep charged spare batteries ready (BBK).
- If you know an interruption is coming, charge your mobile and make sure you have a full portable charger (Crisis Center).
- For an electric wheelchair or other battery-powered aid: buy an extra battery and always keep it charged. If you cannot buy one, ask which organisations can help (Ready.gov).
- Buy spare chargers for electronic devices and keep them charged (Ready.gov).

## The car as a charger and as a radio

- The MCF recommends a mobile charger for the car, and a car with fuel in the tank or a charged battery.
- The BBK and the OFF ON brochure mention the car radio as a way to follow the news.
- The BBK: a car with a full tank keeps you mobile if the fuel supply fails. During a power cut, petrol stations may be out of service (OFF ON).
- The Poison Centre: car exhaust fumes contain CO and are a risk in a small space such as a garage. So do not let the engine run in an enclosed or partly enclosed space.
- The Crisis Center recommends limiting car journeys, because traffic lights may be out of order.

## Using a generator safely

**Before you buy one (BBK)**

- Think about which appliances you want to run. Anything that produces heat, such as kettles, heaters and hobs, uses a lot of power: easily 1,500 to 2,000 watts per appliance.
- You can only connect sensitive appliances such as a computer or the heating controls if the generator keeps the voltage and frequency stable enough. Otherwise they will be damaged.
- Choose a quiet model. In densely populated neighbourhoods and blocks of flats, the noise can disturb neighbours.
- Take into account the rules for storing fuel. For Germany, the BBK gives a maximum of 10 kilograms of petrol or 20 kilograms of diesel in suitable jerrycans in the cellar, and no gas in the cellar. For Belgium, the sources consulted give no quantities.
- Get advice from a specialist dealer or a qualified electrician.

**When using it (BBK and Ready.gov)**

1. Always outdoors. Never in the home, the garage or the cellar. The BBK: there is a risk of suffocation from the exhaust fumes. Ready.gov: at least 20 feet (about 6 metres) from windows, doors and an attached garage.
2. In a dry, well-ventilated place. Keep the generator dry and protected from rain and flooding. Touching a wet generator or the appliances connected to it can give an electric shock (Ready.gov).
3. Connect appliances with heavy-duty extension cables (Ready.gov). For the cables you need an opening in the building; a door or window left open lets the home cool down and makes burglary easier (BBK).
4. Let the generator cool down before you refuel. Fuel spilled on hot engine parts can catch fire (Ready.gov, BBK).
5. Fit CO alarms on every floor (Ready.gov).
6. Follow the manufacturer's instructions (Ready.gov).

**Never connect it to the house wiring yourself**

- Ready.gov: never connect a generator to the electrical installation of your home.
- The BBK: if you want to feed the house installation with a generator, always get advice from a qualified electrician. Work on the house installation may only be carried out by a qualified electrical contractor, with a manual or automatic changeover switch. Also inform your grid operator and your supplier. In a block of flats, inform the owner.

## The phone: charge it, but do not rely on it

**What you need to know**

- Mobile masts can run for one to two hours without electricity. Mobile internet (4G and 5G) drops out quickly, probably immediately (FPS Economy).
- Most landlines stop working without power, because the modem or line equipment needs power. A traditional telephone line with a handset that has no power supply of its own keeps working for about four hours (FPS Economy). With internet telephony, the router, fibre connection and handset all need power (BBK).
- Even if you have power yourself, the mobile network can go down because the mast is in an area without power. The reverse can also happen (FPS Economy).
- Mobile networks have only partial and short-lived emergency power, and quickly become overloaded (BBK).
- When the power comes back, it can take several hours before you can make mobile calls again (FPS Economy).
- Ready.gov recommends checking in advance whether your landline works during a power cut and how long its back-up battery lasts.

**Charge it anyway**

- Charge your mobile, but bear in mind that the mobile and landline networks may be disrupted (OFF ON). Keep your mobile charged so that you can be reached for as long as there is a signal (Red Cross Flanders).
- Turn down the screen brightness and close apps (Denk vooruit).
- Always keep one phone in the house switched on so that you can receive important messages for as long as the network works (Denk vooruit; in Belgium this means BE-Alert).
- Keep your mobile ready to receive at night too if you are affected (BBK).

**While the network works: save it**

- Only call 112 in immediate danger and limit your phone use, so as not to overload the network (Red Cross Flanders).
- Only make calls when really necessary and preferably use text messages (OFF ON).
- In a major emergency, use messaging apps instead of calling (Crisis Center brochure). They need mobile internet, and that drops out quickly during a power cut (FPS Economy).

**When calls and the internet no longer work**

- Follow the news on a battery radio, a wind-up radio or the car radio (OFF ON, FPS Economy).
- The authorities can also inform you with police loudspeaker vans, information boards, leaflets and door-to-door visits (Crisis Center).
- If you can no longer call, ask for help in your neighbourhood. During load shedding, municipalities can open an information and reception point; ask your municipality in advance what it has arranged (FPS Economy).
- If you need to reach the emergency services: see Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call. The map in the app (Nearest button) shows fire stations, police, hospitals and town halls.

## During the power cut

1. Unplug sensitive electrical appliances, such as the TV, computer, electric bikes and cars, to prevent damage from a power surge when the power comes back (Crisis Center, OFF ON).
2. Switch off electric heating appliances: cooker, oven, iron, coffee maker, kettle, electric blanket, fan heater (BBK).
3. For the same reason, turn the central heating thermostat down as low as possible (OFF ON).
4. Leave one switch on, so that you know when the power is back (OFF ON).
5. Use torches instead of candles (Crisis Center).
6. Follow the news on a battery radio or the car radio (OFF ON).
7. Keep the fridge and freezer closed (see the guide on food without electricity).
8. Check with vulnerable relatives and neighbours whether everything is all right (OFF ON). Look out for neighbours or family who need extra help (Red Cross Flanders).
9. Get together with neighbours or family to share light and warmth. Agree in advance whose home you can go to and which supplies you will share (Crisis Center, emergency kit).

A power cut in itself is not an emergency: do not call the emergency numbers to ask for information, as that overloads the emergency call centres (BBK). Only call 112 in immediate danger (Red Cross Flanders). If you cannot get through, see Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call.

## When the electricity comes back

- Do not switch all electrical appliances on at the same time. Wait until the grid has recovered, to avoid overloading it (Crisis Center, OFF ON).
- Top up your emergency kit after use (OFF ON).

## Saving electricity during shortages

According to the Crisis Center, during electricity shortages municipalities can be cut off from the power for a while to prevent a general blackout (the load-shedding plan). You can help reduce the risk:

- Turn off unnecessary lighting. Leave a room, switch off the light.
- Turn thermostats down.
- Switch off appliances on standby.
- Only use the iron, washing machine, tumble dryer, electric cooker, microwave, dishwasher and vacuum cleaner when necessary and preferably after 22:00.
- Use as few hobs as possible and the microwave instead of the ordinary oven.
- Register with BE-Alert in advance, so that you know in time that your municipality is going to be cut off.
- The government announces the day before which zones may be cut off. That information is also given on TV, radio and in the press (FPS Economy).

## People who depend on electricity

- If you use appliances that run on electricity, such as an oxygen machine or an electric wheelchair, discuss with your care providers what to do in a power failure (Crisis Center).
- Discuss with your doctor a plan for electrically powered medical devices and for refrigerated medicines (Ready.gov).
- Ask your energy supplier whether you can be put on a list for priority when power is restored (Ready.gov).
- In home nursing, life-sustaining devices such as ventilators can fail, and services such as personal alarms and meals on wheels can stop. Ask your care service whether and how those services continue during a long power cut. If a family member lives in a residential care home, ask about the emergency power plan (BBK).
- If you use oxygen equipment, pump systems, home dialysis or another device that runs on electricity at home: do not wait for an interruption. Find out how long the fully charged internal battery of your device lasts, and look into where else you could be looked after (FPS Economy).
- If you use the device intermittently, as with home dialysis, make sure it is not connected at the time of an announced load-shedding cut (FPS Economy).

What to do if you cannot call your care provider is explained in the guide on medication and care.

## Electric doors, gates and lifts

- Know how to unlock electronic gates, roller shutters or doors during a power cut (Crisis Center, plan at home).
- Without electricity you cannot use the lift (Crisis Center). People who have difficulty walking should practise whether they can leave the house without the lift and agree who will help (Crisis Center, plan for people who need extra help).

## Checklist

- [ ] A torch per person, preferably wind-up, or with spare batteries.
- [ ] A head torch.
- [ ] A radio on batteries, crank or solar panel, preferably with DAB+ and FM.
- [ ] Rechargeable and spare batteries.
- [ ] A charged power bank at home and in the grab bag.
- [ ] A mobile charger for the car.
- [ ] Candles or tea lights, candle holders, matches or a lighter, kept dry.
- [ ] Smoke alarm and CO alarm, tested.
- [ ] Fire extinguisher or fire blanket.
- [ ] Knowing how to open gates and roller shutters without electricity.
- [ ] A plan for every electrical appliance someone depends on.
- [ ] A paper list of important telephone numbers.
- [ ] Arrangements with your neighbours: how you will reach each other without phone or internet, and whose home you will meet at to share light and warmth.
