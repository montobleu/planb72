Include your animals in your emergency plan and your emergency kit. People who have a plan in advance for themselves and their animals have less trouble, stress and worry when decisions have to be made quickly. In a crisis, you are responsible for the care and welfare of your animal.

Source: [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf) · [Ready.gov: Pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets) · [MCF: In case of crisis or war](https://rib.msb.se/filer/pdf/31523.pdf)

## The plan for your animals

1. **Emergency kit.** Have enough supplies for your animals (see below).
2. **A place to stay.** Think about where you can go if you can no longer stay in your home or neighbourhood, for example with friends or acquaintances. Ask whether there is also room for your animal.
3. **Identification.** Make sure your animal can be identified, preferably with a microchip. Keep your name and address up to date in the online registration (CatID, DogID).
4. **Vaccination.** Make sure your animal is vaccinated and put a copy of the vaccination booklet in your emergency kit.
5. **Transport.** Plan how you will transport your animal safely, with a carrier or bag, and get it used to it.
6. **Contact persons.** Put your vet's telephone number with your important numbers. Calling only works if the network works.

Also:

- **A buddy.** Agree with neighbours, friends or family that someone will look after your animals or evacuate them if you cannot.
- **A place outside the neighbourhood.** Ask family or friends outside your immediate area whether they can help with your animals.
- **Contact in the microchip.** When registering, also give an emergency contact outside your immediate area.
- **A sticker** on a window or front door saying which and how many pets you have.
- **Why vaccination.** If during an evacuation you cannot prove that your animal is vaccinated, it may be refused and placed in quarantine, especially with diseases such as rabies.
- **No guaranteed place in an animal shelter.** Animal shelters want to help but often have hardly any room; arrange it yourself.
- **On paper.** Print important telephone numbers, addresses and routes, so that you have them during power and internet outages. Also note your vet's address. The map in the app (Nearest button) shows vets.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan for animals](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/animals) · [Ready.gov: Pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets) · [Diereninrampen.nl: Save your animal in a disaster](https://www.diereninrampen.nl/red-je-dier-bij-een-ramp)

## Supplies at home

| What | How much |
| --- | --- |
| Food and water, with bowls | for at least 72 hours, preferably a week; dry food and tinned pet food in an airtight, waterproof container |
| Drinking water | about 50 ml per kilogram of body weight per day |
| Medicines | an extra supply of what your animal takes regularly, in a waterproof container |
| First aid kit for the animal | discuss with your vet what should go in it |
| Litter tray and litter, newspapers, kitchen paper, bin bags, household bleach | for sanitary needs |
| Grooming products | shampoo and the like, in case your animal needs to be washed |

Make two kits: a larger one for when you shelter at home and a light one for when you have to evacuate. Check them twice a year, especially food and medicines. When storing water for the household, think of your pets too.

## The bag to take with you

- A carrier or sturdy transport cage for each animal, or something else to transport your animal.
- Lead and collar or harness, and a spare lead, collar and name tag.
- Pet food, water and food and water bowls.
- Pet passport, vaccination booklet and your vet's contact details.
- Copies of registration and other documents in a waterproof container and digitally.
- A photo of you together with your animal.
- Toys and familiar items such as a blanket or treats, to reduce stress.
- Medication, if your animal needs it, or a medication list.
- Bags to clear up droppings.
- For exotic animals: a safe means of transport and the necessary equipment.

Why a photo together with your animal: if you become separated, it helps to show that it is your animal and lets others help to recognise it.

Source: [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf) · [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-home) · [Ready.gov: Pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets)

## Getting used to the carrier

- Leave the carrier open in places where your animal often goes.
- Give treats near the carrier and put a few inside.
- Put a familiar blanket or small bed in the carrier, so that it smells familiar.
- Note where your animals hide, so that you can find them quickly if you have to leave.

Source: [Ready.gov: Pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets)

## Evacuating with your animal

**The rule.** If you have to evacuate, take your animal with you. If you leave your animals behind, they can get lost, be injured or worse. If it is not safe for you, it is not safe for your animal either. Do not leave it behind, and certainly not shut in.

**Where your animal may come with you**

| Destination | Animals |
| --- | --- |
| Family or friends | agree in advance whether your animals may come |
| Government reception centre | small pets are sometimes allowed; the emergency services will tell you; sometimes it is not allowed |

**If your animal cannot or may not come with you**

1. Let the emergency services know that there are one or more animals in your home. A sticker on a window or front door shows which and how many animals there are (see The plan for your animals).
2. Tell them where the animal often hides and how it usually reacts to strangers.
3. Put out enough water and food for 24 hours.
4. Put newspapers on the floor for droppings.
5. Leave behind an item of clothing with your scent to reassure the animal.

If you still cannot go home after 24 hours, you will usually get adapted recommendations for your pets. If your animal can get to food by itself, leave extra food and water.

## Assistance animals and guide dogs

A guide dog or other assistance animal needs the same tips as other animals. Include in its kit enough food, extra water, a collar with a name tag, its medical records and other supplies.

## Aquarium fish

**At home, during a power cut**

- If the filter is not working, halve how often you feed.
- If you think you may have to evacuate, stop feeding.
- On cold days, keep the aquarium warm by insulating it with a blanket or winter coat. On hot days, point a fan at the aquarium.
- Provide oxygen with a battery-powered air pump or an oxidator.
- If necessary, divide the fish between two tanks, so that the water quality stays good for longer.
- Keep water to hand to change 10 to 30 per cent of the aquarium water.

**In an evacuation**

- Transport small fish in a plastic bag with about one third water and two thirds air.
- Larger fish or groups in a bucket or plastic container with a lid.
- Put bags or containers in a polystyrene box or cool box, so that the temperature stays stable.
- Do not feed during the evacuation; this keeps the water clean for longer.
- Use a battery-powered air pump or an oxidator. Do not use plants as a source of oxygen: in the dark they consume oxygen.
- Think in advance about where you can go with your fish, for example to someone from your aquarium club.

Source: [Diereninrampen.nl: Save your animal in a disaster](https://www.diereninrampen.nl/red-je-dier-bij-een-ramp)

## Reptiles

**At home**

- If you can keep the temperature in the terrarium at the right level, you can usually go on feeding as normal. If that is not possible or if you may have to evacuate, stop feeding for the time being. Reptiles can often go a long time without food, but they do need clean drinking water.
- Leave your reptile in its own terrarium; that causes the least stress.
- Focus mainly on the correct temperature. Heat packs can help, but make sure the animal cannot burn itself and has a cooler spot. UV lighting and humidity are temporarily less important than temperature.

**In an evacuation**

- Use a sturdy transport box with ventilation and a tight-fitting lid. You can transport a snake in a pillowcase, but always inside a closed transport box.
- Put the transport box in a polystyrene box with a heat pack at one end and a hiding place at the other, so that the animal can choose a comfortable temperature itself.
- Do not feed during the evacuation. Take enough clean water with you, but do not put a water bowl in the transport box.
- Do not transport a terrapin or other aquatic reptile in water. After arrival, provide water and a dry spot.
- Extra items: a spray bottle for species that do not drink from a bowl, a floating platform for aquatic reptiles, a pillowcase and possibly a snake hook.
- Take CITES documents with you if your reptile needs them, on paper and digitally.

**Hygiene.** Disinfect your hands after contact with your reptile and keep the transport enclosure clean. In general, wash your hands after touching animals, animal food or animal droppings.

Source: [Diereninrampen.nl: Save your animal in a disaster](https://www.diereninrampen.nl/red-je-dier-bij-een-ramp) · [CDC: About Handwashing](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/about/index.html)

## Cold, heat and dirty water

- **Cold.** Keep your pets indoors or make sure they have enough food and drinking water.
- **Storm or disaster.** Bring pets indoors at the first sign or the first warning.
- **Heat.** Outdoors, provide plenty of cool water and shade. Asphalt and dark paving can be too hot for paws. Never leave animals behind in a parked vehicle.
- **Dirty water.** During a prolonged power cut, do not let your pets drink water from ditches or swim in them. Pets that go outside can drink rainwater from a water butt.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Cold wave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/cold-wave) · [National Crisis Center: Heatwave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/heatwave) · [Ready.gov: Pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets) · [Ready.gov: Extreme Heat](https://www.ready.gov/heat) · [Waterschap Zuiderzeeland: leaflet](https://bijlagen.nos.nl/artikel-24778431/260226_digitaal_toegankelijke_versie_folder_noodsituatie_Waterschap_Zuiderzeeland.pdf)

## Horses and livestock

**Preparing**

- Make sure all your animals can be identified, with a microchip or ear tag.
- Evacuate animals early where possible. Set out a main and a back-up route in advance.
- Arrange vehicles and trailers for each type of animal, and experienced handlers and drivers.
- Make sure the destination has food, water, a vet and equipment.
- If evacuation is not possible, you must decide whether to put large animals in a shed or stable or turn them loose outside.
- Always follow the warnings and guidelines of the authorities. In some emergencies there are extra instructions, such as a grazing ban for cows after a radiological accident.

**Water for animals.** Allow per day:

| Animal | Water per day |
| --- | --- |
| Cattle, horses, mules | 20 to 30 litres per animal |
| Goats, sheep, pigs | 10 to 20 litres per animal |
| Chickens | 10 to 20 litres per 100 animals |

In Belgium, the FASFC and the FANC are involved in measures for the food chain.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan for animals](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/animals) · [Ready.gov: Pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets) · [WHO/WEDC: Technical notes](https://wedc-knowledge.lboro.ac.uk/resources/who_notes/WHO_TNE_ALL.pdf) · [National Crisis Center: Nuclear emergency plan](https://crisiscentrum.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/20240616_Nucleair%20Noodplan.pdf)

## Animals in a nuclear or radiological incident

**What the Belgian nuclear emergency plan provides for**

The plan lists measures to prevent or limit radioactive contamination of foodstuffs:

- A grazing ban.
- Housing animals safely with uncontaminated feed and uncontaminated water.
- The evacuation of livestock to uncontaminated zones.
- Disconnecting the rainwater supply, and a ban on watering with water from the water butt or irrigating with surface water.
- A ban on slaughtering, hunting and harvesting.
- Adding additives to animal feed, to limit the uptake of radioactive substances by animals.
- Restrictions on the movement of products and animals to and from the controlled zone.

These measures are often already justified at lower releases than those at which the population itself has to be protected urgently. The evacuation of livestock is only considered if the population is being evacuated, there are too few sheds in the zone and the zone is small enough, and only after the population has been protected. The measures are worked out in consultation with the FANC and the FASFC, and the sector organisations are involved.

**What a neighbouring country specifically asks.** The Luxembourg crisis portal gives farmers these instructions in a nuclear alert:

- Bring livestock into sheds where possible, with priority for dairy cows.
- Limit the natural and artificial ventilation of those sheds as much as possible.
- Feed with stocks of hay and silage, and keep the feed indoors.
- Bring raw materials, grain and animal feed under cover as soon as the alarm sounds.
- Cover silage and plants that are outside with a plastic sheet.

In Belgium, follow the instructions of the Belgian authorities. More about the nuclear measures for people can be found in the nuclear guides.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Nuclear emergency plan](https://crisiscentrum.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/20240616_Nucleair%20Noodplan.pdf) · [National Crisis Center: A plan for animals](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/animals) · [Infocrise Luxembourg: Protective measures for farmers and businesses](https://infocrise.public.lu/en/support/faq/urgence-nucleaire/mesures-protection-agriculteurs-commerces.html)

## Animals in a chemical incident or a toxic cloud

No separate guidelines for animals in a chemical incident were found in the sources consulted. What does apply:

- If you have to shelter, the Crisis Center asks you to stay indoors, close windows and doors and switch off ventilation. Bring pets indoors at the first sign of a disaster.
- Follow the instructions of the authorities; sometimes there are special guidelines for animals.

See the scenario card on a chemical cloud.

- [ ] Vaccinations in order, copy of the booklet in the kit.

- [ ] A buddy who can look after your animal when you are not there.
- [ ] Food and water for at least 72 hours, preferably a week.
- [ ] Medicines and a first aid kit for the animal.
- [ ] A carrier or transport cage for each animal, and the animal used to it.
- [ ] A photo of you with your animal.

- [ ] For livestock and horses: identification, routes, transport and a destination.
