An emergency plan is a set of arrangements you make in advance with the people around you: how you reach or find each other, who does what and who needs extra help. The National Crisis Center explains why: in an emergency you sometimes have to rely on yourself for a while. The emergency services help as quickly as possible, but they have to set priorities.

The Crisis Center has a simple fillable emergency plan that you can download free of charge. Denk vooruit has a separate emergency card and a detailed emergency plan. Ready.gov has a form to fill in. This guide follows the Belgian model and adds what other sources contribute.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/emergency-plan-home) · [National Crisis Center: My own emergency plan](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/MyEmergencyPlan_interactive%20PDF.pdf) · [Denk vooruit: Emergency plan](https://www.denkvooruit.nl/bereid-je-voor/noodplan) · [Ready.gov: Make A Plan](https://www.ready.gov/plan)

## Questions the plan answers

Denk vooruit gives examples of questions your plan should answer:

- You are at work and cannot get home. Your phone does not work. Who picks the children up from school?
- You have difficulty walking and the lift in your block of flats is not working. How do you get outside?
- You cannot reach your partner. Where do you meet?
- You depend on home care or medical equipment. How do you get the care you need?
- You live in student accommodation and your family lives in another city. How do you stay in touch?
- There is no internet. Where do you find reliable information?

Ready.gov asks five questions to start with:

1. How will I receive warnings?
2. What is my plan for sheltering?
3. What is my evacuation route?
4. How will we communicate as a family?
5. Does my emergency kit need topping up?

## The plan in 7 steps (National Crisis Center)

**1. Know your own home**

- Write down where the water supply, gas tap and electricity meters are and learn how to turn them off.
- Fit smoke alarms and CO alarms in all useful places. Test them regularly.
- Know how to switch off the ventilation system. That can be important if you have to shelter.
- Know how to switch the alarm system on and off.
- Know how to unlock electric gates, roller shutters or doors during a power cut.
- Know how to use the fire extinguisher.

**2. Gather useful items** in an emergency kit. See the guides on the emergency kit and emergency supplies.

**3. Learn the quickest way out.** What if the front door cannot be reached, or if there is no light? Think in advance about all the ways to get out safely.

**4. Know the emergency numbers.** Download the 112 BE app. Make a list with your gas, water and electricity supplier, the monitoring centre of your alarm system, your GP, your insurer, your neighbours or contact persons.

**5. Choose contact persons** (see below).

**6. Talk about it.** Make sure everyone in the house has the information. Make the plan together with friends, neighbours, family or acquaintances.

**7. Practise.**

- Teach children the steps, with extra attention to the emergency numbers and a safe escape route.
- Adapt the plan for people with special needs, for example specific medication, a disability or limited digital skills. Practise regularly.
- Include your animals in the plan.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan at home](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/emergency-plan-home)

The Crisis Center's fillable plan asks for:

| Section | What you fill in |
| --- | --- |
| Basics | address, date it was drawn up |
| Who lives here | number of adults and children; for each person name, mobile number and year of birth |
| Pets | name, species or breed, important information |
| Extra help | who in the house needs extra help and what kind, for example people with a disability, small children, people who take special medication |
| My home | where the main water stopcock, the gas tap, the electricity cabinet and the smoke alarms are |

## Contact persons: nearby and outside the region

The Crisis Center recommends two kinds of contact persons:

- **A contact person nearby**, whom you can go to if something happens at your home.
- **A contact person somewhat further from home**, for when you cannot get into your street, neighbourhood or municipality.

For an evacuation, the Crisis Center also recommends asking a family member or friend who does not live in the same zone to be a contact person. As soon as you are safe, you let him or her know. That way, this person knows where everyone is if family members have become separated. Ready.gov calls this an out-of-state contact and recommends calling or emailing that person to say where you are going, if time allows.

**How to make it usable**

1. Make sure everyone in the house knows the addresses and telephone numbers and that there is a list on paper. That way you find each other again, even if you have forgotten your mobile or your battery is flat (Crisis Center).
2. Programme a contact person into your phone. On some devices this is called ICE, in case of emergency (Crisis Center).
3. Ask your contact persons whether you can stay with them, also with your pet (Crisis Center).
4. Give a photo or copy of your completed plan to your contact persons (Crisis Center, Denk vooruit).
5. Learn the telephone numbers and email addresses of family members by heart. Help children remember important information, such as their parents' names (Rode Kruis-Vlaanderen, Restoring Family Links).

## Meeting points

If telephone and internet go down, you must know where to find each other without having to discuss it.

- Denk vooruit: choose with your household, family or friends a place where you meet if there is no telephone or internet connection. Also agree when you will meet there or when you will visit each other. In the example in the information booklet, someone comes to check on his parents after two days, as agreed in advance.
- Ready.gov: choose a familiar place that is easy to find, and adapt it to the circumstances.
- The Crisis Center: discuss the safest escape route and meeting place with the people you live with and put them in your emergency plan.

**Options, from near to far**

| Meeting point | When |
| --- | --- |
| Right next to the house, outside | in a fire or a sudden danger in the home; everyone must get out quickly |
| At the contact person nearby | if you cannot stay at home but the neighbourhood is safe |
| At the contact person somewhat further from home | if the neighbourhood or municipality cannot be reached or is being evacuated |
| The reception centre designated by the authorities | in an evacuation; you will hear where it is through the official channels |

Ready.gov recommends choosing destinations in different directions, so that you have options.

## School and childcare

**As a parent**

- Register the address of your children's school in BE-Alert. That way you get a message in an emergency near the school (Crisis Center).
- Agree who will pick up the children if you cannot and your phone does not work (Denk vooruit).
- Make sure your children have a contact person and know how to reach them. Teach them when and how to call important numbers (Ready.gov). The Crisis Center recommends teaching young children to call 112.
- Agree on a family password or phrase, so that your child does not go with a stranger (Ready.gov).

**What the school itself does (Crisis Center)**

- Keep the internal emergency plan up to date; the template of the Flemish Government can serve as a guide.
- Include the municipality's emergency planning coordinator in the plan, and make sure that person knows whom to call to give the school instructions.
- Check contact persons and telephone numbers regularly.
- Map the risks around the school, including prolonged outages of electricity, gas or internet.
- Have all teachers register with BE-Alert, with the address of the school.
- Practise the plan regularly with teachers and pupils.

For primary schools, the Crisis Center has the BE-Ready teaching pack and game.

**For an activity away from school**, the Crisis Center recommends that the coordinator always carries a list of those present and thinks in advance about how to alert the parents.

## Work

The Crisis Center asks employers and employees to:

- Know the emergency procedures and evacuation routes at work. Usually the prevention adviser is responsible.
- Register with BE-Alert and also register the address of the workplace.
- Map the risks around the workplace, including those for large parts of the country, such as outages of electricity, gas or internet. Employers draw up a plan to keep operations going.
- Give employees who need support the opportunity to come forward discreetly.

Include in your family plan what you do if you have to stay at work. The Crisis Center says: stay where you are until you know for sure that it is safe to go home.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan at work](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/work) · [National Crisis Center: An emergency kit on location](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/build-emergency-kit/emergency-kit-site)

## Neighbours and associations

The Crisis Center recommends building a network with neighbours, acquaintances, family and friends, and using existing networks such as a residents' association or sports club. Make arrangements about:

- **How you alert each other.** Via social media or messaging apps; by telephone, with arrangements about whom you may call at night; and how you reach each other without telephone or internet.
- **Which risks.** Nuclear emergency planning zones, flood areas, Seveso sites nearby, and risks for the whole country such as hours without electricity, heating or internet.
- **Who can offer which help.** Knowledge such as first aid or DIY skills, materials such as tools, space such as a large garage or cellar.
- **Who needs extra help.** For example people with a disability, single parents with young children, older people.

Other ways of helping each other that the Crisis Center mentions: taking a first aid course together, giving blood or plasma, spreading correct information, offering a place to sleep to people who cannot get home, lending things, doing shopping, lending your mobile or sharing a hotspot, cooking for someone.

**In a block of flats**, also discuss who has the keys, who can get to the water supply, gas tap and meters, whether there is a shared ventilation system and who can switch it off, and who helps the residents who need extra help. If you are not the owner, know the emergency exits.

Source: [National Crisis Center: A plan in your neighbourhood or association](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/make-plan/plan-your-neighbourhood-association-or-club) · [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf)

## People with care needs

**General (Crisis Center)**

- Check whether the evacuation routes are accessible and whether you can reach the gas tap, meter and water supply.
- Discuss with people around you how they can help you and how you will keep in touch. They can register your address in BE-Alert, so that they also get a message.
- Ask your GP or pharmacist for a medication list and keep it with your important documents.

**Per situation (Crisis Center)**

| Situation | Arrangements |
| --- | --- |
| Deaf or hard of hearing | make sure you receive warnings, for example a text message from BE-Alert or a fire alarm that vibrates or flashes; think about communication without telephone or internet |
| Blind or partially sighted | voice messages via BE-Alert; aids kept in a fixed place; an alternative |
| Wheelchair | an evacuation route if the lift is not working; who helps you and how you alert them; a wheelchair-accessible reception site |
| Devices that run on electricity (oxygen, electric wheelchair) | discuss with your care providers what to do in a power failure |
| Difficulty walking | practise now and then whether you can get out quickly; who helps and how you reach them |
| Difficulty with digital technology | have someone register you with BE-Alert; learn to recognise messages; agree how others will alert you |
| Little or no Dutch or French | register with BE-Alert, ask people around you to translate messages, have a translation app or someone who interprets |

**Additions from Ready.gov**

- Build a support network and tell them where your emergency supplies are. If appropriate, give a trusted person a key to your home.
- If you are on dialysis or another life-sustaining treatment, know more than one place where you can go. Ask your treatment centre about its emergency plan and a back-up location.
- Wear medical identification and put medical information on your devices.
- If you have a communication disability, carry cards that explain to responders how to communicate with you.

The MCF recommends using the accessibility features on your phone to receive important information.

## Animals in the plan

The Crisis Center asks for:

- The vet's telephone number in your list.
- A place to stay with friends or acquaintances, with room for the animal.
- Identification, preferably with a microchip, and your details up to date in the online registration.
- Vaccination, with a copy of the vaccination booklet in the emergency kit.
- A plan to transport the animal safely, and getting the animal used to the carrier.

## Registering with BE-Alert

BE-Alert is the government's alert system. A mayor, governor or the Minister of the Interior can send you a text message, email or voice call with instructions.

**Two ways to get a message**

1. You are registered and there is an emergency near one of your registered addresses.
2. You are physically near an emergency and the authorities alert everyone with a mobile in that zone. Then you also get a message if you are not registered.

Registering remains useful: then you are also alerted for addresses where you are not at that moment, such as your children's school or the home of a vulnerable family member.

**How to register (Vlaanderen.be)**

1. Go to be-alert.be and fill in the requested details: an email address and a personal password.
2. Fill in your address and check that your home is shown correctly on the map.
3. Confirm the registration via the link in the email.
4. Complete your profile: choice of language, whether you are deaf or hard of hearing, other telephone numbers and email addresses, and other addresses.
5. No internet: ask your municipality to register you.

Registration and messages are free.

**How to recognise a genuine message**

- It always starts with "BE-Alert".
- It comes from the number 1789 or 0477 77 77 55. Emails come from no-reply@be-alert.be.
- It is always free and never asks for bank details.
- Messages of general interest, such as about rubbish collection, do not start with "BE-Alert" and come from 8686 or 0477 77 77 55. You only get those if you gave permission for them.

Save 1789 and 0477 77 77 55 in your phone and mark the email address as trusted, so that messages do not end up in your spam. More about BE-Alert and other channels in the guide on communication and alerts.

## Keeping, updating and practising

1. Fill in the plan together with your family or the people you live with.
2. Take a photo or copy and give it to your contact persons.
3. Hang up or keep the plan in a fixed place: in the meter cupboard, by the entrance (Crisis Center), or with your emergency kit, on the fridge or in the hall cupboard (Denk vooruit).
4. Make sure there is always a paper copy, for when the electricity or internet goes down.
5. Update it when telephone numbers or family situations change.
6. Practise regularly, including playfully with children. The Crisis Center points out that finding the way out in thick smoke is not easy, and suggests practising this blindfolded.
