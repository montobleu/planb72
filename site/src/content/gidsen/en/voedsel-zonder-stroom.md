Without electricity, the fridge, freezer, electric cooker, oven and microwave stop working. This guide deals with three questions: how do you keep what is cold cold for as long as possible, what can you still eat afterwards, and how do you prepare food safely. Which supplies you should keep at home is covered in the guide on emergency supplies.

Source: [BBK: Vorsorgen für Krisen und Katastrophen](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/Buergerinformationen/Ratgeber/BBK-Vorsorgen-fuer-Krisen-und-Katastrophen.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=44)

## Before the interruption

If you know in advance that the electricity will go off, for example because of works or a load-shedding plan:

- Have food ready that you can eat cold.
- Have hot water ready in vacuum flasks, for example for baby bottles.
- Keep your freezer firmly closed.
- Put a bag of ice cubes in the freezer. That way you can check afterwards whether the food has thawed.

Even without advance notice, you can prepare:

- Put plastic bottles of water in the freezer. They work as ice packs during a power cut; when the ice has melted, you drink the water. Do not fill them to the brim, or they will burst.
- Use a thermometer for the fridge and freezer, so that you know how cold it is inside.
- Have an alternative way of cooking and fuel in the house.

## During the interruption: doors closed

The most important rule: do not open the fridge and freezer unnecessarily.

How long it stays cold:

| Appliance | How long |
| --- | --- |
| Freezer | an interruption of 3 to 4 hours does not make your food thaw |
| Freezer, door closed | frozen for 24 to even 36 hours, depending on the temperature; less if the freezer is only half full |
| Fridge, unopened | about 4 hours |

**If it lasts longer than 4 hours**

- Put perishable food in a cool box with ice.
- Dry ice: about 11 kilos of dry ice keeps a freezer of about 280 litres below freezing for three to four days. Do not let dry ice come into direct contact with food. Wear dry, thick gloves. Find out in advance where you can get dry ice.

**Other appliances.** Unplug sensitive electrical appliances, to avoid damage from excessive voltage when the power comes back on.

## After the interruption: the basic rules

**Check the freezer first**

1. Look at the bag of ice cubes you put in the freezer. If the ice has melted and frozen again, there is a good chance that the food has gone off. If it has not thawed, the food has not gone off.
2. If the freezer door stayed closed, the food can stay frozen for 24 to 36 hours. Then it is safe to eat.
3. Check the food in the fridge and freezer. Throw it away at the slightest suspicion that it has gone off.

**What to throw away and what to keep**

- Throw away perishable food from the fridge, such as meat, poultry, fish, eggs and leftovers, after 4 hours without electricity.
- Throw away anything that has been at 4 °C or warmer for more than 2 hours.
- Thawed food is usually still edible if it is still "fridge cold". It may be refrozen if it still contains ice crystals or is at 4 °C or colder; the quality may deteriorate, however.
- Never taste food to decide whether it is still safe.
- Throw away food with an unusual smell, colour or texture.
- Throw away food that has been at room temperature for 2 hours or longer.
- Throw away food that has come into contact with floodwater.

## Supplies without refrigeration: what to eat and what not to eat

- You can eat commercially tinned food straight from the tin without heating it.
- Do not eat from tins that are bulging (bottom or lid curved outwards), dented or rusty, even if the contents look fine. A bulging tin indicates spoiled contents.
- Do not eat anything that looks or smells abnormal, even if the tin looks normal.
- Throw away mouldy food immediately. If in doubt, do not eat it.
- Keep food in closed packaging and keep eating and cooking utensils clean.
- Do not let waste build up indoors, to avoid fire and vermin.

**Tins that have come into contact with floodwater.** You can still use them: wash and disinfect the tin thoroughly with a solution of one part bleach to ten parts water, and open the tin before you heat it. Other food that has come into contact with floodwater must be thrown away.

## Cooking without electricity: the options

Remember this distinction: some appliances may be used indoors, others only outdoors.

| Appliance | Indoors or outdoors | For what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gas cooker on the mains gas network | indoors, if it works without electricity | normal cooking |
| Tea-light food warmer | indoors | heating food, for example ready-made soup |
| Fondue set with fuel paste | indoors | bringing small amounts to the boil |
| Candle warmer, chafing dish, fondue pot | indoors | heating up |
| Gas stove approved for indoor use | indoors | cooking |
| Open fireplace | indoors | cooking |
| Camping stove with gas cartridge | outdoors only | small meals |
| Gas or charcoal barbecue | outdoors only, on a terrace or balcony | cooking |

**Rules for indoors**

- Indoors, only use appliances that are approved for that purpose. They have special safety features.
- Be careful with open fire indoors. If there is too little oxygen, carbon monoxide (CO) is produced. CO poisoning is life-threatening.
- Fit tested and approved CO alarms.
- Ventilate regularly.

**Rules for outdoors**

- Do not use a barbecue or camping stove inside the home: there is a risk of suffocation.
- Only use barbecues and camping stoves outdoors, at least 6 metres from windows.
- Do not bring a barbecue into the house for warmth. The smouldering coals release a lot of CO, even with the garage door open.
- Do not use a gas cooker or oven to heat your home.

**Saving fuel.** Choose food that cooks with little energy, so that your fuel lasts longer, and food that you can eat cold. Print out or write down recipes with long-life food in advance: without the internet you can no longer look them up.

**If electricity is limited but not gone.** In case of electricity shortages, use as few hobs as possible, the microwave instead of the ordinary oven, and preferably use electrical appliances after 22:00.

You can find more about CO in the guide on heat and cold.

## Per type of home

- **Flat without a terrace or balcony.** You have no safe place for a barbecue or camping stove. Rely on food you eat cold and on appliances approved for indoor use, such as a food warmer or fondue set.
- **Flat with a balcony.** A gas or charcoal barbecue can go on the balcony. Keep your distance from windows and doors.
- **House with a garden.** Cook outdoors, far from windows, doors and an attached garage.
- **House with a gas cooker.** It can often keep working normally. Do not use it for heating.

## Food safety without tap water

- Wash your hands with soap and clean water before, during and after preparing food and before eating. If there is no soap and water, use hand gel with at least 60 per cent alcohol. See the guide on hygiene.
- Only wash food and dishes with safe water. Treat water of uncertain quality first. See the guide on water.
- Do not use water from floods, radiators or the central heating.

Source: [CDC: About Handwashing](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/about/index.html) · [CDC: How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html)

## Baby food

**Formula**

- Preferably use ready-to-feed formula. If you have to make up powder, use bottled water, or boiled water as a last resort.
- Do not make baby formula with unsafe water.
- Throw away opened formula that has been in the fridge at 4 °C or warmer for more than 2 hours.
- When an interruption is announced, have vacuum flasks with hot water ready, for example for baby bottles.
- Keep sterilising baby bottles, also during a boil water advice.

**What belongs in your supplies**

- A reserve supply of baby food and formula powder.
- Bottles.
- Porridge, oats and baby food.

**Breastfeeding.** Breastfeeding women need more water.

## During a long interruption

The time limits at a glance:

| Moment | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| First hours | keep the fridge and freezer closed; an interruption of 3 to 4 hours does not make frozen food thaw |
| After about 4 hours | the fridge no longer keeps food safely cool; put perishable food in a cool box with ice |
| After 24 to 36 hours | a closed freezer can no longer hold out, a half-full freezer even sooner |
| After that | eat from your long-life supplies; cook outdoors or on an appliance approved for indoor use |

If there is too little food: be sparing and make sure that children and older people have enough nutritious food.

## When the electricity comes back

- Do not switch all electrical appliances on at the same time. Give the grid time to stabilise.
- Check the fridge and freezer and throw away food if in doubt.
- Top up your emergency supplies again.
