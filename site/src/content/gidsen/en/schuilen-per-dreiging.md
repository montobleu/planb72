## How to use this guide

In Belgium, sheltering means: you go inside the nearest accessible building and you stay there until the danger is over. The National Crisis Center gives three steps:

1. Stay inside or go inside the nearest accessible building, until you are told that it is safe again.
2. Close all windows and doors. Switch off ventilation, air conditioning and other systems that bring in outside air. Preferably go to a central room on the ground floor, away from windows and outside doors.
3. Follow the instructions of the authorities: listen to a battery-powered or wind-up radio and watch for police loudspeaker vans. If the network works, they also come via BE-Alert, official websites and social media.

Not every threat calls for the same place. What is safe for one threat can be dangerous for another. A cellar protects well against an explosion, but is life-threatening in a flood. This guide therefore gives the options per threat and per place in order of preference. Where Belgium gives no advice, the guide gives what foreign authorities recommend. If that differs from the Belgian advice, the Belgian advice applies.

Sheltering is temporary: from a few hours up to a maximum of 24 hours. After that, the authorities may ask you to evacuate. Never evacuate on your own initiative, unless you are in immediate danger.

If calling 112 does not work, wherever this guide mentions it: try another device or have neighbours or bystanders call. If calling does not work at all, ask for help in your neighbourhood or, if you can, go to the nearest police station, fire station or hospital (the map in the app shows them, with the Nearest button). In a major outage the municipality may open an information and reception point. Follow the radio.

## Overview: the direction per threat

| Threat | Go to | Stay away from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air attack, missiles, drones | As deep as possible: cellar, underground car park, metro station; otherwise an inner room without windows | Windows, glass, top floor, open ground |
| Explosion nearby | Away from the scene; inside, under a sturdy piece of furniture if things are falling | Damaged buildings, smoke, suspicious objects |
| Radioactive fallout | Inside a solid building, central room | Outside, windows |
| Chemical cloud | Inside, windows and ventilation closed | Outside, downwind of the cloud, the cellar |
| Flood, cloudburst | Upwards: higher floor, higher ground | Cellar, underground car park, metro, underpass |
| Storm, thunderstorm, tornado | Inside, away from windows and outer walls | Trees, metal structures, open plain |
| Wildfire | Away from fire and smoke, to a paved or built-up place | Forest, downwind of the smoke |
| Earthquake | Under a sturdy piece of furniture; outside, to an open space | Windows, façades, cables |
| Armed violence | Away if it is safe; otherwise hide | Doors, windows, the scene of the incident |
| Heatwave | Cool place inside or in the shade | Sun, parked car |
| Extreme cold | Inside, heated room | Long periods outside, CO sources |

## Air attack, missiles and drones

The National Crisis Center has no separate guideline for air attacks. The options below come from foreign authorities that do have one. They broadly agree.

Why it works: in an explosion, the main dangers are the blast wave, flying debris and shards of glass. Thick walls and earth stop them. Glass does not.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: a real civil defence shelter.** Belgium has no network of public civil defence shelters.
2. **Good choice: an underground space.** A cellar without windows, an underground car park, an underground metro station. A semi-basement or an underpass will also do. The safest places have several exits, one of which leads outside the building.
3. **Good choice without a cellar: an inner room with two walls between you and the outside.** A hallway, stairwell or bathroom in the middle of the building. If the outer wall falls, the second wall stops fragments and debris. A hallway is better than a bathroom, because tiles and mirrors can break.
4. **Emergency solution inside: behind something solid,** such as a kitchen unit or a parapet. If that is not possible either, leave the building and look again outside.
5. **Emergency solution outside: as low as possible.** Lie down in a hollow or ditch, behind a solid barrier, or behind a kerb, concrete litter bin or stairs. Protect your head with your arms. Open your mouth, against the effects of pressure differences.

The two-wall rule does not give complete safety. Move to a better place as quickly as possible.

### Per place

| Where you are | Best choice | If that is not possible | Emergency solution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flat, high floor | Cellar or underground car park of the building, if you can reach it quickly | Hallway or storeroom in the middle of the flat, two walls between you and the outside | On the floor behind an inner wall, away from windows and balcony |
| Flat, low floor | Cellar or underground car park | Hallway or stairwell without large windows | Behind a sturdy piece of furniture or kitchen unit |
| House with a cellar | Cellar room without windows, under the middle of the house | Inner room on the ground floor | On the floor behind an inner wall |
| House without a cellar | Inner room without windows: hallway, bathroom, understairs cupboard | Room with the fewest outer walls | Behind a sturdy piece of furniture; not in the attic |
| Work or school | Cellar or underground space of the building | Inner corridor without glass | Follow the building's emergency plan |
| On foot outside | Underground metro station or underground car park nearby | Inside a solid building, preferably concrete with little glass | Lie in a ditch or behind a solid barrier |
| In the car | Park without obstructing the emergency services and go into a building | Underground car park | Behind walls or other barriers |
| Public transport | Follow the driver; get off at the next stop and go inside | Underground metro station | Behind walls or other barriers |
| Open ground, forest | Lower-lying ground: ditch, hollow | Behind a wall or in a tunnel | Flat on the ground, head protected |

### Drones

The danger zone of explosives can extend to several hundred metres outdoors.

- Do not approach a drone. Stay at least 150 metres away.
- Go inside, especially if the drone is flying unsteadily or low.
- Avoid open ground near the drone. In a forest or open field: get below ground level if you can.
- Inside: stay in the middle of the building, away from windows and doors, in a room with solid walls.
- Call 112 if you see a drone near a critical site (power station, airport, port, water treatment plant) or find a crashed drone.

### Where not to go

- **Not in the attic or on the top floor.** A roof offers little protection against blast waves, glass and debris.
- **Not near windows, glass walls, mirrors, balconies or open entrances.**
- **Not in the lift.**
- **Not in a crawl space** and not near transformer substations, high-voltage pylons or gas, electricity and technical rooms.
- **Not in the kitchen or in a stairwell with a lot of glass.**
- **Not in a lightly built wooden shed or a glass conservatory.**

### How long and when to leave

- Stay until the authorities announce that the danger is over.
- Leave the shelter early only if it is damaged itself.
- Then help injured people and people who are trapped.
- Take only what you have to hand: water, food that you can eat at room temperature, identity document, money, bank card, mobile phone and charger or power bank.

## Explosion nearby

This is about an explosion caused by an accident or an attack, without an air raid alert beforehand. The Crisis Center gives its own guidelines for this.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: away from the scene, if it is safe.** Go to an open space. Do not take belongings and do not make calls on the way.
2. **Good choice if you are inside and things are falling:** take cover under a table, a desk or another sturdy piece of furniture.
3. **Emergency solution if you cannot get away:** keep still and wait until the emergency services arrive.

### What to do immediately

- Stay low if there is fire and smoke.
- Cover your mouth and nose with a handkerchief or other fabric, against dust and toxic gases.
- Warn the people around you or call 112.
- Stay nearby, but outside the danger zone, so that the emergency services can take care of you.

### If a building is damaged

- Leave the building by the quickest route. Take the stairs, not the lift.
- Keep your distance from the building, the debris and the dust cloud: about half the original height of the building.
- Watch out for the smell of gas, hissing sounds and loose electrical cables. Do not use matches or lighters.
- After an attack there may still be explosives. Do not touch anything suspicious, leave that zone and report the location to the emergency services.
- Avoid floors and stairs that are clearly weakened.

### If you are trapped

- Make yourself heard by knocking, with a torch or a whistle, or tap on pipes.
- Shout only as a last resort, so that you do not inhale dust.
- Cover your nose and mouth.

### How long and when to leave

If you are safe, away from the incident: stay where you are, preferably inside, and do not go and look, so that you do not obstruct the emergency services. If the network works, let your family know by text message or social media that you are safe. Agree in advance where you will meet if that does not work. Then follow the instructions on the radio: an explosion can have other consequences, especially with chemical substances.

## Radioactive fallout

In a nuclear accident, the Belgian authorities ask you to shelter: that is the best protection. The full explanation is in the guides of the nuclear module. Only the choice of place is covered here.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice in a nuclear accident:** inside the nearest building, in a central room on the ground floor. Close windows and doors, switch off ventilation, heating and air conditioning and close off the supply of outside air via the chimney.
2. **In the case of a nuclear weapon:** Belgium gives no separate advice for this. Foreign authorities recommend going as deep as possible and putting as many walls as possible between you and the outside: a cellar or the middle of a large brick or concrete building.
3. **Emergency solution:** any building is better than staying outside.

Always follow the instructions given by the authorities at the time.

### Per place

| Where you are | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| At home | Central room on the ground floor, windows, doors and ventilation closed, pets inside |
| Work | Shelter where you are; follow the internal emergency plan |
| School | Leave your children at school. They follow the same recommendations |
| On foot | Go into the nearest building |
| Car, close to a building | Go inside immediately |
| Car, no building within reach | Park in a safe place or drive at right angles to the wind direction; engine off, windows closed, air conditioning off, listen to the radio |
| Open ground without buildings | Call 112. If you cannot get through: move further away from the emergency until you find a safe and accessible building |

### How long

Stay inside until the authorities announce that it is safe. Sheltering lasts from a few hours up to a maximum of 24 hours. In the case of a nuclear weapon: stay in the best-protected place for the first 24 hours, unless there is immediate danger such as fire, a gas leak or collapse.

## Chemical cloud

In an accident involving hazardous substances, for example at a Seveso company or during transport, the rule in Belgium is: go inside and stay inside.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: inside the nearest building.** Close windows and doors and switch off the ventilation. Preferably take up position in a central room on the ground floor.
2. **If you are outside:** walk away from the incident, at right angles to the wind direction, and go into the nearest building.
3. **Emergency solution without a building: the car.** Switch off the ventilation and air conditioning and drive on until you find a safe, accessible building.

### Not to the cellar

Do not go to the cellar. Most gases are heavier than air and collect in cellars and low places. Follow the instructions given at the time; these may differ per substance.

### What to do in the room

- Seal gaps under doors and around windows with wet towels, plastic sheeting or tape, including the ventilation slots in window frames.
- Do not use candles: they use up oxygen.
- If gas does get in, breathe through a cloth or a face mask.
- Do not eat or drink anything that may be contaminated.

### If you came into contact with the substance

- Take off your outer clothing.
- Put your clothes and shoes in a bin bag, seal it and put the bag outside the living area.
- Take a shower. First wash your hands, then your face and hair, including your nose and ears, with soap and water.

### If the gas is released inside the building

Leave the building. If possible, do not go through the contaminated zone; if that is not possible, go as far away as possible inside the building and shelter there.

### How long and when to leave

Stay inside until you are told that it is safe, also in order to help others. Listen to the radio. Leave children at school. Do not make unnecessary calls; if the network works, use text messages or the internet. Evacuate only on the instructions of the emergency services. After the accident there may be an impact on drinking water, vegetables from the garden and livestock: follow the instructions.

## Flood and cloudburst

Here the opposite of an air attack applies: you go up, never down.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: higher ground or a higher floor.** Leave the area in time or go to the upper floors.
2. **Good choice: stay inside on a high floor** and limit your movements.
3. **Emergency solution if you are trapped: the highest point in the building.** Go onto the roof only if you have to and signal from there. Do not go into an enclosed attic, where you can become trapped as the water rises.

### Where not to go

- **Never into the cellar.** If the cellar floods, you will no longer be able to open the door from the inside. There is also a risk of a fatal electric shock.
- **No underground car parks, metro stations or underpasses.**
- **Do not walk, swim or drive through water,** not even on flooded roads. Even shallow moving water can knock you over or sweep your car away, and manhole covers can come loose.
- **Not over bridges above fast-flowing water.**

### Per place

| Where you are | Best choice | If that is not possible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat | Stay on your floor or higher | Top floor, not in the cellar storage unit |
| House with an upper floor | Upper floor | Highest room; roof only if you have to |
| Single-storey home | Leave the area, to higher ground, if you leave in time | Highest point in the house, not an enclosed attic |
| Work or school | Higher floor of the building | Follow the emergency plan |
| On foot | Higher ground, away from moving water | Solid building with an upper floor |
| In the car | Turn back before water on the road | If you are stuck in fast-flowing water: stay in the car; if the water rises inside, get onto the roof |
| Underground (metro, car park, underpass) | Immediately up and to a higher place | A building with upper floors nearby |

### What to take and prepare

Put sandbags in front of cellar openings and outside doors, raise carpets and furniture, shut off gas, electricity and water if necessary and do not leave the car in the garage. If you have to evacuate: take spare clothing and boots. Charge your mobile phone and set aside drinking water, because tap water may become contaminated.

### How long and when to leave

Only go back when the authorities say it is safe. Listen to the recommendations on tap water. Do not touch electrical appliances if they are wet or if you are standing in water.

## Storm, thunderstorm and tornado

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: inside a solid building.** Go inside, stay inside and close windows and doors. In a tornado: stay away from windows, doors and outer walls and watch out for flying debris.
2. **In a tornado, the best place in the building:** a small inner room without windows or a cellar on the lowest floor. Protect your head and neck with your arms and put furniture or blankets around or on top of you.
3. **Good choice in a thunderstorm when travelling: a car with a metal body and roof.** It works as a Faraday cage; keep windows and doors closed. A convertible or a plastic-bodied car is not safe. A metal caravan is also good; disconnect the power cable in that case.
4. **Emergency solution in an open field in a thunderstorm:** crouch on your toes, arms around your knees, head as low as possible. Do not walk during a lightning strike.

### Where not to go

- **Never under a tree**, certainly not under an isolated tree.
- **Not near metal structures**, high summits, fences and forest edges. Stay at least 3 metres from fences.
- **No bus shelter, carport, tent or open shelter**: these do not protect against lightning.
- **Do not park near trees.**
- **Not under a bridge** in a tornado: little protection and dangerous for traffic.
- **In a group:** crouch spread out, at least 2 metres apart.

### Inside during a thunderstorm

- Unplug electrical appliances.
- Stay away from windows, metal pipes and radiators. Do not take a shower.

### Car in a tornado

In a car there is no safe option in a tornado, only less dangerous ones. If the tornado is far away and traffic is light, drive away at right angles to its path. If you are overtaken by wind or debris: park off the traffic lanes, stay in the car with your seatbelt on and your head below the windows. If there is a clearly lower spot next to the road, lie down there with your hands on your head.

### How long and when to leave

Stay inside until the storm has passed. Do not go outside during the storm to repair damage. Afterwards, do not touch cables on the ground. If the network works, use 1722 or the online counter 1722.be for non-urgent help; keep 112 free for life-threatening situations.

## Wildfire

### Order of preference

1. **If you live near a forest or nature area:** call 112 if you see a fire. Close windows and doors. Do not evacuate on your own initiative, wait for the recommendations.
2. **If you are in the forest:** follow paths that lead away from the fire and the column of smoke, preferably at right angles to the wind direction, to a public road, a large paved area or a built-up area.
3. **If there is smoke but no order to evacuate:** stay inside in a safe room, windows closed and ventilation off, or go to a community building where there is less smoke.
4. **Emergency solution if you are trapped:** call 112 and give your location. Help may be delayed. Turn on lights so that rescuers can find you.

### What to do immediately

- Give your location with recognisable landmarks: paths, pools, lookout towers, car parks. If the network works, also use the 112 app.
- Help others to a safe place.
- No mobile phone or signal: evacuate as quickly as possible and alert people.
- If you have to move outside: protect your airways with a mask or scarf. This does not protect against toxic gases.
- Shut off the electricity and gas if you leave the house.
- Choose a room in advance that you can seal off from the outside air.

### Where not to go

- Not in the direction in which the wind is blowing the smoke.
- Do not go back to the area without permission from the emergency services.
- Stay away from hot ash, charred trees and smouldering debris.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Wildfire](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/wildfires) · [Suomi.fi: Wildfires and major building fires](https://www.suomi.fi/guides/preparedness/how-to-prepare-for-incidents-and-crises/wildfires-and-major-building-fires) · [Ready.gov: Wildfires](https://www.ready.gov/wildfires)

## Earthquake

### Order of preference inside

1. **If you are close to an exit** and can leave the building safely, go to an open space, away from buildings.
2. **Otherwise: under a sturdy piece of furniture.** Take cover under a table, desk or bed and hold on until the shaking stops. Cover your head and upper body.
3. **No sturdy furniture:** crouch down against an inner wall, away from windows.

This is how to protect yourself inside: drop onto your hands and knees, protect your head and neck with one arm and crawl under a table if one is close by. Avoid doorways. In bed: turn onto your stomach and cover your head and neck with a pillow. With a wheelchair or walker: lock the wheels, bend forward and cover your head and neck.

### Outside and in the car

- Outside: go to an open space, away from buildings, electrical wires and anything that may collapse, such as bridges. In a public place: take cover somewhere you stay out of the crowd.
- In the car: stop in a safe place where you do not block the road and stay in the vehicle. If electrical wires are lying on the car, do not get out and wait for the emergency services.

### Afterwards

Be prepared for aftershocks. Do not smoke and do not make fire or sparks. Do not go back into a home that seems unsafe to you. Shut off the gas and electricity when you return and have the chimney checked. If you are trapped: knock on a pipe or wall and use a whistle instead of shouting. If the network works, also send a message.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Earthquake](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/earthquake) · [Ready.gov: Earthquakes](https://www.ready.gov/earthquakes)

## Armed violence and terrorism

For someone with a weapon, the Crisis Center gives three steps: run away if it is safe, hide if it is not, and call 112.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: escape.** Run away if you can keep going safely. Leave your belongings behind. Tell the people around you and help them if you are safe yourself.
2. **Good choice if you are inside or cannot escape: hide.**
   - Hide and lie down on the floor.
   - Stay away from doors and windows. Turn off the light.
   - Lock the door or block it if you can.
   - Stay quiet. Put your mobile phone on silent.
   - Hide in a place with thick walls or lock yourself in.
3. **Raise the alarm:** call 112 if you can. If the network works but you cannot speak, chat via the 112 app. Give information about the attacker and the location.

### If you are in a safe place

- Stay where you are, preferably inside, so that you do not obstruct the emergency services.
- Do not go and look or go back to look at the situation.
- If the network works, let your family know by text message or social media that you are safe. If it does not, go to the place you agreed with your family in advance.
- Follow the directions of the emergency responders.

### Preparing

In crowded places, first check how you can get away quickly and where the emergency exits are. If you see a suspicious object or person: call 101 and do not touch the object.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Attack on a soft target](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/security-risks/attack-against-soft-target) · [Denk vooruit: An attack](https://www.denkvooruit.nl/risicos/risicos-in-nederland/een-aanslag) · [Ready.gov: Mass gatherings incidents](https://www.ready.gov/mass-gatherings-incidents) · [National Crisis Center: Disruption of the digital infrastructure](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/societal-risks/failure-digital-infrastructure)

## Heatwave

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: stay inside until it is cooler** if it is really very hot.
2. **Good choice if it is too hot at home: a cool public place,** such as a service centre, library or shopping centre.
3. **Emergency solution outside: in the shade as much as possible**, with a cap or hat.

### What to do

- Drink more than usual; do not wait until you are thirsty. Water is better than soft drinks.
- Take water with you if you are on a bus, train or in the car for a long time.
- Wet your forehead and neck regularly. Wear light, light-coloured clothing.
- Avoid heavy exertion in the hottest part of the day.
- Never leave people or animals in a parked vehicle.
- A fan does not lower body temperature, even if it feels that way. Close curtains and use the oven less.
- Call on people who need help.
- If the network works, call your GP in case of cramps, high fever, headache or fainting. Call 112 only if a life is in danger.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Heatwave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/heatwave) · [Ready.gov: Extreme heat](https://www.ready.gov/heat)

## Extreme cold, snow and freezing rain

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice: stay inside** as much as possible.
2. **At home:** set the thermostat to a minimum temperature that is still comfortable. Close rooms you do not use. Air the house for 10 minutes every day.
3. **If you have to go outside:** wear several layers, cover your whole body and wear a hat.
4. **Stuck in the car:** stay in the car. Keep a blanket, ice scraper, water and a hot drink in the car.

### Where not and what not

- Watch out for CO poisoning with an open fire, coal, paraffin or pellet stove, gas water heater, gas or heating oil.
- Never heat your home with a gas cooker or oven. Use generators and grills only outside and away from windows.
- Avoid overexertion when clearing snow.
- Keep pets inside or make sure they have food and drinking water.
- After the frost: frozen pipes may leak.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Cold wave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/cold-wave) · [Ready.gov: Winter weather](https://www.ready.gov/winter-weather)

## In a tunnel during a fire

A tunnel is a possible shelter in an air attack, but dangerous in a fire. In a fire in a tunnel:

1. Switch on your hazard warning lights.
2. Drive out of the tunnel if you can. If that is not possible, put the car as close as possible to the tunnel wall.
3. Switch off the engine and leave the key in the ignition.
4. Leave your vehicle. See which way the smoke is going and walk in the opposite direction to an emergency exit.
5. Call for help from an emergency telephone in the tunnel.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Fire or collapse of a tunnel](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/technological-risks/fire-or-collapse-tunnel)

## When to leave the shelter earlier

The rule is: stay until the authorities say it is safe. There are a few exceptions:

- **Fire, gas leak, collapse or serious injury** at your shelter, also during radioactive fallout.
- **The shelter itself is damaged.**
- **Gas or hazardous substances inside the building**: go outside.
- **Water rising in a cellar or underground space**: go upstairs immediately.
- **You are in immediate danger**: then you may evacuate without waiting for an order, and do not lose time looking for belongings.

Source: [Ready.gov: Radiation emergencies](https://www.ready.gov/radiation) · [Dovidka.info: What is a shelter](https://dovidka.info/en/what-is-shelter/) · [BBK: Schutz suchen](https://www.bbk.bund.de/DE/Warnung-Vorsorge/Vorsorge/Schutz-suchen/schutz-suchen_node.html) · [National Crisis Center: Evacuating](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/evacuate)

## Children, vulnerable people and animals

- **Children at school or in childcare:** leave them there and do not go to collect them. Teachers follow their internal emergency plan. Otherwise you create traffic jams, obstruct the emergency services and put yourself and the children in danger. The school will let you know when you can collect your child. Register the school's address in BE-Alert in advance.
- **People who need help:** discuss the three steps of sheltering in advance with family, neighbours or colleagues and make arrangements. Arrange transport to a protected place in advance. Let others shelter with you if necessary.
- **Pets:** first take care of your own safety. If you can let your animals shelter without risk, do so.
- **Warn the people around you** that they need to shelter. If the network works, preferably use WhatsApp, Signal or social media, so that the telephone network does not become overloaded.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Sheltering](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/right-reflex/seeking-shelter) · [MCF: In case of crisis or war](https://rib.msb.se/filer/pdf/31523.pdf)
