In an emergency you want to know three things: what is going on, what you need to do yourself and how you can help others. The Crisis Center therefore asks you to know and follow the official channels. This guide first describes how the authorities reach you, then how you receive information yourself if the power and network fail, and finally how to stay in contact with each other. How to call 112 is in the guide on the emergency call; distress signals without a device are in the guide on signals and distress codes.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Information during an emergency](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-can-you-do/stay-informed/get-informed-emergency-situation)

## The official channels in Belgium

During an emergency, the authorities and emergency services often use several channels at the same time:

| Channel | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BE-Alert | text message, voice call or email; in some municipalities also on information boards along the road | Crisis Center, BE-Alert |
| Media | radio, TV, online media | Crisis Center |
| Websites | municipality, province, National Crisis Center | Crisis Center |
| Social media | for example @CrisiscenterBE and the channels of the municipality, police and fire service | Crisis Center |
| Police loudspeaker vans | loudspeakers in the street | Crisis Center |
| Information number | a number of the municipality or province, or the national information number 1771 | Crisis Center |
| In certain emergencies | information boards, leaflets, door-to-door communication | brochure |

**How to find the right channels in advance (Crisis Center)**

- Municipality: for most municipalities the website is the name of the municipality followed by .be.
- Fire service: on brandweer.be you can find the fire service zones.
- Police: on politie.be you can find the website of your local police using your postcode.
- Social media: look for the social media logos on the official website; they lead to the real profiles.

Subscribe in advance to the channels of your municipality, province, police zone and fire service zone, and register with BE-Alert.

## BE-Alert

**What it is.** The alert system of the authorities, operational throughout Belgium since 2017. A mayor, governor or the Minister of Home Affairs can send you a message saying what has happened and what you need to do. BE-Alert reports more than 1.25 million registered addresses and 93 per cent of municipalities connected.

**Three ways you receive a message (BE-Alert)**

1. **At your registered addresses.** You have registered and there is an emergency near an address you registered: your home, work, your children's school or the address of a vulnerable family member. You receive a text message, email or voice call, even if you are not at home.
2. **Based on your location.** In major emergencies or if you need to act urgently, such as in an evacuation, the authorities can send a text message to everyone who is physically in the zone. You do not need to be registered for this. The operators only check which phones are in the risk zone and automatically delete that list six months after the message.
3. **In the street.** Since 2022, BE-Alert messages can also appear on other screens, such as an information board in your municipality.

**Recognising a genuine message (Crisis Center)**

- It always starts with "BE-Alert".
- It comes from 1789 or 0477 77 77 55. Emails come from no-reply@be-alert.be.
- It is always free and never asks for bank details.
- Messages of general interest, for example about waste collection, do not start with "BE-Alert" and come from 8686 or 0477 77 77 55, if you have given consent for them.

Save 1789 and 0477 77 77 55 in your phone and mark the email address as trusted.

**Test messages.** According to the Crisis Center, towns and municipalities can test BE-Alert on the first Thursday of every month, and the Crisis Center carries out a larger-scale test on the first Thursday of every quarter. According to the national test strategy, a test message always contains "BE-Alert", the sentence that it is a test message and that you do not need to do anything, and a reference to more information.

**Registering** is done on be-alert.be or via your municipality if you do not have internet. When completing your profile you can indicate whether you are deaf or hard of hearing. The steps are in the guide on the family emergency plan.

## Sirens in Belgium

The situation is less clear than in neighbouring countries. This is what the sources say:

- The national test strategy of the Crisis Center states that until December 2018 the siren network was tested audibly for the public four times a year: on the first Thursday of January, April, July and October. That schedule was kept for BE-Alert.
- Vlaanderen.be mentions sirens and police loudspeakers alongside radio and TV as ways in which the authorities give warnings, with BE-Alert as an additional direct channel.
- In a VRT article from 2022, the mayor of Tienen says that the siren system in Belgium is being phased out and replaced by BE-Alert, and that the last siren of the Seveso company in her town was removed in 2021.

A current official description of a Belgian siren signal (how it sounds, what it means) was not found in the sources consulted. Therefore rely on BE-Alert, radio and the official websites. If you hear a siren, look for information via those channels.

**At the border.** In the Netherlands, a siren outside 12:00 on the first Monday of the month means: go inside immediately, close windows, doors and ventilation and follow the information via radio or TV (Denk vooruit). In Germany, a rising and falling tone lasting one minute means: there is danger, inform yourself; a continuous tone lasting one minute means that the danger is over (BBK).

## The information number 1771

To deal with questions from residents, a municipality, province or the Crisis Center can open an information number. They can use their own number or the national information number 1771. That number can be operational within the hour, day and night. Thanks to a partnership with the FPS Public Health, it can handle both general questions and sensitive calls about possible victims. Municipalities must first conclude an agreement to use it.

1771 is therefore not a number that always answers. It is opened during an emergency, and the authorities then announce that it is active. For life-threatening situations, call 112.

Source: [National Crisis Center: Communication](https://crisiscenter.be/en/role-national-crisis-center/communication) · [National Crisis Center: Information to the population](https://crisiscenter.be/en/what-do-authorities-do/crisis-management/information-provision-citizens)

## Information without electricity: the radio

During a power cut, the internet and mobile networks can also fail. The Crisis Center therefore asks for:

- A battery-powered or wind-up radio, preferably with DAB+ and FM.
- A charged power bank.
- Spare batteries.
- A paper list of important telephone numbers.

A car radio also works during a power cut (BBK, OFF ON). The BBK writes that radio stations keep working if the mobile network and internet fail, and that a radio is therefore important in emergencies.

**What the Belgian sources do not say.** The Belgian sources consulted do not designate a separate emergency broadcaster. They refer to radio, TV and online media in general. In the Netherlands, the regional public broadcasters are the emergency broadcasters (Denk vooruit).

**Do this now (following the example of Denk vooruit):** look up the frequency of your station, write it down and stick it on your radio, and tune the radio to it in advance.

## Calling or sending messages

In a major emergency, the telephone network quickly becomes overloaded. That can disrupt the emergency response.

- Do not make unnecessary phone calls. Use messaging apps and social media (brochure).
- Only phone if it is really necessary and preferably use text messages (OFF ON).
- In a large-scale emergency, contact your family by text message or messaging apps rather than by phone; mass calling overloads the network (Crisis Center, evacuating).
- Do not call the emergency numbers to ask for information. During earlier power cuts, the emergency numbers were often called for information, which overloaded the emergency call centres (BBK).
- If you are affected, keep your mobile phone ready to receive calls at night as well (BBK).
- Always keep one phone switched on so that you can receive warnings (Denk vooruit).

**What you need to know about the network (BBK).** With internet telephony, the router, fibre and handset need electricity, so the landline also fails. Mobile networks have only partial and short-lived emergency power. If 112 and the other emergency numbers no longer work, in an emergency go to the nearest police station or hospital if possible.

## Recognising false and misleading messages

During an emergency a lot of information circulates, especially on social media. People often share false information without realising it; sometimes it is done deliberately to spread panic or to turn people against each other (Crisis Center).

**Six questions (Crisis Center)**

1. **Does it come from an official source?** Look at the bio, the URL, earlier posts and the logo.
2. **Who is the author or distributor?** Is that a reliable source?
3. **Do other reliable sources confirm it?** If you find the same information elsewhere, there is a good chance that it is correct.
4. **What does it look like?** Many spelling mistakes, exclamation marks or capital letters in the title point to disinformation.
5. **What is the purpose?** If the message plays strongly on emotions, there is a good chance that it is disinformation.
6. **Is the photo genuine?** Photos and videos are sometimes taken out of context. Look up the photo in a search engine.

The BBK adds: check whether a website has an imprint with a real name and full address, not just an anonymous email address, and whether sources and references are given.

**What to do**

- If in doubt, do not spread it (Crisis Center).
- Share only messages from the authorities and emergency services yourself (brochure).
- Do not take unnecessary photos or videos of an emergency and do not share images that harm the privacy of victims (Crisis Center).
- Alert people around you who are isolated or have few means of keeping informed (Crisis Center).

## Contact without telephone or internet: arrangements

The Crisis Center asks neighbours and associations to agree in advance how they will reach each other if the telephone or internet does not work, and who has useful equipment, such as a battery-powered radio. ## Walkie-talkies (PMR446) and CB

**PMR446.** According to a 2020 BIPT consultation document on the radio interfaces for PMR-446:

- Frequency band 446 to 446.2 MHz.
- Hand-held devices with a fixed antenna, peer-to-peer over short distances. No use as a base station or repeater.
- Transmit power up to 500 mW e.r.p.
- Applications under these interfaces are exempt from a BIPT licence.

On its page about radio for hunting, the BIPT writes that CB and PMR-446 radios may be used without a licence.

An official emergency channel for PMR446 was not found in the sources consulted.

**CB radio.** For CB, no channel arrangements or emergency channels were found in the official sources consulted, only the statement by the BIPT that CB may be used without a licence for hunting.

## Radio amateurs and B-EARS

**B-EARS** (Belgium Emergency Amateur Radio Service) is part of the UBA, the Belgian association of radio amateurs, and has more than 300 members throughout Belgium. Its predecessor, the Emergency Radio Network, worked at the request of the Red Cross from 1962.

What B-EARS does according to the UBA:

- Providing a basic radio communication service to official bodies, and exclusively at their request, when their normal communication is no longer available.
- Setting up a radio network with a central net controller from, for example, a crisis centre or an operational command post.
- Working with analogue FM voice (simplex or via a repeater), digital modes such as DMR and Winlink, and shortwave and satellite for long distances.
- Organised per province, with cooperation agreements with provincial governors.

B-EARS is therefore not a service that you can call on yourself. If you want to help, UBA members can join as volunteers.

## Meshtastic (mention)

Meshtastic is an open-source project that uses inexpensive LoRa radios as a communication platform without a mobile network, Wi-Fi or internet. According to the project documentation:

- Each device forwards the messages it receives, so that a network of devices (mesh) is formed and distant group members also receive messages.
- You send text messages between members of the network.
- A router is not needed; no phone is needed for the mesh network itself either.
- A device can be paired with one phone, for one user at a time.
- According to the project, LoRa can be used without an additional licence in most regions, unlike amateur radio.

Meshtastic is a community project, not an official alert channel. It does not replace BE-Alert, radio or 112.

Source: [Meshtastic: Introduction](https://meshtastic.org/docs/introduction/)

## Per person

- **Deaf or hard of hearing.** Make sure you receive warnings, for example text messages from BE-Alert, and state when you register that you are deaf or hard of hearing. Think about communication without telephone or internet (Crisis Center, Vlaanderen.be).
- **Blind or partially sighted.** Choose voice messages via BE-Alert (Crisis Center).
- **Limited digital skills.** Have someone register you with BE-Alert and learn to recognise messages (Crisis Center).
- **Little or no Dutch or French.** Register with BE-Alert, ask people around you to translate messages and download a translation app if needed (Crisis Center). The brochure is also available in French, German and English.
- **Family at a different address.** Register their address in your BE-Alert profile (Crisis Center).
