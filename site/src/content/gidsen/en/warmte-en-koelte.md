If the power fails, the heating often fails too. The Crisis Center's OFF ON brochure explicitly mentions the shutdown of boilers and circulation pumps as a consequence of a power cut. The BBK writes: without power your heating may not work, and in winter rooms cool down quickly. In summer, fans and air conditioning do not work. This guide covers both situations.

## Keeping the house warm: the options

In the order in which the sources mention them first.

**1. Keeping the heat in**

- Close windows and doors to keep the heat in (OFF ON).
- Close off the rooms you do not use or rarely use (Crisis Center, cold wave).
- Hang blankets over the windows and put rugs on the floor (MCF).

**2. Living in one room**

- As far as possible, choose one room to stay in and keep the doors closed, so that the heat does not escape (BBK).
- Gather together in one room (MCF).
- In a power cut, several people can gather together to share light and warmth (Crisis Center).

The sources do not say which room to choose. Choose a room that you can ventilate: even in one room, regular ventilation remains necessary (see below).

**3. Keeping the body warm instead of the room**

- With warm clothing, blankets and sleeping bags you can bridge the outage for a while (BBK).
- In the example in the Dutch information booklet, someone wears several jumpers and stays under the blankets in one room.

**4. Another heat source**

- Gas heaters, ethanol fireplaces and paraffin heaters that are approved for indoor use work without power and can be used safely indoors (BBK).
- With gas appliances: make sure they have an oxygen depletion safety device and an ignition safety device (BBK).
- A wood stove or open fireplace, with a supply of fuel (BBK).
- See the section on heating safely. The Poison Centre recommends avoiding mobile combustion appliances as much as possible.

**5. Going to a warm place**

- Go to a place in the municipality with power if the heat or cold is extreme (Ready.gov). Ask the local authorities which warm or cool places are open.

**What not to do**

- Do not heat your home with a gas cooker or oven. That increases the risk of CO poisoning, fire and burns (Ready.gov).
- Do not bring a barbecue indoors for heating (Poison Centre).
- Only use generators outdoors (Ready.gov). See the guide on light and energy.

## Ventilating, even when it is cold

It seems contradictory, but every source asks for it.

- Ventilate your home for 10 minutes every day (Crisis Center, cold wave).
- Keep windows and doors closed to avoid cooling down, but ventilate regularly: open the windows wide for a few minutes to refresh the air (BBK).
- If you use candles as a light source, regular ventilation is especially important to refresh the oxygen (BBK, Stromausfall).
- If you use another heat source, open a window for breathing air (MCF).

Source: [National Crisis Center: Cold wave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/cold-wave) · [BBK: Vorsorgen für Krisen und Katastrophen](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/Buergerinformationen/Ratgeber/BBK-Vorsorgen-fuer-Krisen-und-Katastrophen.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=44) · [BBK: Vorsorge und Handeln bei Stromausfall](https://www.bbk.bund.de/DE/Warnung-Vorsorge/Vorsorge/Stromausfall/stromausfall_node.html) · [MCF: In case of crisis or war](https://rib.msb.se/filer/pdf/31523.pdf)

## Clothing in layers

- Put on several layers and cover your whole body. Also wear a hat (Crisis Center, cold wave).
- The MCF mentions woollen clothing, thick socks, hats, gloves and scarves.
- If you have to go outside: limit your time outdoors and wear layers of warm clothing. Watch for signs of frostbite and hypothermia (Ready.gov).
- Avoid overexertion when shovelling snow and walking in snow, to reduce the risk of a heart attack (Ready.gov).

Source: [National Crisis Center: Cold wave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/cold-wave) · [MCF: In case of crisis or war](https://rib.msb.se/filer/pdf/31523.pdf) · [Ready.gov: Winter Weather](https://www.ready.gov/winter-weather)

## Sleeping warm

- Sleeping bags, blankets and sleeping mats (MCF, BBK).
- Put out heat sources before you go to sleep (MCF).
- Never use mobile combustion appliances in a bedroom (Poison Centre).
- Put out candles before you go to sleep (OFF ON).
- Ready.gov recommends CO detectors on every floor, in central locations and with a backup battery.

## Heating safely and carbon monoxide (CO)

The Poison Centre writes that in Belgium carbon monoxide is responsible for most fatal poisoning accidents. Every year a few dozen people die and about 1,000 end up in hospital. Most accidents happen between October and April. All heating appliances can produce CO, except electric ones.

**What CO is.** A gas without smell, colour or taste. You cannot detect it yourself. It is produced by incomplete combustion of wood, coal, natural gas, butane, propane and paraffin, when there is too little oxygen. The Poison Centre: 0.1 per cent CO in the air is fatal within 1 to 3 hours, 1 per cent within just 15 minutes. Children are especially sensitive. In pregnant women, prolonged exposure can harm the unborn child.

**By appliance (Poison Centre)**

| Appliance | Risk | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portable paraffin heater, portable butane appliance, decorative bio-ethanol fireplace | not connected to a chimney; uses up the oxygen in the room and the combustion gases stay in the room | do not use continuously; refresh the air regularly; leave a door or window ajar; not in small spaces such as a caravan; never in a bedroom |
| Barbecue indoors | smouldering charcoal releases a lot of CO, even with the garage door open | do not do it |
| Car or machine with a petrol engine in a garage | exhaust gases contain CO | do not let it run in a small or closed space |
| Wood stove | damp wood disrupts the draught; ash up to the grate leads to incomplete combustion | only dry and clean wood; remove ash regularly; sweep the chimney once a year |
| Kitchen water heater without a flue | | use for at most about ten minutes per half hour; never for a shower or bath; no cooker hood or extractor at the same time |
| Gas appliance | a yellow-orange flame indicates a lack of oxygen; a good flame is blue | with an orange flame, contact a professional |

Condensation on windows or walls can indicate that combustion gases are not being removed properly (Poison Centre).

**General rules (BBK, Crisis Center, Ready.gov)**

- Indoors, only use appliances approved for that purpose (BBK).
- Install a tested, approved CO detector (BBK). The Crisis Center asks for CO detectors in all relevant places in the home and for them to be tested regularly.
- If you heat your home with gas, heating oil, an open fireplace or a coal, paraffin or pellet stove, take the necessary precautions against CO (Crisis Center, cold wave).

## Recognising CO poisoning and what to do

**Recognising (Poison Centre)**

- **Acute**, with a lot of CO: first headache and dizziness, great tiredness, nausea, vomiting. Someone who does not realise they must leave the room loses consciousness and can then no longer save themselves.
- **Chronic**, with small amounts over a long time: vague complaints such as headache, a heavy stomach, muscle weakness, difficulty concentrating, changing moods.
- **Suspicion.** It becomes stronger if several people have the same complaints at the same time, or if the complaints come on in the same room and disappear when you leave the room.

**What to do (Poison Centre)**

1. Never enter a room where an unconscious casualty is lying. Protect yourself.
2. Call the emergency services immediately.
3. Open windows and doors, if you can do so without risk to yourself.
4. If the casualty is unconscious: call 112 and say that CO is suspected. If possible, switch off the appliance. Get the casualty out of the room. If the casualty is no longer breathing, give mouth-to-mouth ventilation; according to the Poison Centre, this is safe. If the casualty is still breathing, lay them on their side without a pillow.
5. If the casualty is conscious: call the GP.

The Poison Centre can be reached free of charge 24 hours out of 24 on 070 245 245. See also the scenario card on CO.

Source: [Poison Centre: Preventing CO poisoning](https://www.poisoncentre.be/app/uploads/2026/02/Brochure-CO-vergiftiging-voorkomen-NL-1.pdf)

## Fire from heating and candles

- During a power cut, the fire risk increases through incorrect use of gas appliances, candles and the like, or because they are left burning unattended. Smoke alarms in every living space are an extra safety measure (BBK, Stromausfall).
- Immediately after the power cut begins, switch off all electrical heating appliances: cooker, oven, iron, coffee maker, kettle, electric blanket, fan heater. This prevents them from switching on uncontrolled when the power comes back (BBK, Stromausfall).
- Keep extinguishing equipment ready: a fire extinguisher, a garden hose, a fire blanket (BBK, Stromausfall).
- Never extinguish burning fat, wax or liquid fuel with water; the water evaporates immediately and causes a fat explosion (BBK).

More about candles in the guide on light and energy.

## Hypothermia and frostbite

Ready.gov describes the signs and the first steps.

| | Signs | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frostbite | numbness; white or greyish-yellow skin; firm or waxy skin, especially on the face, fingers and toes | go to a warm room; soak in warm water; warm with body heat; do not massage and do not use a heating pad |
| Hypothermia | shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech, drowsiness; a body temperature below 35 °C (95 °F) is an emergency | go to a warm room; first warm the centre of the body (chest, neck, head, groin); keep the person dry and wrapped in warm blankets, including the head and neck |

See also the first aid pages.

Source: [Ready.gov: Winter Weather](https://www.ready.gov/winter-weather)

## Pipes and after the cold

- In severe frost, water pipes can freeze and burst (BBK, Stromausfall).
- Be careful when the thaw sets in: frozen pipes may be damaged and leak (Crisis Center, cold wave).
- After a gas shortage or energy scarcity, the authorities sometimes ask people to turn the thermostat down and use less hot water (Crisis Center, natural gas).

## Cold by person and by place

- **Older people, children, sick people and pets** are at greater risk in winter weather (Ready.gov).
- **Pets.** Keep them indoors or make sure they have enough food and drinking water (Crisis Center, cold wave).
- **Family members who need care.** The BBK recommends preparing to care for family members who need care yourself for a while.
- **In the car.** Take a blanket, hot drinks or water, in case you get stuck in a traffic jam (Crisis Center). If you are stuck in the car, stay in it (Ready.gov). See the guide on the emergency kit for the winter kit in the car.
- **Outdoors.** According to the Crisis Center, without a head covering you lose 30 per cent of your body heat.
- **Flat.** The sources give no separate guidelines. Use the rules for one room and ventilation. According to the Poison Centre, portable heaters without a chimney require extra attention to ventilation.

## Heat: staying cool without power

According to the Crisis Center, a heatwave is a period of at least five consecutive summer days of at least 25 degrees, of which three days reach at least 30 degrees. Ready.gov points out that a heatwave can also cause power cuts.

**Before the heat (Crisis Center)**

- Follow the RMI weather forecast.
- If you are a person at risk, such as a heart patient or older person, ask your GP about the risks.
- Ask your doctor or pharmacist whether your medicines may have an adverse effect in hot weather.
- Visit people who need help, to help them prepare.

**Keeping the house cool (Ready.gov)**

- Cover windows with curtains or shutters.
- Seal doors and windows with draught strips.
- Use reflective window film that reflects heat back outside.
- Use the oven less.

**Keeping the body cool**

| What | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Drink enough, more than usual; do not wait until you are thirsty; water is better than soft drinks | Crisis Center |
| Take a bottle of water with you if you spend a long time on the bus, train or in the car | Crisis Center |
| Wet your forehead and neck regularly | Crisis Center |
| Take a cool shower or bath | Ready.gov |
| Wear light, loose, light-coloured clothing | Crisis Center, Ready.gov |
| Wear a cap or hat; outdoors a hat that protects your face | Crisis Center, Ready.gov |
| Avoid the sun and stay indoors until it is cooler; if that is not possible, look for shade | Crisis Center |
| Avoid heavy exertion and sport, especially in the hottest part of the day | Crisis Center, Ready.gov |
| Use sunscreen with at least factor 30 | Crisis Center |
| If it is too hot at home, find a cool place such as a service centre, library or shopping centre | Crisis Center |

**Fan.** Ready.gov warns: do not rely on a fan as your main means of cooling. It gives a false sense of comfort, but does not lower body temperature and does not prevent heat illness.

**Never.** Do not leave people or animals in a parked vehicle (Crisis Center, Ready.gov).

Source: [National Crisis Center: Heatwave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/heatwave) · [Ready.gov: Extreme Heat](https://www.ready.gov/heat)

## Recognising heat illness

Ready.gov:

| | Signs | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat cramps | muscle pain or cramps in the abdomen, arms or legs | go to a cooler place, remove excess clothing, drink small sips of water or a sports drink; call your care provider if it gets worse or lasts longer than an hour |
| Heat exhaustion | heavy sweating, paleness, muscle cramps, tiredness, weakness, fast or weak pulse, dizziness, headache, fainting, nausea, vomiting | same as for heat cramps |
| Heat stroke | the source gives no separate signs here | call 112 or take the person to a hospital; move them to a cool, shady place; remove outer clothing; put cold wet cloths or ice on the head, neck, armpits and groin, or wet the clothing with cool water; raise the feet |

The Crisis Center: if you have cramps, a high fever, a headache or feel faint, call your GP. Only call 112 in a life-threatening situation.

Source: [Ready.gov: Extreme Heat](https://www.ready.gov/heat) · [National Crisis Center: Heatwave](https://crisiscenter.be/en/risks-belgium/natural-risks/heatwave)

## Heat by person

- **Older people, children and people with certain illnesses** are at greater risk (Ready.gov).
- **Spinal cord injury.** The body sometimes cannot cool down by sweating (Ready.gov).
- **Severe mental health conditions.** Medicines can interfere with the body's cooling (Ready.gov).
- **Dementia and intellectual disability.** A person may find it hard to understand or explain how the heat is affecting them (Ready.gov). Ask carers, family and friends to visit regularly.
- **Electrical medical devices.** Ask your energy supplier about priority during power cuts (Ready.gov). See the guide on medication and care.
- **Pets.** Provide plenty of cool water and shade. Tarmac can be too hot for their paws (Ready.gov).
- **Neighbours.** Check on family, older people and neighbours (Ready.gov).

After the heat: consult your GP if you continue to feel unwell, keep drinking and get enough rest (Crisis Center).
