## Which number for which emergency

In Belgium there are two numbers for urgent help. They only work if you can call: if you cannot get through, see No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage. Write the numbers down on paper in advance.

| Number | What for | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **112** | Ambulance and fire service. Also the European emergency number throughout the EU. | Free, 24 hours out of 24 |
| **101** | Urgent police assistance in Belgium | Free, 24 hours out of 24 |
| 1722 | Non-urgent fire service assistance during storms and flooding. The online desk 1722.be is always open; the number only during storms or thunderstorms. | Only active during storms |
| 1733 | Doctor on duty, for medical problems that cannot wait until your own doctor's surgery hours | Zonal rate |
| 070 245 245 | Poison Centre | Free, 24 hours out of 24 |
| 1711 | Infrabel: stop rail traffic in acute danger (a tree in the overhead line, a problem at a level crossing, people on the tracks) | |
| 04 257 66 00 | Speleohulp: underground rescues | |

**Call 112 or 101** if a building, forest or car is on fire or smoke is coming out of a building, if someone is trapped, in a serious accident with injured people, if someone is unconscious, does not stop bleeding or is choking, in a suicide attempt, in aggression and in a burglary that is in progress.

**Do not call 112** to test it, as a joke, for general information, because you cannot get back into your house, or for a taxi. For ordinary storm damage, call 1722 or, if the internet works, use 1722.be. If your car breaks down without danger, contact your breakdown service or insurer.

**Need the police? Call 101 directly.** Via 112 you are transferred, and that costs time.

**False calls and calling by accident.** Hoax callers risk prosecution and a criminal conviction, also for text messages to the emergency text numbers. In most EU countries a false call is an offence. If you called by accident, do not hang up; tell the operator that everything is fine.

## What happens when you call 112

1. Dial 112, or 101 for urgent police assistance.
2. You hear a menu: "Emergency centre. For ambulance or fire service, press 1. For police, press 2." If you make no choice, the message is repeated once and you are put through to the emergency centre responsible for the place where you are.
3. **Do not hang up.** If you hang up and call again, you go to the back of the queue.
4. Answer the operator's questions briefly and precisely.
5. Only hang up when the operator says so, and then keep your line free: the emergency centre must be able to call you back.
6. If the situation gets worse or better, call back.

No ringing tone, an engaged tone or an error message? See No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.

In the emergency centres 112 there are also medical directors and nurses, in the centres 101 police officers. The operators reassure you and tell you what to do until help arrives.

## Which questions the operator asks

The operator asks three questions. Know the answers before you call.

- **Where?** Municipality, street and house number, or a junction or conspicuous landmark. How the emergency services can get there most easily. If you are not calling from the place of the incident, say so.
- **What?** What has happened (fire, accident, aggression, heart attack) and whether there are hazards that require specialised help: someone trapped, a risk of explosion, a high-voltage cable on the ground.
- **Who?** How many casualties there are and their age, whether they are conscious and breathing normally, and which injuries you see, such as bleeding or an open fracture.

The operator can give you instructions, for example to resuscitate. That is why calling is better than a message if you can speak.

## Giving your location

Without a location, the emergency centre cannot send help. Always give the address verbally, even if your device sends your location automatically.

| Where you are | What you pass on |
| --- | --- |
| At an address | Municipality, street, house number. Say so if the address is hard to find with a satnav. |
| Flat, school, large building | Which side the emergency services should enter from, which entrance, which floor |
| On a street or in the countryside without an address | The junction, or a conspicuous building, monument or car park nearby |
| On the motorway | The number of the road (for example E40) or the usual name (for example Kennedy Tunnel), the direction of travel, the nearest kilometre marker and exit |
| In a forest or nature area | Paths, ponds, lookout towers, car parks |

**Make it easy for the emergency services:** a large house number that is legible from the street, someone waiting for the emergency services in the street (or otherwise the door open if the emergency centre asks), the outside lights on in the evening, pets in a closed room, passageways clear.

**Automatic location (AML).** Without automatic location, the emergency centre only sees the coverage area of the mobile phone mast for a mobile call, in the countryside sometimes several square kilometres. Since the end of June 2017, with AML (Advanced Mobile Location) your device sends its own position during a call to 112 or 101, up to 4,000 times more precisely. In 2017 this worked on Android with a Belgian SIM card; for the iPhone you needed the 112 BE app. So always give your location verbally as well.

## No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage

### Your device

- With a SIM card in your phone you can call 112, even if the device is locked. In Belgium a SIM card is needed, but not an active subscription or registration.
- If you see "network not available, emergency calls only", there is an outage on your own network and you can temporarily only make emergency calls. That is not a reason to call 112.

Source: [112.be: Frequently asked questions](https://112.be/en/frequently-asked-questions) · [Vlaanderen.be: Emergency calls](https://www.vlaanderen.be/noodoproepen)

### During a power cut

- Mobile phone masts can run for one to two hours without electricity. Mobile internet drops out quickly, probably straight away.
- As long as your landline gives a dial tone, you can call the emergency services, but it is unlikely that the landline will still work then.
- With enough signal you can call 112, whichever operator you are with. Whether 101 remains reachable is not guaranteed.
- Do not call or browse unnecessarily, so that the network stays free for important calls.

### If 112 does not answer

112 is in principle always reachable: if it takes a while, keep waiting. If you hear no ringing tone, no announcement, an engaged tone or an error message, do the following, in this order:

1. Try **another device**, landline or mobile, or another operator (Orange, Proximus, Telenet). Ask neighbours or bystanders to call.
2. If you are calling from a mobile and have no other device: **move a few metres** or turn around, and try again.
3. If you can make calls but 112 cannot be reached: call **101**.
4. If 112 and 101 cannot be reached because of a technical problem, **three temporary mobile numbers** can be activated per province. They are usually not active. Only use them in an emergency.
5. If those cannot be reached either: call a **hospital**, the **fire service zone** or the **local police** directly.

### If you cannot call at all

If no device works any more, ask for help in your neighbourhood or, if you can, go to the nearest police station, fire station or hospital. The map in the app shows them, with the Nearest button. In a major outage the municipality may open an information and reception point. A power cut in itself is not an emergency.

**With an injured or sick person**

- Only leave the casualty alone if there is no other option.
- Shout loudly for help and ask a bystander directly to fetch help. Do not assume that someone else is already doing so.
- Tell whoever goes for help where it is, what has happened, how many casualties there are and what state they are in.
- With a group, send two or more people to raise the alarm; the others stay with the injured person.
- If you are alone with the injured person, first help them as well as you can and then go and look for help.
- If you need to resuscitate and you are alone without a phone: for an adult, fetch help first and then start resuscitation; for a child, resuscitate for 1 minute first and then go and fetch help.
- Find a place with coverage, or ask someone to go there and call. Anyone who is not calling from the place of the incident should tell the operator so.
- In open terrain, with nobody nearby: see the guide Signals and emergency codes.

**Help and information in your neighbourhood**

- Ask neighbours and bystanders for help, and look out for anyone who needs extra help.
- Follow the news on a battery radio, a wind-up radio or the car radio.
- The authorities can inform people with police loudspeaker vans, information boards, leaflets and door-to-door communication.
- Mayors and governors have prepared emergency measures for a load-shedding power cut, such as an information and reception point. Ask your municipality in advance where that point will be.
- In larger emergencies the municipality or the province sets up a reception centre, for example in a sports hall, with a contact person who gives information.

### Arrange in advance

- Make a paper list with the temporary mobile numbers for your province and the numbers of your hospital, your fire service zone and your local police. Keep it with your emergency plan.

- Agree with your family where you will meet, and with your neighbours how you will reach each other without a phone or internet.

## In a major disaster or storm

- In large-scale disasters the operator only asks you for additional, usable information. If you have nothing new, hang up as soon as the operator says so.
- If the RMI announces code orange or red and you need the fire service, but nobody's life is in danger: call 1722 or, if the internet works, use 1722.be.
- If someone's life may be in danger, always call 112. Keep 112 free for such calls.

## The 112 BE app

The 112 BE app is the official app of the Belgian emergency centres, from the FPS Home Affairs. You do not need to remember a number: you tap fire service, ambulance or police.

- **Location.** The emergency centre sees where you are. With internet, the app sends your position, even if you are moving. Without internet, the emergency data go via a free text message.
- **Extra information.** The emergency centre immediately sees what you filled in when registering, for example that you are deaf, a heart patient or allergic to certain medication. These data are not systematically kept, only sent along when you call via the app.
- **Alarm.** If you are unconscious and the emergency services cannot find you, the emergency centre can make your mobile phone ring.
- **Chat.** If you cannot speak or hear, the emergency centre can activate the chat.

**Register now**, not during an emergency, and switch on the location function. The app is free, works **only in Belgium** and is available in Dutch, French, German and English. It needs the mobile network: if that drops out, see No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.

**If you cannot talk**, for example because you are injured or being threatened, call via the app or let someone else call.

## Deaf, hard of hearing or a speech disorder

You can reach the emergency centres in two ways without speaking. Both work **only in Belgium** and only if the mobile network works.

1. **The 112 BE app with chat (best choice).** When registering, tick your impairment. The chat then starts automatically when you make an emergency call via the app, and your location is sent along.
2. **The text message service.** There is one text message number for fire service and ambulance and one for police. Only use the latter if you need only the police. You request the numbers by text message or email, and in doing so declare on your word of honour that you cannot reach the emergency centres by an ordinary voice call. You need a **Belgian SIM card**. As long as your impairment lasts, you may send text messages from any device and any mobile number. The emergency centres **cannot receive MMS**: do not send a photo. Questions about the service: 112@ibz.fgov.be.

The text message numbers are not on the public page. Request them before you need them and save them in your device.

**If you can call, call:** that way the operator can respond faster and guide you, for example with resuscitation.

**Without a network.** Agree with your neighbours in advance how you will communicate without a phone or internet.

## From abroad

- 112 works free of charge in all 27 member states of the European Union and in some countries outside it, such as Switzerland and South Africa. In Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Malta, the Netherlands, Portugal, Romania and Sweden it is the only national emergency number.
- You reach the emergency centre of the region where you are. You **cannot call a Belgian emergency centre from abroad**, nor a foreign one from Belgium. If help is needed elsewhere, ask someone who is there to call.
- Speak the language of the country, or otherwise English: in 21 member states you can be helped in English, in some also in French, German or the language of a neighbouring country.
- The 112 BE app and the text message service for deaf people do **not** work abroad. If you are going outside the EU, look up the emergency number of that country before you leave.

## In the car: eCall and the motorway

**eCall** automatically calls 112 after a serious accident. It works free of charge in all EU countries, wherever the car was bought or registered.

- The system starts automatically, or with a button. You talk to the nearest emergency centre, which receives the exact location, the time of the accident, the vehicle identification number and the direction of travel.
- Outside a serious accident, eCall is not active: your car is not tracked.
- Mandatory for new car models approved after 31 March 2018, for vehicles with up to 8 seats and light commercial vehicles.
- Now that 2G and 3G networks are being switched off, there are gaps in the eCall service. So also call yourself if you can.

**On the motorway in Flanders**

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| You have broken down yourself, in a lane or on the hard shoulder | Call 101. The traffic police automatically call out an F.A.S.T. towing service. |
| Serious accident with injured people, a vehicle fire or a major hazard (an object on the road) | Call 112 |
| A vehicle is a hazard or disturbs public order | Call 101 |
| Breakdown without danger | Contact your breakdown service or insurer yourself. Do not call 112. |

Give your location as in Giving your location. Do not use your phone while driving, not even in a traffic jam.

## Teaching children to call

Children can learn to call 112 as early as the first and second cycle of primary school.

**What a child needs to know**

1. The number 112 by heart.
2. When to call: in case of severe bleeding, someone who does not respond, a bad fall, fire or a mouthful of a poisonous product, when there is no other adult who can help.
3. What to say: **where**, **what**, **who**.
4. Not to hang up until the operator says so, and never to call 112 as a joke.
5. That they can also alert an adult or a doctor, as Jeugd Rode Kruis teaches.

**How to practise**

- Fill in the 112.be address template and hang it by the phone.
- Watch the 112 kids film together and do the quiz on 112.be.
- Act out a conversation with a switched-off phone: you are the operator.
- Called by accident? Do not hang up, but say that everything is fine.
- Teach older children that 112 also works with a parent's locked mobile phone, if there is a SIM card in it.

## Costs and privacy

- Calling 112 and 101 is free. You pay yourself for transport and care by an ambulance. The amount is set by royal decree; 112.be lists a flat rate of 75.36 euros.
- Emergency calls, including messages via the app and text messages, may be recorded and kept for up to one year so they can be listened to again. Operators are bound by confidentiality.

Source: [112.be: Frequently asked questions](https://112.be/en/frequently-asked-questions) · [112.be: For deaf people](https://112.be/en/deaf-people)

## Preparing per person

- **Everyone:** see Arrange in advance, make your house number clearly visible and register in the 112 BE app.
- **Older people and people who live alone:** put the most important numbers on paper by the phone and agree with a neighbour who will wait for the emergency services.
- **People with a chronic illness or allergy:** fill these in when registering in the app.
- **Deaf people, people who are hard of hearing, people with a speech disorder:** see Deaf, hard of hearing or a speech disorder.
- **Speakers of other languages and visitors:** learn 112. In Belgium you can speak English if necessary.

These lines are not for acute emergencies. In a life-threatening situation or a suicide attempt, call 112 (see Which number for which emergency). During the crisis, support is what people on the spot do for each other: see the guide Mental resilience.

| 106 | Tele-Onthaal | Free, anonymous, 24 hours out of 24 |
| 102 | Awel, helpline for children and young people | Free, anonymous |
