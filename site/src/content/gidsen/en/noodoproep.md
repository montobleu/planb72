## Which number for which emergency

In Belgium there are two numbers for urgent help. The other numbers in the table are for situations that are not life-threatening or for a specific problem.

All these numbers only work if you can call. If you cannot get through, read No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage further on in this guide. Write the numbers down on a paper list in advance.

| Number | What for | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **112** | Ambulance and fire service. Also the European emergency number throughout the EU. | Free, 24 hours out of 24 |
| **101** | Urgent police assistance in Belgium | Free, 24 hours out of 24 |
| 1722 | Non-urgent fire service assistance during storms and flooding. The online desk 1722.be is always open; the number only during storms or thunderstorms. | Only active during storms |
| 1733 | Doctor on duty, for medical problems that cannot wait until your own doctor's surgery hours | Zonal rate |
| 070 245 245 | Poison Centre | Free, 24 hours out of 24 |
| 1711 | Infrabel: stop rail traffic in acute danger (a tree in the overhead line, a problem at a level crossing, people on the tracks) | |
| 04 257 66 00 | Speleohulp: underground rescues | |

Helplines such as Tele-Onthaal, the Zelfmoordlijn and Awel are listed at the end, in After the crisis, when the phone works again.

**Call 112 or 101 if** a building, forest or car is on fire or smoke is coming out of a building, if someone is trapped, in a serious accident with injured people, if someone is unconscious, does not stop bleeding or is choking, in a suicide attempt, in aggression or a fight, and in a burglary that is in progress.

**Do not call 112** to test whether the number works, as a joke, for general information, because you cannot get back into your house, or for a taxi. Do not call for ordinary storm damage either: call 1722 instead or, if the internet works, use the online desk 1722.be. If your car breaks down without danger, contact your breakdown service or insurer yourself.

**Need the police? Call 101 directly.** If you call 112 for the police, you are transferred to 101. That costs time.

## What happens when you call 112

1. Dial 112. For urgent police assistance, dial 101.
2. You hear a menu: "Emergency centre. For ambulance or fire service, press 1. For police, press 2."
3. If you make no choice, the message is repeated once. After that you are automatically put through to the emergency centre responsible for the place where you are.
4. Wait calmly for an answer. **Do not hang up.** If you hang up and call again, you go to the back of the queue.
5. The operator asks questions. Answer briefly and precisely.
6. Only hang up when the operator says you may. After that, keep your line free: the emergency centre must be able to call you back.
7. If the situation gets worse or better, call back to report it.

If you hear no ringing tone, no announcement, an engaged tone or an error message, follow the steps in No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.

In every provincial capital and in Brussels there is an emergency centre 112 and a communication and information centre 101. Walloon Brabant has no emergency centre 112 of its own; the surrounding centres take over those calls. About 650 operators and call takers of the FPS Home Affairs work there. In the emergency centres 112 there are also medical directors and nurses, in the centres 101 police officers. The operators reassure you and tell you what to do until help arrives.

Preferably speak the language of the country you are in. If you do not speak it, give the information in English, for example.

## Which questions the operator asks

The Red Cross and 112.be use three questions: **where, what, who**. Try to know these answers before you call.

**Where?**

- Municipality or town, street and house number.
- Or: the junction of two roads, or a conspicuous landmark (shopping centre, monument, town hall).
- How the emergency services can get there most easily, and whether there is a special access route.
- If you are not calling from the place of the incident, say so explicitly.

**What?**

- What has happened: fire, road accident, aggression, burglary, heart attack.
- Whether there are hazards that require specialised help: someone trapped, a risk of explosion, a high-voltage cable on the ground.

**Who?**

- How many casualties there are, and whether people are in danger.
- How they are: are they conscious, are they breathing normally?
- Which injuries you see, for example bleeding or an open fracture.
- Whether they are adults or children, and their age.

Keep your message short but meaningful. The operator can also give you instructions, for example to resuscitate. That is why an ordinary phone call is better than a message if you can speak.

If someone else has to go and fetch help because you cannot call, give them the same answers: where, what, who.

## Giving your location

Your location is the most important thing you pass on. Without a location, the emergency centre cannot send help. Even if your device sends your location automatically, you must state or confirm the address verbally.

| Where you are | What you pass on |
| --- | --- |
| At home or at an address | Municipality, street, house number. Say so if the address is hard to find with a satnav. |
| Flat, school, large building, shopping centre | Which side the emergency services should enter from, which entrance, which floor |
| On a street without a house number | The junction, or a conspicuous building or monument nearby |
| On the motorway | The number of the motorway (for example E40), the usual name (for example Kennedy Tunnel), the direction of travel, the number of the nearest kilometre marker, the nearest exit |
| In a forest or nature area | Recognisable points: paths, ponds, lookout towers, car parks |
| In the countryside without an address | Landmarks nearby, such as a car park or a monument |

**Make it easy for the emergency services**

- Make sure your house number is large, legible and visible from the street.
- Have someone wait for the emergency services at the front door or in the street and show them the way.
- If that is not possible, open the door if the emergency centre asks you to.
- Switch on the outside lights in the evening and at night.
- Put pets in a closed room.
- Clear passageways.

**Why the location matters so much.** Without automatic location, the emergency centre only sees the coverage area of the mobile phone mast for a mobile call. In the countryside that can be an area of several square kilometres. The operator then has to keep asking about landmarks until the location is known.

## No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage

### Your device

- If there is a SIM card in your phone, you can call 112, even if your device is still locked.
- In Belgium a SIM card is needed to call the emergency numbers from a mobile phone. An active subscription or registration is not needed.
- If you see "network not available, emergency calls only" on your screen, there is an outage on your own network and you can temporarily only make emergency calls. That is not a reason to call 112.

Source: [112.be: Frequently asked questions](https://112.be/en/frequently-asked-questions) · [Vlaanderen.be: Emergency calls](https://www.vlaanderen.be/noodoproepen)

### During a power cut

- Mobile phone masts can run for one to two hours without electricity. Mobile internet drops out quickly, probably straight away.
- As long as your landline gives a dial tone, you can call the emergency services. It is unlikely that the landline will still work then.
- If you have enough signal, you can call 112, whichever operator you are with. Whether 101 remains reachable is not guaranteed.
- Only call if it is really necessary. Do not call or browse unnecessarily: that keeps the network free for important calls.

### If 112 does not answer

112 is in principle always reachable. If it takes a while, keep waiting. If you hear no ringing tone, no announcement, an engaged tone or an error message, do the following, in this order:

1. Try **another device**, landline or mobile. Another operator (Orange, Proximus, Telenet) can help. Ask neighbours or bystanders to help and to call.
2. If you have no other device and you are calling from a mobile: **move a few metres** or turn around, and try again.
3. If you can make calls but 112 cannot be reached: call **101**.
4. If 112 and 101 cannot be reached because of a technical problem, **three temporary mobile numbers** can be activated per province. These only work during such an outage and are usually not active. Only use them in an emergency.
5. If you can still make calls but those numbers cannot be reached either: call a **hospital**, the **fire service zone** or the **local police** directly, depending on what has happened.

### If you cannot call at all

If no device works any more, the Belgian government says: ask for help in your neighbourhood and follow the information the authorities put out.

**With an injured or sick person**

- Only leave the casualty alone if there is no other option.
- Shout loudly for help. Speak to a bystander directly and ask them to fetch help. Do not assume that someone else is already doing so.
- Tell whoever goes for help where it is, what has happened, how many casualties there are and what state they are in.
- If you are with a group, send two or more people to raise the alarm. The others stay with the injured person.
- If you are alone with the injured person, first help them as well as you can and then go and look for help.
- If you need to resuscitate and you are alone without a phone: for an adult, fetch help first and then start resuscitation. For a child, resuscitate for 1 minute first and then go and fetch help.
- Find a place with coverage, or ask someone you meet to go there and call. Anyone who is not calling from the place of the incident should tell the operator so.
- In open terrain, with nobody nearby: see the guide Signals and emergency codes.

### Advice from abroad

We did not find an official Belgian source that sends you to a fire station, a police station or a hospital in the event of a complete outage. Authorities in other countries do give that advice.

- **Germany.** The BBK advises: if the emergency numbers 110 and 112 no longer work, go to the nearest police station or to the hospital in an emergency, if you can. Find out in advance where you can get information locally, for example at the town hall or at an emergency meeting point (Notfalltreffpunkt). Ask your municipality where you can report an emergency if the telephone network fails. A power cut in itself is not an emergency.
- **Netherlands.** During the nationwide 112 outage of 24 June 2019, the government used NL-Alert to send anyone with an urgent report to the nearest fire station or police station. Citizens could also approach police officers in the street. For medical emergencies they had to go to the nearest hospital. Not all regions had staffed their fire stations at the time.
- **Sweden.** The Swedish authorities advise: if 112 does not work, go to the nearest fire station. In many places fire stations are used for this. Municipalities can also designate places where you can make an emergency call.

The map in the app (Nearest button) shows fire services, police, hospitals and town halls near you.

### Arrange in advance

- Make a paper list of important phone numbers: the temporary mobile numbers for your province and the numbers of your hospital, your fire service zone and your local police. Keep them with your emergency plan.
- Ask your municipality what arrangements it makes if the power is cut, for example where the information and reception point will be.
- Agree with your family where you will meet if you cannot reach each other.
- Agree with your neighbours how you will reach each other without a phone or internet.
- Get a battery radio or a wind-up radio, preferably with DAB+ and FM.

## In a major disaster or storm

- In large-scale disasters many people call at the same time. The operator then only asks you for additional, usable information. If you have nothing new, hang up as soon as the operator says so. This frees the lines faster.
- If the RMI announces code orange or red for a storm or flooding and you need the fire service, but nobody's life is in danger: call 1722 or, if the internet works, use the online desk 1722.be.
- If someone's life may be in danger, always call 112. If that fails, see No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.
- Keep 112 free for people whose life is in danger. Only call 112 in immediate danger and limit your phone use, so that the network does not become overloaded.

## Automatic location (AML)

Since the end of June 2017, the emergency centres 112 and 101 can locate a call automatically with AML (Advanced Mobile Location). Your device then sends its own position during an emergency call. According to the FPS Home Affairs, this is up to 4,000 times more precise than the classic location via the mobile phone mast.

What the 2017 press release says:

- At that time AML worked on Android devices with a Belgian SIM card.
- For the iPhone, you then had to use the 112 BE app to pass on your location automatically.
- The location data are only sent when you call 112 or 101. They serve only to find the incident faster and are not kept by your provider or the emergency services.
- An example from that press release: walkers in the province of Liège who had a child with a broken leg could not give an address, but were found through the automatic location. The emergency services were sent to the nearest road.

This press release dates from 2017. Whether the situation for iPhones has changed since then is not stated on 112.be. So do not assume that your location comes through automatically: always give it verbally as well.

Source: [FPS Home Affairs: press release on AML and the 112 BE app (2017)](http://kcce.eu/sites/default/files/persbericht_2017-07-13-localisatie_app112be_2.pdf)

## The 112 BE app

The 112 BE app is the official app of the Belgian emergency centres, from the FPS Home Affairs. Its biggest advantage: you do not need to remember a number. You tap the icon for fire service, ambulance or police.

| Function | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| One app, three emergency services | You choose fire service, ambulance or police yourself |
| Location | The emergency centre sees where you are. With internet, the app sends your position during the call, even if you are moving. |
| Extra information | The emergency centre immediately knows who you are and what you filled in when registering, for example that you are deaf, a heart patient or allergic to certain medication |
| Alarm | If you are unconscious and the emergency services cannot find you, the emergency centre can make your mobile phone ring |
| Chat | If you cannot speak or hear, the emergency centre can activate the chat function |
| Without internet | According to the developer, if there is no internet the emergency data go to the emergency centre via a free text message |
| Other numbers | A second page with useful numbers and websites, including 1722 |

**Important to know**

- You must **register** first. Do it now, not during an emergency.
- Switch on the **location function** of your mobile phone, and the sound if needed.
- The data you fill in when registering are not systematically kept in the emergency centres. They are sent along when you call via the app.
- The app works **only in Belgium**.
- The app is free, just like the emergency numbers. It is available in Dutch, French, German and English.
- The app needs the mobile network: with mobile internet, or without internet via text message. If the network drops out, follow No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.

**Who benefits most from the app?** Everyone who might forget the number in a panic, people who cannot speak or hear or have difficulty doing so, people with an illness or allergy that the emergency services need to know about immediately, and those who are often in forests, in the countryside or on the move, where there is no address.

## Deaf, hard of hearing or a speech disorder

If you are deaf or hard of hearing or have a speech disorder, you can reach the emergency centres in two ways without speaking. Both work **only in Belgium** and only if the mobile network works.

**1. The 112 BE app with chat (best choice)**

- When registering, tick that you are deaf or hard of hearing or have a speech disorder.
- The chat is then activated automatically when you make an emergency call via the app. You communicate with the emergency centre through messages.
- Your location is sent along.

**2. The text message service**

- There is one text message number for fire service and ambulance, and one for police. Only use the police number if you need only the police. In all other cases, use the number for fire service and ambulance.
- You request these specific numbers by text message or email. In doing so you declare on your word of honour that you cannot reach the emergency centres by an ordinary voice call.
- You need a **Belgian SIM card**.
- As long as you are deaf or hard of hearing or have a speech impairment, you may send a text message from any device and any mobile number.
- The emergency centres **cannot receive MMS**. So do not send a photo.
- Questions or comments about the service: 112@ibz.fgov.be.

**If you can call, call.** The emergency centres explicitly ask you not to use the chat or text messages if an ordinary call is possible. Verbally, the operator can respond faster and give medical guidance, such as resuscitation instructions.

The text message numbers themselves are not on the public page. Request them before you need them and save them in your device.

**Without a network.** Think in advance about other ways to communicate with others, including when you have no phone or internet. Agree with your neighbours how you will reach each other then. If you cannot get through, see No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.

## If you cannot talk

There are situations in which you cannot or must not speak, for example because you are injured or because someone is threatening you.

| Situation | What the sources say |
| --- | --- |
| You have the 112 BE app | Call via the app. The emergency centre sees where you are and, in exceptional cases, can activate the chat if you cannot speak. |
| You are deaf, hard of hearing or have a speech disorder | Use the app with chat or the text message service (see above) |
| You have lost consciousness and the app is installed | The emergency centre can make your mobile phone ring to find you |
| Someone else can call | Let that person call. With a casualty: one person stays with the casualty, another calls. |

A silent emergency call without the app, as in some other countries, is not described on 112.be. So do not assume that it exists.

## From abroad

- 112 works free of charge in all 27 member states of the European Union. It is also used in some countries outside the EU, such as Switzerland and South Africa.
- In Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Malta, the Netherlands, Portugal, Romania and Sweden, 112 is the only national emergency number. Elsewhere it works alongside the national numbers.
- You always reach the emergency centre of the region where you are at that moment.
- You **cannot call a Belgian emergency centre from abroad**, nor a foreign emergency centre from Belgium. If help is needed in a region other than where you are, ask a relative or acquaintance who is there to call.
- Speak the language of the country. If that does not work, use English: in 21 member states you can be helped in English. Some countries also help in French, German or the language of a neighbouring country.
- If you travel to another EU country, your provider must send you a text message with information about 112.
- The 112 BE app and the text message service for deaf people do **not** work abroad.
- If you are going outside the EU, look up the emergency number of that country before you leave.

## In the car: eCall and the motorway

**eCall** is a system in the car that automatically calls 112 after a serious accident. It works free of charge in all EU countries, wherever the car was bought or registered.

- The system starts automatically in a serious accident. You can also start it yourself with a button.
- You are connected to the nearest emergency centre and can talk to the operator.
- The emergency centre receives a minimum set of data: the exact location, the time of the accident, the vehicle identification number and the direction of travel.
- Outside a serious accident, eCall is not active. Your car is not tracked.
- Mandatory for new car models approved after 31 March 2018, for vehicles with up to 8 seats and light commercial vehicles.
- If the system does not work, you receive a warning.
- A manufacturer or breakdown service can offer its own eCall service. It may be a paid service and must automatically fall back on the ordinary eCall if it fails.
- According to the European Commission there are gaps in the eCall service now that 2G and 3G networks are being switched off. So also call yourself if you can.

**On the motorway in Flanders**

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| You have broken down yourself, in a lane or on the hard shoulder | Call 101. The traffic police automatically call out an F.A.S.T. towing service. |
| You witness a serious accident with injured people, or a major hazard (an object on the road, a burning vehicle) | Call 112 |
| Serious accident with injured people or a vehicle fire | 112 (ambulance, fire service) |
| A vehicle is a hazard or disturbs public order | 101 |
| Breakdown without danger | Contact your breakdown service or insurer yourself. Do not call 112. |

On the motorway, give the number of the road, the direction of travel, the nearest kilometre marker and exit. Do not use your phone while driving, not even in a traffic jam. If you cannot get through, see No coverage, no SIM card or a network outage.

## Teaching children to call

Research that the Red Cross refers to shows that children in the first and second cycle of primary school can learn how to call 112. 112.be tells the story of a six-year-old boy from Spa who saved his mother because he knew how to call 112.

**What a child needs to know**

1. The number 112 by heart.
2. When to call 112: in case of severe bleeding, someone who does not respond, a bad fall, fire, a mouthful of a poisonous product. When there is no other adult who can help.
3. What to say: **where** you are, **what** has happened, **who** is injured.
4. Not to hang up until the operator says so.
5. Never to call 112 as a joke.
6. That they can also alert an adult or a doctor. Jeugd Rode Kruis names these, alongside the emergency centre 112, as help you can alert.

**How to practise**

- Fill in the 112.be template with your address and hang it by the phone. A child must be able to give their address or find it quickly.
- Watch the 112 kids film together and do the quiz on 112.be. There are also a memory game, a bingo and drawings.
- Act out a conversation with a switched-off phone: you are the operator, the child answers where, what and who.
- Teach a child that calling 112 by accident is not a disaster: do not hang up, but say that everything is fine.
- Also teach older children to use a parent's mobile phone when it is locked: with a SIM card in the device, 112 still works.

## False calls and calling by accident

- Anyone who calls the emergency services without a valid reason keeps the line busy for people who are really in need.
- Hoax callers expose themselves to prosecution, which can lead to a criminal conviction. This also applies to text messages to the emergency text numbers.
- According to the European Commission, false calls are an offence in most countries.
- If you called **by accident**, do not hang up. Tell the operator that everything is fine. That way the operator knows for certain that there is no emergency.
- Never test the emergency numbers.

## Costs and privacy

- Calling 112 and 101 is free.
- You pay yourself for transport and care by an ambulance after a 112 call. The amount is set by royal decree; 112.be lists a flat rate of 75.36 euros. Your health insurance fund can explain more.
- Emergency calls may be recorded and kept for up to one year, so that those responsible can listen to them again. This also applies to messages via the app and text messages. Operators are bound by confidentiality.
