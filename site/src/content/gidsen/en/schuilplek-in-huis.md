## Why you choose the place in advance

With many threats you have little time. Finland says that in an air threat there is very little time between the alarm and the actual danger, and that you therefore shelter where you are at that moment. In sparsely populated areas and in neighbourhoods with detached houses, people almost always shelter on the spot, according to the Finnish authorities. Those who know in advance which room in the house is best lose no time.

The National Crisis Center recommends making an emergency plan with the people you live with, discussing the three steps of sheltering in advance with family, neighbours or colleagues, and practising the plan regularly. Finland recommends learning how to switch off the ventilation, at home and in buildings where you often are. Also agree with your neighbours how you will reach each other if the telephone or internet does not work (Crisis Center).

A shelter at home does not replace a real civil defence shelter. The Czech authorities developed their guide precisely because permanent civil defence shelters cover only a small part of the population and an improvised shelter (improvizovaný úkryt) is then the solution. The Ukrainian civil protection service says that the two-wall rule does not give complete safety.

Note: the Czech guide is an archived version from 2001 on the website of the Czech Ministry of the Interior. It is aimed at authorities, companies and self-employed people, but also describes rooms in homes.

## One home, several places

Do not choose one place, but two or three, because the best place depends on the threat.

| Threat | Best place in your home | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air attack, explosion | Cellar without windows; otherwise an inner room with two walls between you and the outside | Attic, rooms with windows, crawl space |
| Radioactive fallout | Belgian advice: central room on the ground floor; Germany: preferably the cellar | Rooms along the outer wall, near windows |
| Chemical cloud | Belgian advice: central room on the ground floor, well sealed | Cellar (gases sink downwards, BBK) |
| Flood | Highest floor | Cellar, basement |
| Storm, tornado | Inner room away from windows and outer walls | Near windows, under the roof |

In Belgium the general advice for sheltering is: a central room on the ground floor, away from windows and outside doors. The cellar is therefore mainly an option for explosions and air attacks, and according to the German guideline also for radioactive substances.

## The criteria

These criteria come from the German, Czech, Dutch (TNO) and Ukrainian guidelines. They are mainly about protection against explosions and radiation.

### Depth and location

- **Underground is best.** A cellar offers effective protection because of its sheltered position (TNO). Against radiation, the shielding in cellars is particularly high because of the surrounding earth and the storeys above (BBK).
- **As deep as possible in the ground and in the middle of the building.** Czechia mentions a cellar in the central part of the building, dug in as deep as possible, with vaulted or reinforced concrete ceilings and thick outer walls.
- **The floor above the groundwater level** (Czechia).

### Mass and construction

TNO names three properties: a strong structure (thick walls, columns, floors and ceilings, often in concrete), robust walls (thick, few openings, little glass) and a sheltered position. The Czech guide gives minimum thicknesses for the outer walls of a cellar shelter: 45 cm of masonry or 30 cm of reinforced concrete. Frame construction in reinforced concrete or steel and solid buildings in brick or stone are the most suitable. According to Czechia, buildings with a lot of combustible material and buildings with only one or two storeys are unsuitable.

### Number of walls between you and the outside

Make sure there are two walls between you and the outside. If the outer wall is damaged, the second wall protects you against fragments and debris. The deeper the room lies in the building, the better (BBK).

### Windows and glass

Inner rooms with as few outer walls and doors as possible and without windows protect well, because shards of glass cannot then cause injuries (BBK). Avoid rooms with a direct line of sight to the outside (TNO).

### Exits

- A shelter in a block of buildings must have at least two emergency exits or other escape routes (Czechia). That way you do not get trapped if the main entrance is buried under debris.
- Ukraine asks for at least two exits, or the possibility of making a second exit or route, and exits that are usable, clear and not permanently locked.
- The entrance door must open outwards (Czechia).

### What is above and next to it

Czechia says that you should not set up a shelter:

- under rooms where more than 1,000 kg per square metre is stored;
- under production or storage areas with tanks of dangerous chemical liquids or molten metal;
- within 100 metres of reservoirs of dangerous substances or heavy gases;
- within 50 metres of companies or storage facilities with a risk of explosion or highly flammable substances;
- above pipes carrying high-pressure steam, high voltage or compressed air.

Ukraine adds: no room where fuel, gas cylinders, chemicals or combustible materials are kept. TNO recommends keeping your distance from gas, electricity and technical rooms.

### What you had better not choose

- **Attic and top floor:** a roof offers little protection against blast waves, glass and debris (BBK).
- **Crawl space** (TNO).
- **Kitchen:** glass, heavy appliances and other hazards (Ukraine).
- **Bathroom** only as a last resort, and only without a gas boiler or gas water heater. Tiles and mirrors can break (Ukraine).
- **Rooms with windows, balconies or loggias**, or directly opposite windows and large mirrors (Ukraine).

## Per type of home

### House with a cellar

1. **Best choice:** a cellar room without windows, under the middle of the house, directly accessible from the stairs (Czechia).
2. **Good choice:** a cellar room with a small window that you shield (see below).
3. **If the cellar is not an option** (flood, gas leak, chemical cloud): an inner room on the ground floor or, in the case of water, the upper floor.

### House without a cellar

1. **Best choice:** an inner room without windows: a hallway, understairs cupboard or storeroom in the middle of the house (BBK).
2. **Good choice:** the room with the fewest outer walls and the least glass.
3. **Emergency solution:** behind a robust object, such as a kitchen unit or the parapet of an outer wall (TNO).

### Flat

1. **Best choice in an air attack:** the cellar or underground car park of the building, if you can reach it quickly.
2. **Good choice in the flat:** the hallway, entrance hall or cloakroom, if it lies more towards the middle of the flat, or a storeroom without windows. Ukraine prefers the hallway to the bathroom.
3. **Stairwell:** a fallback option if there are no large windows or glass walls and you can stay against an inner wall. If the stairwell has a lot of glass, go back to the hallway of your flat (Ukraine).
4. **Position:** sit or crouch by a load-bearing inner wall, away from windows, balconies, mirrors and glass (Ukraine). TNO: as low as possible, preferably lying down, head and vital parts protected with your arms or blankets.

Ukraine warns that the cellars of some older blocks of flats are small and narrow, often contain pipes and cannot take any extra load. Old cellars can leak, be damp, contain hazardous substances or be poorly ventilated. Unfinished buildings are also not recommended: weak ceilings, no doors, poor ventilation.

## Shielding windows and doors

### Against glass and debris (explosion, air attack)

- Close windows, doors and shutters. Cover them, for example with curtains (TNO).
- Use mass as extra shielding: tables, mattresses or other sturdy furniture (TNO).
- Ukraine: if you have doubts about the protection, for example with thin walls, reinforce them with sandbags, bags of earth or concrete blocks.

### Against radiation (cellar)

Czechia recommends blocking cellar windows with bricks, stones or bags of sand or earth, at least to the thickness of the outer wall. Cover them on the outside with planks, boards or sheet steel and cover these with earth, sand or gravel. Leave room for ventilation when doing so.

### Against gas and dust (chemical cloud, radioactive cloud)

- Close all windows and doors and all openings where outside air comes in: broken windows, open fireplaces, the cooker hood in the kitchen. Make the bottom of doors draught-proof with damp cloths (Nuclear risk, frequently asked questions).
- Close the ventilation slots in window frames (BBK).
- Seal windows, doors and air vents with thick plastic sheeting and tape. Measure and cut the sheeting in advance, a few centimetres wider than the opening, and label each piece. Tape the corners first, then all the edges (Ready.gov).
- Gaps around cooker and dryer vents can be sealed with wet towels, sheeting, tape, wax paper or aluminium foil (Ready.gov).
- Czechia mentions as sealing material: tape, self-adhesive foam strip, plaster, sheeting, gypsum, putty, modelling clay or clay.

### Doors

Czechia recommends reinforcing the entrance door with planks and crossbars, making it gas-tight with sealing material and an adapted threshold, and, once the shelter is occupied, securing it with beams and wire against being sucked open after a blast wave. Ukraine: the door does not need to be airtight, but must be strong enough to withstand a blast wave.

### Propping up ceilings

The Czech guide describes how to prop up cellar ceilings with wooden beams and posts. It expressly adds that you should have any reinforcement of ceilings assessed by a structural engineer, and that you should not prop up vaults that rest in the walls. Never knock out a load-bearing wall to obtain material.

## Ventilation

Here two aims conflict: keeping outside air out and keeping enough oxygen.

- **In a chemical or radioactive cloud:** switch off ventilation, air conditioning and heating that draw in outside air (Crisis Center). Ready.gov: if that is not possible, set the system to 100 per cent recirculation.
- **Do make sure the air is refreshed,** so that you do not feel unwell (Nuclear risk, frequently asked questions).
- **A completely sealed room without a filter installation** can only be used for a limited time, depending on the size of the room and the number of people. Czechia warns of poisoning by exhaled carbon dioxide.
- **Ready.gov** calls sealing a room a temporary measure.
- **No candles:** they use up oxygen unnecessarily (BBK). Czechia: open flames only in exceptional cases and only at the ventilation outlet. Do not smoke.
- **In an air attack without gas:** Ukraine asks that the air circulates freely, without strong smells, smoke or stuffiness. For more than 50 people, natural ventilation is often not enough.

## What to have ready

### Basics according to the Crisis Center

- Drinking water: between 3 and 10 litres per person per day. 3 litres is just enough for drinking and cooking.
- Long-life food that you can prepare without water, gas or electricity, and a tin opener or pocket knife.
- A battery-powered or wind-up radio, preferably with DAB+ and FM, rechargeable batteries and spare batteries. That way you stay informed even without electricity: in a power cut, the internet and mobile networks can also fail.
- A paper list of important telephone numbers.
- A torch, preferably wind-up. Warm clothing or blankets.
- Iodine tablets. Never take them on your own initiative.
- Baby food, nappies and toys if you have young children. Food and equipment for pets.
- A small first aid kit, medication for at least three days, a whistle and a charged power bank.
- Enough water and food to manage on your own for at least 72 hours.

### What not to take

Finland prohibits alcohol, drugs, weapons, devices that produce heat (gas heaters, storm lanterns) and strongly scented products in civil defence shelters. Ukraine also mentions substances with a strong smell.

### Where to keep it

Keep everything together in a fixed, easily accessible place and make sure everyone knows where it is. Store water and food in a cool, dark place and check regularly that everything is still usable (Crisis Center). Switzerland recommends placing the emergency supply in or near the shelter early on when a conflict threatens.

## Behaviour while sheltering

- Follow the instructions of the authorities on a battery-powered or wind-up radio. Also watch for police loudspeaker vans in the street. If the network works, the instructions also come via BE-Alert and official websites (Crisis Center).
- Do not make unnecessary calls. An overloaded network can disrupt the emergency response (Nuclear risk).
- Do not smoke in the shelter (Czechia).
- Be considerate: do not make noise and avoid arguments. A shelter is demanding on people, physically and mentally (Czechia). Ukraine asks for discipline and as little walking back and forth as possible.
- Expect that it may be uncomfortable and crowded (Finland).
- Before you go to a civil defence shelter outside your home, shut off the gas, electricity and water and close windows and air vents (Ukraine). Switzerland also mentions: switch off electrical appliances, put out open fires, alert the neighbours, house pets as well as possible with water and food.
- If you cannot leave the room on your own, tell your neighbours (Ukraine).

## Children

- Create a calm atmosphere. Children quickly pick up on what adults feel.
- Listen to their questions and feelings. Say that it is normal to be afraid, sad, angry or uncertain.
- Explain it simply and honestly, in a way suited to their age. Say so if you do not know something.
- Practise simple safety rules in a playful way.
- Keep to the daily routine as much as possible.
- Let children help with the emergency plan and the emergency kit, and let them choose a small toy or cuddly toy for the kit.

These are the tips of the Crisis Center. Ukraine places families with children in a separate section of a shelter.

## People who need extra help

The Crisis Center mentions older people, people with a disability, people who live alone, people who understand Dutch, French or German less well, people who need extra support for medical reasons and homeless people.

- Discuss in advance with family, friends, neighbours or care providers what help is needed (Crisis Center, MCF).
- Include information about prescribed medication and aids in your emergency plan (MCF).
- Arrange transport to a shelter or protected place in advance (MCF).
- Consider whether someone depends on an assistant or electronic aids; that changes the preparation (Finland).
- Ukraine places people in poor health in a medical room or closer to the ventilation.
- During an emergency, check regularly whether vulnerable people need help, without putting yourself in danger (Crisis Center).

## Pets

- Include your pets in your emergency plan, with the vet's number. Make sure every animal is identified with a microchip or ear tag (Crisis Center).
- Have ready: a cage or basket, lead and collar or harness, food, water and bowls, pet passport and vaccination booklet, a photo of you and your animal, toys, medication and bags for droppings (Crisis Center).
- If you can let your animals shelter safely without risk to yourself, do so (Crisis Center).
- Sweden: during an air raid alert you may take your pet to cellars, garages and metro stations. If it has to stay at home, leave extra food and water.
- Finland: your home is the safest place for your pet; guide dogs and assistance dogs go into the civil defence shelter with you.
- Ukraine has officially allowed pets in shelters since January 2024.

## Step-by-step plan for setting up your shelter

The Czech guide gives this order for preparing an improvised shelter:

1. Choose the suitable room.
2. Determine how many people can shelter there, based on floor area and volume.
3. Determine which adaptations are needed, what material you need and how much time it will take.
4. Find out where the main taps for gas, water and heating are.
5. Clear the room.
6. Carry out the adaptations outside, including ventilation if needed.
7. Carry out the adaptations inside and seal the room.
8. Furnish the room.

Ukraine adds: appoint people responsible for the keys, the water, the first aid kit and the fire extinguisher; make a simple checklist; agree basic rules; mark the entrance, exits and contact details. Check the wiring and the electrical cabinets, provide ventilation and dry the room if needed, and remove anything that can fall, break or burn.

## Short checklist

| Question | Right answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is the room underground or in the middle of the building | Yes |
| Are there two walls between the room and the outside | Yes |
| Are there windows or glass walls | No, or shielded |
| Is there a second exit | Yes |
| Is there anything heavy or dangerous above or next to the room | No |
| Can the room flood during heavy rain | No, or you have an alternative on a higher floor |
| Does everyone in the house know where it is and how to switch off the ventilation | Yes |
| Are water, light, radio, toilet and first aid ready | Yes |
| Do you have a second and third place for other threats | Yes |
