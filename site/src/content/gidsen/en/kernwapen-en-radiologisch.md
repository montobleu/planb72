## What the Belgian authorities say

The Belgian authorities give no separate advice for nuclear weapons. For the release of nuclear or radioactive substances, including in an attack on a nuclear or radiological installation, the same advice applies:

- Sheltering is the best way to protect yourself.
- Go inside the nearest building and stay inside until you are told that it is safe.
- Close windows and doors and switch off ventilation systems. Take up position in a central room on the ground floor. Walls and ceilings protect better than windows.
- Listen to the authorities via the radio, preferably a battery-powered or wind-up radio. If the internet works, also check the official websites and social media.
- Leave your children at school.
- Never evacuate on your own initiative.
- Take iodine tablets only on the instruction of the authorities.

For the blast wave, the debris and the heat of a nuclear weapon, Belgium gives no specific guideline. The rest of this guide therefore follows the civil protection authorities of other European countries and the United States. That advice is intended for war or an attack and may differ from the Belgian advice for an accident at a nuclear power plant. Always follow the instructions of the Belgian authorities at the time.

## The dangers of a nuclear weapon

A nuclear detonation is the most dangerous radiation emergency. It causes a large, deadly blast wave. If a nuclear weapon explodes on the ground, the explosion can suck material up into the air and form dangerous radioactive particles, as fine as sand: fallout.

The dangers follow one after another:

| Danger | When | What protects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat | At the detonation | Cover; outside, face down on the ground so that bare skin is protected |
| Blast wave, debris, glass | Shortly after the detonation | Behind something solid, away from windows |
| Radioactive fallout | After 10 minutes or more | Deep inside a solid building or underground |

A civil defence shelter that is not right next to the centre of the explosion protects against blast wave, heat, debris, shocks, radioactive radiation and radioactive fallout.

Source: [Ready.gov: Radiation emergencies](https://www.ready.gov/radiation) · [BABS: Der Schutzraum](https://www.sz.ch/public/upload/assets/87489/Der_Schutzraum.pdf?fp=2) · [MCF: Good to know about civil defence shelters](https://www.mcf.se/en/advice-for-individuals/civil-defence-shelters-evacuation-and-warning-systems/good-to-know-about-civil-defence-shelters/) · [BBK: Explosionen, hier finden Sie Schutz](https://www.bbk.bund.de/DE/Warnung-Vorsorge/Vorsorge/Schutz-suchen/Schutz-vor-Explosion/schutz-vor-explosion_node.html)

## The first seconds and minutes

### If you are warned

- Shelter as you would in an air raid. A civil defence shelter gives the best protection. After that, cellars, garages and underground metro stations, then tunnels and walls.
- Inside is better than outside: choose a room without windows or with the sturdiest structure, such as the middle of a building.
- There is little time between the alarm and the danger. You will therefore often shelter where you are.
- Take cover from the blast wave behind anything that can offer protection.

### If you are outside

- Lie face down on the ground immediately, so that your bare skin is protected against the heat and flying debris. A hollow, ditch, kerb or stairs can offer extra protection.
- Cover your head with your hands.
- Once the blast wave has passed, go into the nearest building as quickly as possible.

### If you are inside

- Stay away from windows. Put as many walls as possible between you and the outside.
- Go as deep into the building as possible: cellar or inner room without windows.
- Do not use a lift.

## After the detonation: finding the best shelter

After a detonation you have 10 minutes or more to find a suitable shelter before the fallout arrives.

### Order of preference

1. **Best choice:** a building with several storeys or a cellar that you can reach safely within a few minutes. Go there immediately. The safest buildings have brick or concrete walls. In countries with civil defence shelters: the nearest civil defence shelter.
2. **Good choice:** an underground car park, garage or metro station. Thick walls and earth increase the protection.
3. **Inside the building:** the cellar or the middle of the building, as far as possible from outer walls and roof.
4. **Emergency solution:** any building is safer than staying outside.

### Per place

| Where you are | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| At home, with a cellar | To the cellar, the part furthest from outer walls |
| At home, without a cellar | Central room, away from outer walls, roof and windows |
| Flat | Cellar or underground car park of the building; otherwise the middle of the building, as far as possible from outer walls and roof |
| Work or school | Stay where you are; schools and childcare have emergency plans |
| On foot | Immediately into the nearest solid building |
| In the car | Go into a building immediately |
| Open ground | Lie down until the blast wave has passed, then look for the nearest building |

## The first hours: stay inside

- **Stay inside.** Shelter unless you are told otherwise.
- **Switch off ventilation:** fans, air conditioning and warm-air heating that draw in outside air. Close windows and doors. Close the chimney damper.
- **Pets and assistance animals inside.**
- **Wash yourself** if you were outside when the fallout came down (see below).
- **Eat and drink safely:** bottled water and food in sealed packaging.
- **Stay informed** via radio and TV; if the network works, also via your telephone and other channels. The emergency services will say where you can be checked for contamination. If the power fails, a battery-powered or wind-up radio helps.
- **Do not go to collect your family.** Stay where you are.

Source: [Ready.gov: Radiation emergencies](https://www.ready.gov/radiation) · [CDC: What to do, get inside](https://cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/response/get-inside.html) · [National Crisis Center: brochure Prepared together](https://crisiscenter.be/sites/default/files/documents/files/WEB-Brochure%20Prepared%20Together-EN.pdf)

## The first 24 hours and the first days

- Stay in the best-protected place (cellar or middle of a large building) for the first 24 hours, unless there is an immediate danger (fire, gas leak, collapse, serious injury) or the authorities say you may leave.
- Staying inside for at least 24 hours protects you and your family until it is safe to leave the area. Sheltering usually lasts at least 24 hours.
- Radiation levels drop quickly and become considerably less dangerous in the first 24 hours. After a few days they have dropped sharply.
- Prepare for a stay of hours to a few days in a shelter.

### When to leave earlier

Only if there is an immediate danger at your shelter: fire, gas leak, collapse of or damage to the shelter, or a serious injury.

### When to leave

When the authorities say so. If you have to evacuate, listen to the information about routes, reception points and procedures. Only go back when the local authorities say it is safe.

## After the first days

After sheltering:

- **Help others.** When the "all clear" signal sounds, you may leave the civil defence shelter. Help injured people and people who are trapped.
- **Do not touch munitions.** Military operations can leave unexploded munitions or cartridge cases in the terrain. Do not touch suspicious objects and do not move them.
- **Evacuation in wartime.** The authorities say which areas and groups evacuate. Take only what is necessary and take your pet with you. You can go to a place you arrange yourself, such as a holiday cottage or the home of someone close to you; those who cannot do that go to the reception and registration centres designated by the authorities.
- **Damaged building.** Leave a damaged building by the stairs, keep your distance from debris and watch out for gas and electricity.
- **Disinformation.** Foreign powers spread disinformation and propaganda, especially via online platforms and social media. Share only messages from the authorities or emergency services.
- **Food and returning home:** see the guide "After the cloud".

### What a civil defence shelter offers, and what it does not

Civil defence shelters with a concrete shell and ventilation give a high chance of survival in most threat situations. Against nearby impacts and fragments the protection is very high. Against a direct hit from conventional weapons they offer hardly any protection. A filter and overpressure keep contaminated outside air out in the case of biological and chemical weapons. Civil defence shelters protect better than other rooms against radioactive fallout, gas from chemical weapons and biological weapons. Belgium has no network of public civil defence shelters; see the guide "Public shelters".

## Decontamination after fallout

After a radioactive cloud:

1. Take off your clothes and put them in a plastic bag. Put the bag outside.
2. Take a shower and wash yourself with soap.
3. Put on clean clothing.
4. Drink enough.

Also:

- Taking off your outer layer of clothing can remove up to 90 per cent of the radioactive material. Do it carefully.
- Use soap and shampoo, not conditioner.
- Do not scrub, scratch or burn your skin. Cover small wounds.
- No shower: wash your hands, face and bare skin at a tap, or wipe them with a wet wipe or a wet cloth.
- Blow your nose and wipe your eyelids, eyelashes and ears.
- Pets that were outside: brush the coat out carefully and wash the animal with soap and water.

More details are in the guide "Nuclear accident, sheltering and radiation".

## Food, drink and medication while sheltering

After a nuclear detonation:

- **Food:** what was already inside, and food in packaging from outside that you have wiped off, is safe. Do not eat fresh food from the garden.
- **Water:** drink enough. Bottled water and sealed drinks are best. Water from the toilet cistern (not the bowl) and from the water heater is also safe. If there is nothing else, use tap water to stay hydrated.
- **Medication:** keep taking your medication. Medication that was inside, or in wiped-off packaging from outside, is safe.
- **Cleaning:** wipe all packaging, cutlery and surfaces with a damp cloth. Put the cloths in a closed bag, away from people and pets. Then wash your hands.

## A dirty bomb

A dirty bomb is a mixture of explosives, such as dynamite, with radioactive powder or pellets. Another name is radiological dispersal device. The greatest danger of a dirty bomb comes from the explosion, not from the radiation.

### What to do

1. **At the explosion:** leave the scene as quickly as possible if it is safe to do so. If you cannot get away, keep still and wait for the emergency services. Cover your nose and mouth against dust and toxic gases. If things are falling, take cover under a sturdy piece of furniture. Stay low if there is smoke.
2. **Afterwards:** go inside, stay inside and stay informed. Close windows and doors and switch off the ventilation.
3. **Wash yourself** if you were nearby: clothes off, into a bag, shower.
4. **Stay nearby, outside the danger zone,** if you need to be taken care of by the emergency services. The authorities may set up a reception centre for decontamination and medical checks.
5. **Do not touch suspicious objects.** After an attack there may still be other explosives.

## Other radiological incidents

### A hidden radiation source

This is called a radiological exposure device: a source hidden somewhere in order to irradiate people.

- Report a suspicion to the police immediately. In Belgium: 101, or 112 in an acute situation.
- Stay as far away as possible from the suspected source.
- If you think you have been exposed, follow the instructions of the competent services and contact your doctor.

### An accident involving a transport of radioactive substances

On Belgian territory about 400,000 packages containing radioactive substances are transported every year, about 40,000 transports. Usually this is low-level radioactive material, sometimes high-level radioactive material. Belgium is also a transit country.

- Report the accident to the emergency services immediately. In Belgium: 112.
- Stay as far away as possible from the scene of the accident.
- Do not touch any cargo from the container.
- Follow the instructions; the authorities may ask you to shelter.

If you cannot get through, have someone else call or ask for help nearby (see Emergency numbers in the app, If you cannot call).

## Children, vulnerable people and animals

- **Children:** leave them at school. Schools, childcare, hospitals and care homes have emergency plans. Do not go to collect your family. Children are more sensitive to radiation: have them checked medically after the emergency as soon as the authorities say it is safe.
- **People who need help:** arrange transport to a shelter or protected place in advance. In countries with civil defence shelters, guide dogs and other assistance dogs may go into the civil defence shelter.
- **Pets:** keep them inside. The home is the safest place for a pet.

## Preparing

- **Know how you will be warned.** Keep a battery-powered or wind-up radio ready, preferably with DAB+ and FM. Register with BE-Alert. Make sure you have several ways of receiving warnings.
- **Know your best place at home and at work:** cellar or the middle of the building, and an underground space nearby.
- **Have ready:** water, sealed food, medication, a set of spare clothing per family member and a bin bag for contaminated clothing.
- **Make a communication plan:** where you will meet if you cannot meet at home, a contact person far away, and know the shelter and evacuation plans of school, work and care facility.
- **Practise:** learn the steps for sheltering indoors and how to switch off the ventilation. Keep tape ready and keep iodine tablets as the authorities recommend.

## Do not

- Do not stay in the car if you can reach a building.
- Do not go to collect your children or family.
- Do not evacuate or take iodine on your own initiative.
- Do not leave the shelter in the first 24 hours without immediate danger or an order from the authorities.
- Do not eat fresh food from the garden.
- Do not use conditioner when washing after contamination.
